## Vectors in Space

### Basic Definitions and Concepts
* **Definition of a vector:** A vector in space is a directed line segment.
* **Definition of magnitude:** The magnitude (or length) of a vector in space is the distance between its initial point and its terminal point.
* **Line of action:** The straight line passing through the initial point and terminal point of a vector is called the line of action (or supporting line) of that vector.
* **Collinear vectors:** Two vectors are called collinear (or parallel) if their lines of action are parallel or coincident.
* **Direction of collinear vectors:** If two vectors are collinear, they are either in the same direction or in opposite directions.
* **Equal vectors:** Two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are called equal, denoted by $\vec{a} = \vec{b}$, if they have the same magnitude and the same direction.
* **Zero vector:** Vectors whose initial point and terminal point coincide, such as $\overrightarrow{AA}, \overrightarrow{BB}, \dots$, are called zero vectors.

### Notes and Conventions
* **Point notation:** A vector with initial point $A$ and terminal point $B$ is denoted by $\overrightarrow{AB}$.
* **General notation:** When it is not necessary to specify the initial point and terminal point of a vector, the vector is also denoted by $\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{x}, \vec{y}, \dots$
* **Magnitude notation:** The magnitude of vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$ is denoted by $|\overrightarrow{AB}|$, and the magnitude of vector $\vec{a}$ is denoted by $|\vec{a}|$.
* **Point determination property:** In space, for any given point $O$ and vector $\vec{a}$, there exists a unique point $M$ such that $\overrightarrow{OM} = \vec{a}$.
* **Conventions on the zero vector:** By convention, the zero vector has a magnitude of $0$, and has the same direction (and therefore is collinear) with every vector. Consequently, all zero vectors are equal and denoted collectively by $\vec{0}$.

## Sum and Difference of Two Vectors in Space

### a) Sum of Two Vectors in Space (Vector Addition)
* **Definition of the sum:** In space, let $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ be two vectors. Choose an arbitrary point $A$ and points $B, C$ such that $\overrightarrow{AB} = \vec{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{BC} = \vec{b}$. Then, the vector $\overrightarrow{AC}$ is called the sum of the two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$, denoted by $\vec{a} + \vec{b}$.
* **Definition of vector addition:** In space, the operation of finding the sum of two vectors is called vector addition.
* **Three-point rule (Triangle rule):** If $A, B, C$ are three arbitrary points, then $\overrightarrow{AB} + \overrightarrow{BC} = \overrightarrow{AC}$.
* **Parallelogram rule:** If $ABCD$ is a parallelogram, then $\overrightarrow{AB} + \overrightarrow{AD} = \overrightarrow{AC}$.
* **Parallelepiped rule (Box rule):** For a parallelepiped $ABCD.A'B'C'D'$, we have:
  $$\overrightarrow{AB} + \overrightarrow{AD} + \overrightarrow{AA'} = \overrightarrow{AC'}$$

#### Properties of Vector Addition
* **Commutative property:** If $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are two arbitrary vectors, then $\vec{a} + \vec{b} = \vec{b} + \vec{a}$.
* **Associative property:** If $\vec{a}, \vec{b}$ and $\vec{c}$ are three arbitrary vectors, then $(\vec{a} + \vec{b}) + \vec{c} = \vec{a} + (\vec{b} + \vec{c})$.
* **Addition with the zero vector:** If $\vec{a}$ is any vector, then $\vec{a} + \vec{0} = \vec{0} + \vec{a} = \vec{a}$.
* **Sum of multiple vectors:** From the associative property of vector addition in space, we can write the sum of three vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b}$ and $\vec{c}$ as $\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}$ without using parentheses. The same applies to the sum of multiple vectors in space.

### b) Difference of Two Vectors in Space (Vector Subtraction)
* **Definition of opposite vector:** In space, a vector having the same magnitude and opposite direction to vector $\vec{a}$ is called the opposite vector of vector $\vec{a}$, denoted by $-\vec{a}$.
* **Property of opposite vectors:** Two vectors are opposite if and only if their sum equals $\vec{0}$.
* **Notes on opposite vectors:**
  * The vector $\overrightarrow{BA}$ is an opposite vector of vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$.
  * The vector $\vec{0}$ is considered the opposite vector of itself.
* **Definition of the difference:** The vector $\vec{a} + (-\vec{b})$ is called the difference of two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$, and is denoted by $\vec{a} - \vec{b}$.
* **Definition of vector subtraction:** In space, the operation of finding the difference of two vectors is called vector subtraction.
* **Subtraction rule:** For any three points $O, A, B$ in space, we have:
  $$\overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA} = \overrightarrow{AB}$$

## Multiplication of a Vector by a Scalar in Space

### Definition
* **Definition of scalar multiplication:** In space, the product of a real number $k \ne 0$ and a vector $\vec{a} \ne \vec{0}$ is a vector, denoted by $k\vec{a}$, defined as follows:
  * In the same direction as vector $\vec{a}$ if $k > 0$; in the opposite direction to vector $\vec{a}$ if $k < 0$;
  * Having magnitude equal to $|k| \cdot |\vec{a}|$.
* **Definition of the operation:** In space, the operation of finding the product of a number and a vector is called the multiplication of a vector by a scalar.

### Notes and Condition for Collinearity
* **Conventions:** $0\vec{a} = \vec{0}$ and $k\vec{0} = \vec{0}$.
* **Zero product condition:** If $k\vec{a} = \vec{0}$, then $k = 0$ or $\vec{a} = \vec{0}$.
* **Condition for collinearity:** In space, a necessary and sufficient condition for two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ ($\vec{b} \ne \vec{0}$) to be collinear is that there exists a real number $k$ such that $\vec{a} = k\vec{b}$.

### Properties of Multiplication by a Scalar
* **Associative property:** If $h, k$ are two real numbers and $\vec{a}$ is any vector, then $h(k\vec{a}) = (hk)\vec{a}$.
* **Distributive property with respect to scalar addition:** If $h, k$ are two real numbers and $\vec{a}$ is any vector, then $(h + k)\vec{a} = h\vec{a} + k\vec{a}$.
* **Distributive property with respect to vector addition:** If $k$ is a real number and $\vec{a}, \vec{b}$ are two arbitrary vectors, then $k(\vec{a} + \vec{b}) = k\vec{a} + k\vec{b}$.
* **Multiplication by 1 and -1:** If $\vec{a}$ is any vector, then $1\vec{a} = \vec{a}$ and $(-1)\vec{a} = -\vec{a}$.

## Dot Product of Two Vectors in Space

### a) Angle Between Two Vectors in Space
* **Definition:** In space, given two vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b}$ distinct from $\vec{0}$. Choose an arbitrary point $O$ and let $A, B$ be two points such that $\overrightarrow{OA} = \vec{a}, \overrightarrow{OB} = \vec{b}$. Then, the angle $\widehat{AOB}$ ($0^\circ \le \widehat{AOB} \le 180^\circ$) is called the angle between the two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$, denoted by $(\vec{a}, \vec{b})$.
* **Perpendicular vectors:** If the angle between two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ is $90^\circ$, we say that the two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are perpendicular (or orthogonal) to each other, denoted by $\vec{a} \perp \vec{b}$.
* **Method to determine the angle:** To determine the angle between two vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{CD}$ in space, we can choose a point $E$ such that $\overrightarrow{AE} = \overrightarrow{CD}$, then $(\overrightarrow{AB}, \overrightarrow{CD}) = \widehat{BAE}$.
* **Convention on the angle:** By convention, the angle between an arbitrary vector and $\vec{0}$ can take any value from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$.

---

### b) Dot Product of Two Vectors in Space
* **Definition and formula:** In space, given two vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b}$ both distinct from $\vec{0}$. The dot product (or scalar product) of two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ is a real number, denoted by $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$, defined by the formula:
  $$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}| \cdot |\vec{b}| \cdot \cos(\vec{a}, \vec{b})$$
* **Convention:** By convention, if $\vec{a} = \vec{0}$ or $\vec{b} = \vec{0}$, then $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$.
* **Orthogonality condition:** For two vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b}$ both distinct from $\vec{0}$, we have:
  $$\vec{a} \perp \vec{b} \Leftrightarrow \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$$
* **Scalar square:** For every vector $\vec{a}$, we have:
  $$\vec{a}^2 = |\vec{a}|^2$$
* **Cosine formula:** If $\vec{a}, \vec{b}$ are two vectors distinct from $\vec{0}$, then:
  $$\cos(\vec{a}, \vec{b}) = \frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{a}| \cdot |\vec{b}|}$$

#### Properties of the Dot Product
* **Commutative property:** $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = \vec{b} \cdot \vec{a}$
* **Associative property with a scalar:** $(k\vec{a}) \cdot \vec{b} = k(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}) = \vec{a} \cdot (k\vec{b})$
* **Distributive property:** $\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} + \vec{c}) = \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + \vec{a} \cdot \vec{c}$

#### Application in Physics
* **Mechanical work:** If a constant force $\vec{F}$ acts on an object at point $M$ causing the object to undergo a displacement $\overrightarrow{MN}$, then the work $A$ done by the force is calculated by:
  $$A = \vec{F} \cdot \overrightarrow{MN}$$
  *(where force $\vec{F}$ has magnitude measured in Newtons, displacement $MN$ is measured in meters, and work $A$ is measured in Joules).*

## Extensions: Centroid of a Triangle and Centroid of a Tetrahedron

* **Property of the centroid of a triangle:** Similar to the plane, if $G$ is the centroid of triangle $ABC$, then for any arbitrary point $O$, we have:
  $$\overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OC} = 3\overrightarrow{OG}$$
* **Centroid of a tetrahedron:** Point $I$ is called the centroid of tetrahedron $ABCD$ if it satisfies:
  $$\overrightarrow{IA} + \overrightarrow{IB} + \overrightarrow{IC} + \overrightarrow{ID} = \vec{0}$$
  *(which is equivalent to $\overrightarrow{AI} = 3\overrightarrow{IG}$, where $G$ is the centroid of triangle $BCD$).*
