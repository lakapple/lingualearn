## Range

\<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_khoang_bien_thien"/>

### a) Definition and Meaning of Range

Consider grouped data with $k$ classes (groups):
• Class 1: $[a_1; a_2)$ with frequency $m_1 > 0$;
• Class $i$: $[a_i; a\_{i+1})$ with frequency $m_i$;
• Class $k$: $[a_k; a\_{k+1})$ with frequency $m_k > 0$;
where the sample size is $n = m_1 + m_2 + \dots + m_k$.
Definition: The range of the grouped data above, denoted by $R$, is the difference between the upper boundary of the last class containing data and the lower boundary of the first class containing data:
$$R = a\_{k+1} - a_1$$
Meaning:
• The range of grouped data provides an approximation of the range of the raw (ungrouped) data.
• The range is used to measure the degree of dispersion of grouped data. A larger range indicates that the data is more widely spread out.

### b) Examples and Practice on Range

**Example 1:** Statistics on daily social media usage time of students in Group 1 and Group 2 of class 12A are recorded as follows:
• Group 1: $[0; 10)$ (4 students), $[10; 30)$ (2 students), $[30; 60)$ (3 students), $[60; 90)$ (1 student).
• Group 2: $[0; 10)$ (5 students), $[10; 30)$ (1 student), $[30; 60)$ (3 students), $[60; 90)$ (0 students).
Find the range of social media usage time for students in each group and explain its meaning.

Solution to Example 1:
• For Group 1, the first class containing data is $[0; 10)$ and the last class containing data is $[60; 90)$.
  The range for Group 1 is:
  $$R_1 = 90 - 0 = 90\text{ (minutes).}$$
• For Group 2, the class $[60; 90)$ has frequency 0, so the actual last class containing data is $[30; 60)$.
  The range for Group 2 is:
  $$R_2 = 60 - 0 = 60\text{ (minutes).}$$
• Meaning: Since $R_1 > R_2$ ($90 > 60$), the daily social media usage time of students in Group 1 is more dispersed (varies more widely) than that of Group 2.

**Practice 1:** The completion time of a Mathematics test by students in class 12C is given as follows: $[25; 30)$ (8 students), $[30; 35)$ (16 students), $[35; 40)$ (4 students), $[40; 45)$ (2 students).
a) Compute the range $R$ for the grouped data above.
b) If it is known that the earliest student finished in 27 minutes and the latest finished in 43 minutes, what is the range of the raw data?

Solution to Practice 1:
• a) The first class is $[25; 30)$ and the last class is $[40; 45)$.
  The range of the grouped data is:
  $$R = 45 - 25 = 20\text{ (minutes).}$$
• b) For the raw data, the minimum value is $x\_{\min} = 27$ minutes and the maximum value is $x\_{\max} = 43$ minutes.
  The range of the raw data is:
  $$R\_{\text{raw}} = x\_{\max} - x\_{\min} = 43 - 27 = 16\text{ (minutes).}$$
  Remark: The range of the grouped data ($R = 20$) is larger than the range of the raw data ($R\_{\text{raw}} = 16$).

## Interquartile Range

\<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_khoang_tu_phan_vi"/>

### a) Definition and Formula for the Interquartile Range

Consider grouped data with sample size $n = m_1 + m_2 + \dots + m_k$.
Formula for the $r$-th quartile $Q_r$ ($r \in \\{1, 2, 3\\}$):
$$Q_r = a_p + \frac{\frac{r \cdot n}{4} - (m_1 + m_2 + \dots + m\_{p-1})}{m_p} \cdot (a\_{p+1} - a_p)$$
where $[a_p; a\_{p+1})$ is the class containing the $r$-th quartile.
Definition: The interquartile range of grouped data, denoted by $\Delta_Q$ (or $IQR$), is the difference between the third quartile $Q_3$ and the first quartile $Q_1$ of the data:
$$\Delta_Q = Q_3 - Q_1$$
Meaning and Remarks:
• The interquartile range $\Delta_Q$ measures the dispersion of the middle 50% of the data values.
• A larger $\Delta_Q$ indicates greater variability among the central observations.
• The interquartile range is not influenced by extreme outliers at either tail, making it a robust and reliable measure of dispersion when data contains abnormal extreme values.

### b) Examples and Practice on the Interquartile Range

**Example 2:** The waiting times of patients at Clinic X are recorded in the table: $[0; 5)$ (3 patients), $[5; 10)$ (12 patients), $[10; 15)$ (15 patients), $[15; 20)$ (8 patients).
a) Find the interquartile range of this grouped data.
b) At Clinic Y, the calculated interquartile range of waiting times is 9.23 minutes. In which clinic is the patients' waiting time more dispersed?

Solution to Example 2:
• a) The sample size is $n = 3 + 12 + 15 + 8 = 38$.
  - Finding $Q_1$: We have $\frac{n}{4} = \frac{38}{4} = 9.5$. The cumulative frequency of $[0; 5)$ is 3; of $[5; 10)$ is $3 + 12 = 15 > 9.5$. Thus, the class containing $Q_1$ is $[5; 10)$.
    $$Q_1 = 5 + \frac{9.5 - 3}{12} \cdot (10 - 5) = 5 + \frac{6.5}{12} \cdot 5 \approx 7.71\text{ (minutes).}$$
  - Finding $Q_3$: We have $\frac{3n}{4} = \frac{3 \cdot 38}{4} = 28.5$. The cumulative frequency up to $[10; 15)$ is $15 + 15 = 30 > 28.5$. Thus, the class containing $Q_3$ is $[10; 15)$.
    $$Q_3 = 10 + \frac{28.5 - 15}{15} \cdot (15 - 10) = 10 + \frac{13.5}{15} \cdot 5 = 14.5\text{ (minutes).}$$
  - The interquartile range for Clinic X is:
    $$\Delta_Q = Q_3 - Q_1 \approx 14.5 - 7.71 = 6.79\text{ (minutes).}$$
• b) Since $\Delta\_{Q(X)} = 6.79 < 9.23 = \Delta\_{Q(Y)}$, the waiting times of patients at Clinic Y are more dispersed (vary more widely) than at Clinic X.

**Practice 2:** The duration of phone calls made by a user was recorded as follows:
$[0; 1)$ (8 calls), $[1; 2)$ (17 calls), $[2; 3)$ (25 calls), $[3; 4)$ (20 calls), $[4; 5)$ (10 calls).
Calculate the interquartile range of this grouped data.

Solution to Practice 2:
• Sample size: $n = 8 + 17 + 25 + 20 + 10 = 80$.
• Determining $Q_1$: We have $\frac{n}{4} = \frac{80}{4} = 20$.
  The cumulative frequency of $[0; 1)$ is 8; of $[1; 2)$ is $8 + 17 = 25 > 20$. Thus, the class containing $Q_1$ is $[1; 2)$.
  $$Q_1 = 1 + \frac{20 - 8}{17} \cdot (2 - 1) = 1 + \frac{12}{17} \approx 1.71\text{ (minutes).}$$
• Determining $Q_3$: We have $\frac{3n}{4} = \frac{3 \cdot 80}{4} = 60$.
  The cumulative frequency up to $[2; 3)$ is $25 + 25 = 50$; of $[3; 4)$ is $50 + 20 = 70 > 60$. Thus, the class containing $Q_3$ is $[3; 4)$.
  $$Q_3 = 3 + \frac{60 - 50}{20} \cdot (4 - 3) = 3 + \frac{10}{20} = 3.5\text{ (minutes).}$$
• The interquartile range is:
  $$\Delta_Q = Q_3 - Q_1 = 3.5 - 1.71 = 1.79\text{ (minutes).}$$

## Applications and Exercises

### a) Real-World Applications

**Application (Analysis of Daily Maximum June Temperatures in Hanoi):** Statistics on the daily maximum temperatures in June 2021 and June 2022 in Hanoi ($n = 30$ days):
• June 2021: $[30; 32)$ (2 days), $[32; 34)$ (8 days), $[34; 36)$ (5 days), $[36; 38)$ (6 days), $[38; 40)$ (9 days).
• June 2022: $[28; 30)$ (2 days), $[30; 32)$ (3 days), $[32; 34)$ (4 days), $[34; 36)$ (11 days), $[36; 38)$ (8 days), $[38; 40)$ (2 days).
In which June did the daily maximum temperatures in Hanoi fluctuate more?

Solution to Application:
• 1. Range $R$:
  - June 2021: First class containing data is $[30; 32)$, last class is $[38; 40) \implies R\_{2021} = 40 - 30 = 10^{\circ}\text{C}$.
  - June 2022: First class is $[28; 30)$, last class is $[38; 40) \implies R\_{2022} = 40 - 28 = 12^{\circ}\text{C}$.
  - Considering the overall range across all days, June 2022 had a larger range ($12 > 10$).
• 2. Interquartile range $\Delta_Q$ (measuring the dispersion of the middle 50% of days):
  - June 2021 ($n = 30$):
    + $\frac{n}{4} = 7.5 \implies$ Class containing $Q_1$ is $[32; 34)$:
      $$Q_1 = 32 + \frac{7.5 - 2}{8} \cdot 2 = 32 + \frac{5.5}{4} = 33.375^{\circ}\text{C}.$$
    + $\frac{3n}{4} = 22.5 \implies$ Class containing $Q_3$ is $[38; 40)$:
      $$Q_3 = 38 + \frac{22.5 - 21}{9} \cdot 2 = 38 + \frac{3}{9} \approx 38.333^{\circ}\text{C}.$$
    + $\Delta\_{Q(2021)} = Q_3 - Q_1 \approx 38.333 - 33.375 = 4.958^{\circ}\text{C}$.
  - June 2022 ($n = 30$):
    + $\frac{n}{4} = 7.5 \implies$ Class containing $Q_1$ is $[32; 34)$:
      $$Q_1 = 32 + \frac{7.5 - 5}{4} \cdot 2 = 32 + \frac{2.5}{2} = 33.25^{\circ}\text{C}.$$
    + $\frac{3n}{4} = 22.5 \implies$ Class containing $Q_3$ is $[36; 38)$:
      $$Q_3 = 36 + \frac{22.5 - 20}{8} \cdot 2 = 36 + \frac{2.5}{4} = 36.625^{\circ}\text{C}.$$
    + $\Delta\_{Q(2022)} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 36.625 - 33.25 = 3.375^{\circ}\text{C}$.
• Conclusion: Since $\Delta\_{Q(2021)} \approx 4.96 > 3.38 = \Delta\_{Q(2022)}$, the temperatures of the middle half of the days in June 2021 varied significantly more than those in June 2022.

### b) End-of-Lesson Exercises

**Exercise 3.1:** The number of yellow cards received by 20 English Premier League clubs in the 2021-2022 season was: 101, 79, 79, 78, 75, 73, 68, 67, 67, 63, 63, 61, 60, 59, 57, 55, 55, 50, 47, 42.
a) Group the data into classes of equal width with the first class being $[40; 50)$.
b) Calculate the range and interquartile range for both the raw data and the grouped data. Which values are exact, and which are approximations?

Solution to Exercise 3.1:
• a) Grouped classes of width 10:
  - $[40; 50)$: 42, 47 (frequency 2);
  - $[50; 60)$: 50, 55, 55, 57, 59 (frequency 5);
  - $[60; 70)$: 60, 61, 63, 63, 67, 67, 68 (frequency 7);
  - $[70; 80)$: 73, 75, 78, 79, 79 (frequency 5);
  - $[80; 90)$: frequency 0;
  - $[90; 100)$: frequency 0;
  - $[100; 110)$: 101 (frequency 1).
• b) Raw data ($n = 20$):
  - Sorted values: 42, 47, 50, 55, 55, 57, 59, 60, 61, 63, 63, 67, 67, 68, 73, 75, 78, 79, 79, 101.
  - Exact range: $R\_{\text{raw}} = 101 - 42 = 59$.
  - Raw quartiles: $Q_1 = \frac{55 + 57}{2} = 56$; $Q_3 = \frac{75 + 78}{2} = 76.5$.
  - Exact interquartile range: $\Delta\_{Q(\text{raw})} = 76.5 - 56 = 20.5$.
• Grouped data:
  - Approximate range: $R = 110 - 40 = 70$.
  - $\frac{n}{4} = 5 \implies$ Class containing $Q_1$ is $[50; 60) \implies Q_1 = 50 + \frac{5 - 2}{5} \cdot 10 = 56$.
  - $\frac{3n}{4} = 15 \implies$ Cumulative frequency up to $[60; 70)$ is $2 + 5 + 7 = 14$; class containing $Q_3$ is $[70; 80)$:
    $$Q_3 = 70 + \frac{15 - 14}{5} \cdot 10 = 70 + 2 = 72.$$
  - Approximate interquartile range: $\Delta_Q = 72 - 56 = 16$.
• Conclusion: The statistics obtained from the raw data are exact values; those calculated from the grouped frequency distribution are approximations.

**Exercise 3.2:** The monthly income of workers at Factory A ($n_A = 155$) and Factory B ($n_B = 110$) across the classes $[5; 8), [8; 11), [11; 14), [14; 17), [17; 20)$ (million VND):
• Factory A: 20, 35, 45, 35, 20 workers.
• Factory B: 17, 23, 30, 23, 17 workers.
Calculate the mean income for both factories. Based on the interquartile range, determine which factory exhibits greater variability in workers' incomes.

Solution to Exercise 3.2:
• 1. Mean monthly income (class midpoints: 6.5, 9.5, 12.5, 15.5, 18.5):
  $$\bar{x}\_A = \frac{20(6.5) + 35(9.5) + 45(12.5) + 35(15.5) + 20(18.5)}{155} = \frac{1937.5}{155} = 12.5\text{ (million VND).}$$
  $$\bar{x}\_B = \frac{17(6.5) + 23(9.5) + 30(12.5) + 23(15.5) + 17(18.5)}{110} = \frac{1375}{110} = 12.5\text{ (million VND).}$$
  The mean income at both factories is identical (12.5 million VND/worker).
• 2. Interquartile range:
  - Factory A ($n = 155$):
    + $\frac{n}{4} = 38.75 \implies$ Class containing $Q_1$ is $[8; 11)$:
      $$Q_1 = 8 + \frac{38.75 - 20}{35} \cdot 3 = 8 + \frac{18.75}{35} \cdot 3 \approx 9.607.$$
    + $\frac{3n}{4} = 116.25 \implies$ Class containing $Q_3$ is $[14; 17)$:
      $$Q_3 = 14 + \frac{116.25 - 100}{35} \cdot 3 = 14 + \frac{16.25}{35} \cdot 3 \approx 15.393.$$
    + Interquartile range: $\Delta\_{Q(A)} = Q_3 - Q_1 \approx 15.393 - 9.607 = 5.786\text{ (million VND)}$.
  - Factory B ($n = 110$):
    + $\frac{n}{4} = 27.5 \implies$ Class containing $Q_1$ is $[8; 11)$:
      $$Q_1 = 8 + \frac{27.5 - 17}{23} \cdot 3 = 8 + \frac{10.5}{23} \cdot 3 \approx 9.370.$$
    + $\frac{3n}{4} = 82.5 \implies$ Class containing $Q_3$ is $[14; 17)$:
      $$Q_3 = 14 + \frac{82.5 - 70}{23} \cdot 3 = 14 + \frac{12.5}{23} \cdot 3 \approx 15.630.$$
    + Interquartile range: $\Delta\_{Q(B)} = Q_3 - Q_1 \approx 15.630 - 9.370 = 6.260\text{ (million VND)}$.
• Conclusion: Because $\Delta\_{Q(B)} \approx 6.26 > 5.79 \approx \Delta\_{Q(A)}$, the worker incomes at Factory B exhibit greater dispersion and variability than at Factory A.

**Exercise 3.3:** The heights of students in classes 12A and 12B are grouped into classes (cm):
• Classes: $[150; 155), [155; 160), [160; 165), [165; 170), [170; 175)$.
• Class 12A ($n_A = 43$): 15, 12, 1, 10, 5 students.
• Class 12B ($n_B = 42$): 17, 0, 10, 9, 6 students.
a) Find the range and interquartile range of heights for students in both classes.
b) To compare the dispersion of student heights between the two classes, should we use the range or the interquartile range? Why?

Solution to Exercise 3.3:
• a) Calculating statistics:
  - Range:
    + Both classes have the first class as $[150; 155)$ and the last class as $[170; 175)$.
    + Therefore: $R_A = R_B = 175 - 150 = 25\text{ (cm)}$.
  - Interquartile range for Class 12A ($n_A = 43$):
    + $\frac{n}{4} = 10.75 \implies$ Class containing $Q_1$ is $[150; 155) \implies Q_1 = 150 + \frac{10.75 - 0}{15} \cdot 5 = 153.58\text{ cm}$.
    + $\frac{3n}{4} = 32.25 \implies$ Class containing $Q_3$ is $[165; 170) \implies Q_3 = 165 + \frac{32.25 - 28}{10} \cdot 5 = 167.125\text{ cm}$.
    + $\Delta\_{Q(A)} = 167.125 - 153.58 = 13.545\text{ cm}$.
  - Interquartile range for Class 12B ($n_B = 42$):
    + $\frac{n}{4} = 10.5 \implies$ Class containing $Q_1$ is $[150; 155) \implies Q_1 = 150 + \frac{10.5 - 0}{17} \cdot 5 \approx 153.09\text{ cm}$.
    + $\frac{3n}{4} = 31.5 \implies$ Cumulative frequency up to $[160; 165)$ is $17 + 0 + 10 = 27$; class containing $Q_3$ is $[165; 170)$:
      $$Q_3 = 165 + \frac{31.5 - 27}{9} \cdot 5 = 165 + 2.5 = 167.5\text{ cm}.$$
    + $\Delta\_{Q(B)} = 167.5 - 153.09 = 14.41\text{ cm}$.
• b) To compare the dispersion of heights between the two classes, we should use the INTERQUARTILE RANGE.
  - The ranges for both classes are identical ($R_A = R_B = 25\text{ cm}$), thus failing to capture the differences in the internal distribution of heights.
  - The interquartile range focuses specifically on the spread of the middle 50% of the students and is immune to the disproportionate influence of extreme heights at the outer boundaries.
