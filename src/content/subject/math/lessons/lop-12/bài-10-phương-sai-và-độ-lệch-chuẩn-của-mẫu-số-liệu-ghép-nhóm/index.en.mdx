## Variance and Standard Deviation of Grouped Data

\<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_phuong_sai_va_do_lech_chuan"/>

### a) Formulas for Variance and Standard Deviation

Let a grouped dataset consist of $k$ class intervals $[a_i; a\_{i+1})$ with corresponding frequencies $m_i$ ($i = 1, 2, \dots, k$) and total sample size $n = m_1 + m_2 + \dots + m_k$.
• The midpoint (class mark) of the interval $[a_i; a\_{i+1})$ is: $x_i = \frac{a_i + a\_{i+1}}{2}$.
• The sample mean of the grouped data is given by:
$$\overline{x} = \frac{m_1 x_1 + m_2 x_2 + \dots + m_k x_k}{n}$$
Definition of Variance:
The variance of the grouped data, denoted by $s^2$, is computed as:
$$s^2 = \frac{m_1(x_1 - \overline{x})^2 + m_2(x_2 - \overline{x})^2 + \dots + m_k(x_k - \overline{x})^2}{n}$$
Computational Shortcut Formula for Variance:
$$s^2 = \frac{1}{n}\left(m_1 x_1^2 + m_2 x_2^2 + \dots + m_k x_k^2\right) - (\overline{x})^2$$
Standard Deviation:
The standard deviation of the grouped data, denoted by $s$, is the non-negative square root of the variance:
$$s = \sqrt{s^2}$$
Statistical Meaning:
• The variance and standard deviation of grouped data are approximations of the variance and standard deviation of the underlying raw dataset.
• They measure the degree of dispersion (spread) of the data points around their mean. Larger values of variance and standard deviation indicate greater spread and higher volatility.
• The standard deviation has the same measurement unit as the original data.
Note: In inferential statistics, the sample-adjusted variance is also used: $\hat{s}^2 = \frac{n}{n-1}s^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum\_{i=1}^k m_i(x_i - \overline{x})^2$.

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Variance and Standard Deviation

**Example 1:** Tracking the weight change (in kg) after three months on a dietary plan for a group of men and women yielded the following data:
• Weight change intervals: $[-1; 0), [0; 1), [1; 2), [2; 3), [3; 4)$.
• Number of men: 2, 3, 5, 3, 2.
• Number of women: 2, 7, 12, 7, 2.
Calculate the mean, variance, and standard deviation, and compare the weight changes between men and women.

Solution for Example 1:
• The class midpoints for the intervals are: $-0.5; 0.5; 1.5; 2.5; 3.5$.
• 1. For the male group ($n_1 = 2 + 3 + 5 + 3 + 2 = 15$):
  - Mean weight loss:
    $$\overline{x}\_1 = \frac{2(-0.5) + 3(0.5) + 5(1.5) + 3(2.5) + 2(3.5)}{15} = \frac{22.5}{15} = 1.5\text{ (kg).}$$
  - Variance:
    $$s_1^2 = \frac{1}{15}\left[2(-0.5)^2 + 3(0.5)^2 + 5(1.5)^2 + 3(2.5)^2 + 2(3.5)^2\right] - (1.5)^2 = \frac{55.5}{15} - 2.25 = 3.7 - 2.25 = 1.45.$$
  - Standard deviation: $s_1 = \sqrt{1.45} \approx 1.21\text{ (kg)}$.
• 2. For the female group ($n_2 = 2 + 7 + 12 + 7 + 2 = 30$):
  - Mean weight loss:
    $$\overline{x}\_2 = \frac{2(-0.5) + 7(0.5) + 12(1.5) + 7(2.5) + 2(3.5)}{30} = \frac{45}{30} = 1.5\text{ (kg).}$$
  - Variance:
    $$s_2^2 = \frac{1}{30}\left[2(-0.5)^2 + 7(0.5)^2 + 12(1.5)^2 + 7(2.5)^2 + 2(3.5)^2\right] - (1.5)^2 = \frac{97.5}{30} - 2.25 = 3.25 - 2.25 = 1.$$
  - Standard deviation: $s_2 = \sqrt{1} = 1\text{ (kg)}$.
• Conclusion: On average, men and women achieved the same weight loss after 3 months ($1.5\text{ kg}$). However, since $s_2 = 1 < 1.21 \approx s_1$, the weight loss among women exhibits lower dispersion and is more uniform than that among men.

**Practice:** An athlete recorded the completion times for a 100-meter sprint across 20 training trials as follows:
• $[10.2; 10.4)$: 3 trials;
• $[10.4; 10.6)$: 7 trials;
• $[10.6; 10.8)$: 8 trials;
• $[10.8; 11)$: 2 trials.
Find the variance and standard deviation of this grouped dataset. What information do these metrics provide?

Solution for Practice:
• The class midpoints are: $x_1 = 10.3; x_2 = 10.5; x_3 = 10.7; x_4 = 10.9$.
• Sample size: $n = 3 + 7 + 8 + 2 = 20$.
• Mean sprint time:
  $$\overline{x} = \frac{3(10.3) + 7(10.5) + 8(10.7) + 2(10.9)}{20} = \frac{30.9 + 73.5 + 85.6 + 21.8}{20} = \frac{211.8}{20} = 10.59\text{ (seconds).}$$
• Variance of the grouped data:
  $$s^2 = \frac{1}{20}\left[3(10.3)^2 + 7(10.5)^2 + 8(10.7)^2 + 2(10.9)^2\right] - (10.59)^2$$
  $$s^2 = \frac{1}{20}(318.27 + 771.75 + 915.92 + 237.62) - 112.1481 = \frac{2\\,243.56}{20} - 112.1481 = 112.178 - 112.1481 = 0.0299\text{ (seconds}^2\text{).}$$
• Standard deviation of the grouped data:
  $$s = \sqrt{s^2} = \sqrt{0.0299} \approx 0.1729\text{ (seconds).}$$
• Interpretation: The variance and standard deviation indicate the spread of the athlete's 100m sprint times around the mean of $10.59$ seconds. The small standard deviation ($s \approx 0.173\text{ s}$) shows that the athlete's performance is highly consistent and stable.

## Using Variance and Standard Deviation to Measure Risk

\<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_do_do_rui_ro_tai_chinh"/>

### a) Meaning in Finance and Business

Application in Financial Investment Analysis:
• In corporate finance and portfolio management, the standard deviation of returns serves as a fundamental quantitative metric of risk.
• The expected (mean) return reflects profitability, while the standard deviation measures the volatility and uncertainty of returns around the mean. An investment option with a larger standard deviation is regarded as carrying higher risk.
Limitations and the Coefficient of Variation ($CV$):
• Comparing risk via standard deviation is only valid when the investment options have comparable mean returns.
• When the mean returns differ substantially or different measurement units are used, the Coefficient of Variation ($CV$) is utilized:
$$CV = \frac{s}{\overline{x}}$$
The option with the larger coefficient of variation possesses higher relative risk per unit of expected return.

### b) Illustrative Examples and Notes on Risk Comparison

**Example 2:** An invested equal amounts of capital into two business sectors A and B over a 60-month period:
• Monthly return intervals: $[5; 10), [10; 15), [15; 20), [20; 25), [25; 30)$ (million VND).
• Number of months in sector A: 5, 10, 30, 10, 5 months.
• Number of months in sector B: 20, 5, 10, 5, 20 months.
Compare the mean and standard deviation of monthly returns. Which sector is riskier?

Solution for Example 2:
• Class midpoints: $7.5; 12.5; 17.5; 22.5; 27.5$.
• Mean monthly returns ($n = 60$ months):
  $$\overline{x}\_A = \frac{5(7.5) + 10(12.5) + 30(17.5) + 10(22.5) + 5(27.5)}{60} = \frac{1\\,050}{60} = 17.5\text{ (million VND).}$$
  $$\overline{x}\_B = \frac{20(7.5) + 5(12.5) + 10(17.5) + 5(22.5) + 20(27.5)}{60} = \frac{1\\,050}{60} = 17.5\text{ (million VND).}$$
  On average, both sectors yield identical monthly returns ($17.5$ million VND/month).
• Standard deviation of monthly returns:
  $$s_A = \sqrt{\frac{1}{60}\left[5(7.5)^2 + 10(12.5)^2 + 30(17.5)^2 + 10(22.5)^2 + 5(27.5)^2\right] - (17.5)^2} = \sqrt{331.25 - 306.25} = \sqrt{25} = 5\text{ (million VND).}$$
  $$s_B = \sqrt{\frac{1}{60}\left[20(7.5)^2 + 5(12.5)^2 + 10(17.5)^2 + 5(22.5)^2 + 20(27.5)^2\right] - (17.5)^2} = \sqrt{377.083 - 306.25} = \sqrt{70.833} \approx 8.42\text{ (million VND).}$$
• Conclusion: Because $s_B \approx 8.42 > 5 = s_A$, returns in sector B exhibit much greater volatility than in sector A. Therefore, investing in sector B is riskier.

**Example 3:** Monthly profits (in million VND) over 20 months for two investors are summarized as follows:
• Small-scale investor: $[10; 20)$ (2), $[20; 30)$ (4), $[30; 40)$ (8), $[40; 50)$ (4), $[50; 60)$ (2).
• Large-scale investor: $[510; 520)$ (4), $[520; 530)$ (3), $[530; 540)$ (6), $[540; 550)$ (3), $[550; 560)$ (4).
Calculate the standard deviation of both grouped datasets. Should standard deviation alone be used to compare their investment risk?

Solution for Example 3:
• 1. Small-scale investor ($n = 20$, midpoints $15, 25, 35, 45, 55$):
  - Mean profit: $\overline{x}\_A = \frac{2(15) + 4(25) + 8(35) + 4(45) + 2(55)}{20} = \frac{700}{20} = 35\text{ (million VND).}$
  - Standard deviation: $s_A = \sqrt{\frac{1}{20}\left[2(15^2) + 4(25^2) + 8(35^2) + 4(45^2) + 2(55^2)\right] - 35^2} = \sqrt{1\\,345 - 1\\,225} = \sqrt{120} \approx 10.95\text{ (million VND).}$
• 2. Large-scale investor ($n = 20$, midpoints $515, 525, 535, 545, 555$):
  - Mean profit: $\overline{x}\_B = \frac{4(515) + 3(525) + 6(535) + 3(545) + 4(555)}{20} = \frac{10\\,700}{20} = 535\text{ (million VND).}$
  - Standard deviation: $s_B = \sqrt{\frac{1}{20}\left[4(515^2) + 3(525^2) + 6(535^2) + 3(545^2) + 4(555^2)\right] - 535^2} = \sqrt{286\\,415 - 286\\,225} = \sqrt{190} \approx 13.78\text{ (million VND).}$
• Remark: Although $s_B > s_A$ ($13.78 > 10.95$), one SHOULD NOT rely solely on standard deviation to compare risk between these two investors, because their mean profit scales differ drastically ($535$ million VND vs. $35$ million VND).
  Examining the relative Coefficient of Variation ($CV$):
  $$CV_A = \frac{s_A}{\overline{x}\_A} = \frac{10.95}{35} \approx 31.3\%; \qquad CV_B = \frac{s_B}{\overline{x}\_B} = \frac{13.78}{535} \approx 2.58\%.$$
  The profits of the small-scale investor are far more volatile relative to their capital base.

## Applications and Exercises

### a) Real-World Applications

**Application (Testing Hygrometer Measurement Stability - Introductory Problem):** To evaluate the stability of an electronic hygrometer, a technician performs 20 repeated humidity measurements. If the standard deviation of the measurements exceeds 0.15%, the instrument must be sent for recalibration. The grouped sample data obtained is:
• $[52; 52.1)$: 1 measurement;
• $[52.1; 52.2)$: 5 measurements;
• $[52.2; 52.3)$: 8 measurements;
• $[52.3; 52.4)$: 4 measurements;
• $[52.4; 52.5)$: 2 measurements.
Does this hygrometer need to be sent for recalibration/repair?

Solution for Application:
• The class midpoints are: $52.05; 52.15; 52.25; 52.35; 52.45$.
• Sample size: $n = 1 + 5 + 8 + 4 + 2 = 20$.
• Mean measured humidity:
  $$\overline{x} = \frac{1(52.05) + 5(52.15) + 8(52.25) + 4(52.35) + 2(52.45)}{20} = \frac{1\\,045.1}{20} = 52.255\text{ (\%).}$$
• Variance of the grouped data:
  $$s^2 = \frac{1}{20}\left[1(52.05)^2 + 5(52.15)^2 + 8(52.25)^2 + 4(52.35)^2 + 2(52.45)^2\right] - (52.255)^2$$
  $$s^2 = \frac{54\\,611.735}{20} - 2\\,730.585025 = 2\\,730.58675 - 2\\,730.585025 = 0.010475.$$
• Standard deviation of the grouped data:
  $$s = \sqrt{s^2} = \sqrt{0.010475} \approx 0.1023\text{ (\%).}$$
• Conclusion: Because $s \approx 0.1023\% < 0.15\%$, the hygrometer satisfies the required precision and stability standard; recalibration is NOT required.

Solution for Exercise 3.4:
• a) Grouped frequency distribution table ($n = 30$ bags):
  - $[48.5; 49)$: 48.7, 48.9, 48.8, 48.8, 48.7, 48.6 $\implies$ Frequency: 6;
  - $[49; 49.5)$: 49.3, 49.2 $\implies$ Frequency: 2;

• b) Computing dispersion statistics:
  - Raw dataset ($n = 30$):
    Sum $\sum x_i = 1\\,504.3 \implies \overline{x}\_{\text{raw}} = \frac{1\\,504.3}{30} \approx 50.1433\text{ kg}$.
    Exact variance: $s\_{\text{raw}}^2 = \frac{1}{30}\sum x_i^2 - (\overline{x}\_{\text{raw}})^2 \approx 0.8718\text{ (kg}^2\text{)}$.
    Exact standard deviation: $s\_{\text{raw}} = \sqrt{0.8718} \approx 0.9337\text{ kg}$.
  - Grouped dataset ($n = 30$):
    Class midpoints: $48.75; 49.25; 49.75; 50.25; 50.75; 51.25$.

    Approximate variance: $s\_{\text{grouped}}^2 = \frac{1}{30}\sum m_i x_i^2 - (\overline{x}\_{\text{grouped}})^2 \approx 0.8622\text{ (kg}^2\text{)}$.
    Approximate standard deviation: $s\_{\text{grouped}} = \sqrt{0.8622} \approx 0.9286\text{ kg}$.
  - Conclusion: The values computed directly from the raw data ($s^2 \approx 0.8718; s \approx 0.9337$) are exact; the values derived from the grouped frequency table ($s^2 \approx 0.8622; s \approx 0.9286$) are approximations.

**Exercise 3.5:** Operational lifespan (in years) of electronic components manufactured by two workshops:
• Lifespan intervals: $[1.5; 2), [2; 2.5), [2.5; 3), [3; 3.5), [3.5; 4)$.

**Exercise 3.8:** Should variance (or standard deviation) be used to compare data dispersion in each of the following scenarios? Explain why.
a) National graduation Mathematics exam scores of students from two high schools of comparable academic quality.
b) Revenue of 100 small grocery retailers versus revenue of 100 large supermarket chains.

Solution for Exercise 3.8:
• a) For the graduation exam scores of two high schools of comparable quality: YES, standard deviation SHOULD BE USED.
  Since both schools have comparable academic standards and are evaluated on the same 10-point scale, their mean scores are very close. Therefore, standard deviation provides an accurate, fair comparison of score consistency and student performance variance.
• b) For the revenue of 100 small retailers versus 100 large supermarkets: NO, standard deviation SHOULD NOT BE USED.
  Supermarket revenue operates on an incomparably larger scale than small corner stores (the means differ by orders of magnitude). Due to absolute scaling, supermarkets will inevitably show a vastly larger standard deviation regardless of relative consistency. In this scenario, the Coefficient of Variation ($CV = \frac{s}{\overline{x}}$) must be used to compare relative dispersion.
