## Coordinate Representations of Vector Addition, Subtraction, and Scalar Multiplication

\<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_phep_toan_vecto_oxyz"/>

### a) Coordinate Formulas for Linear Operations and Collinearity Condition

In three-dimensional space $Oxyz$, let $\vec{a} = (x; y; z)$, $\vec{b} = (x'; y'; z')$ and $k \in \mathbb{R}$:
• Vector addition: $\vec{a} + \vec{b} = (x + x'; y + y'; z + z')$.
• Vector subtraction: $\vec{a} - \vec{b} = (x - x'; y - y'; z - z')$.
• Scalar multiplication: $k\vec{a} = (kx; ky; kz)$.
• Opposite vector of $\vec{a} = (x; y; z)$ is $-\vec{a} = (-x; -y; -z)$.
• Collinearity condition: Vector $\vec{a} = (x; y; z)$ is collinear (parallel) to vector $\vec{b} = (x'; y'; z') \ne \vec{0}$ if and only if there exists a scalar $k \in \mathbb{R}$ such that:
$$\begin{cases} x = kx' \\\ y = ky' \\\ z = kz' \end{cases} \iff \frac{x}{x'} = \frac{y}{y'} = \frac{z}{z'} \quad (\text{provided } x', y', z' \ne 0).$$

### b) Coordinates of the Midpoint of a Segment and Centroid of a Triangle

Given points $A(x_A; y_A; z_A), B(x_B; y_B; z_B)$ and $C(x_C; y_C; z_C)$:
• The coordinates of the midpoint $M$ of line segment $AB$ are:
$$M\left(\frac{x_A + x_B}{2}; \frac{y_A + y_B}{2}; \frac{z_A + z_B}{2}\right)$$
• The coordinates of the centroid $G$ of triangle $ABC$ are:
$$G\left(\frac{x_A + x_B + x_C}{3}; \frac{y_A + y_B + y_C}{3}; \frac{z_A + z_B + z_C}{3}\right)$$

**Example 1:** In space $Oxyz$, given two vectors $\vec{a} = (2; 1; 5)$ and $\vec{b} = (2; 2; 1)$. Determine the coordinates of each vector: a) $\vec{a} - \vec{b}$; b) $3\vec{a} + 2\vec{b}$.

Solution for Example 1:
• a) $\vec{a} - \vec{b} = (2 - 2; 1 - 2; 5 - 1) = (0; -1; 4)$.
• b) We have $3\vec{a} = (6; 3; 15)$ and $2\vec{b} = (4; 4; 2)$, which yields $3\vec{a} + 2\vec{b} = (6 + 4; 3 + 4; 15 + 2) = (10; 7; 17)$.

**Practice 1:** In space $Oxyz$, given three vectors $\vec{u} = (1; 8; 6), \vec{v} = (-1; 3; -2)$, and $\vec{w} = (0; 5; 4)$. Find the coordinates of the vector $\vec{u} - 2\vec{v} + \vec{w}$.

Solution for Practice 1:
• We have $-2\vec{v} = (2; -6; 4)$.
• Thus: $\vec{u} - 2\vec{v} + \vec{w} = (1 + 2 + 0; 8 - 6 + 5; 6 + 4 + 4) = (3; 7; 14)$.

**Example 2:** In space $Oxyz$, given three points $A(1; 2; 3), B(3; 2; 1)$, and $C(2; -1; 5)$. Find the coordinates of the midpoint $M$ of segment $AB$ and the centroid $G$ of triangle $ABC$.

Solution for Example 2:
• Midpoint $M$ of $AB$: $M\left(\frac{1 + 3}{2}; \frac{2 + 2}{2}; \frac{3 + 1}{2}\right) \implies M(2; 2; 2)$.
• Centroid $G$ of $\triangle ABC$: $G\left(\frac{1 + 3 + 2}{3}; \frac{2 + 2 + (-1)}{3}; \frac{3 + 1 + 5}{3}\right) \implies G(2; 1; 3)$.

**Practice 2:** In space $Oxyz$, given points $A(2; 9; -1), B(9; 4; 5)$, and $G(3; 0; 4)$. Find the coordinates of point $C$ such that $G$ is the centroid of triangle $ABC$.

Solution for Practice 2:
• Since $G$ is the centroid of $\triangle ABC$, we have:
  $$\begin{cases} x_C = 3x_G - x_A - x_B = 3(3) - 2 - 9 = -2 \\\ y_C = 3y_G - y_A - y_B = 3(0) - 9 - 4 = -13 \\\ z_C = 3z_G - z_A - z_B = 3(4) - (-1) - 5 = 8 \end{cases}$$
• Therefore, the coordinates of $C$ are $C(-2; -13; 8)$.

## Coordinate Representation of the Dot Product and Geometric Applications

\<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_tich_vo_huong_oxyz"/>

### a) Dot Product Formula, Vector Magnitude, and Distance

Coordinate Formula for the Dot Product:
For two vectors $\vec{a} = (x; y; z)$ and $\vec{b} = (x'; y'; z')$, the dot product is defined by:
$$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = xx' + yy' + zz'$$
Key Geometric Consequences:
• Orthogonality condition: $\vec{a} \perp \vec{b} \iff \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0 \iff xx' + yy' + zz' = 0$.
• Magnitude (length) of a vector: $|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{a}} = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$.
• Distance between two points: For $A(x_A; y_A; z_A)$ and $B(x_B; y_B; z_B)$:
$$AB = |\vec{AB}| = \sqrt{(x_B - x_A)^2 + (y_B - y_A)^2 + (z_B - z_A)^2}$$
• Angle between two vectors: For non-zero vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b} \ne \vec{0}$:
$$\cos(\vec{a}, \vec{b}) = \frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{a}| \cdot |\vec{b}|} = \frac{xx' + yy' + zz'}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2} \cdot \sqrt{x'^2 + y'^2 + z'^2}}$$

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on the Dot Product

**Example 3:** In space $Oxyz$, given vectors $\vec{a} = (1; 4; 2)$ and $\vec{b} = (-4; 1; 0)$.
a) Compute $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$ and determine whether $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are orthogonal.
b) Find the magnitude of vector $\vec{a}$.

Solution for Example 3:
• a) $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 1(-4) + 4(1) + 2(0) = -4 + 4 + 0 = 0$. Since the dot product is 0, $\vec{a} \perp \vec{b}$.
• b) $|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 4^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{1 + 16 + 4} = \sqrt{21}$.

**Practice 3:** In Example 3, compute $(\vec{a} + \vec{b})^2$.

Solution for Practice 3:
• We have $\vec{a} + \vec{b} = (1 - 4; 4 + 1; 2 + 0) = (-3; 5; 2)$.
• Thus: $(\vec{a} + \vec{b})^2 = |\vec{a} + \vec{b}|^2 = (-3)^2 + 5^2 + 2^2 = 9 + 25 + 4 = 38$.
• (Alternative method: Since $\vec{a} \perp \vec{b} \implies \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$, we have $(\vec{a} + \vec{b})^2 = |\vec{a}|^2 + 2\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + |\vec{b}|^2 = 21 + 0 + (16 + 1) = 38$).

**Example 4:** Given a pyramid $S.ABCD$ with rectangular base $ABCD$ and $SA \perp (ABCD)$, where $SA = 2, AB = 3, AD = 4$. Choose a Cartesian coordinate system $Oxyz$ such that $O \equiv A$, with rays $Ox, Oy, Oz$ along rays $AB, AD, AS$ respectively.
a) Determine the coordinates of the vertices of the pyramid.
b) Compute the lengths of segments $BD$ and $SC$.
c) Find the angle between vectors $\vec{BD}$ and $\vec{SC}$.

Solution for Example 4:
• a) $A(0; 0; 0), B(3; 0; 0), D(0; 4; 0), S(0; 0; 2), C(3; 4; 0)$.
• b) $\vec{BD} = (-3; 4; 0) \implies BD = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + 4^2 + 0^2} = 5$.
  $\vec{SC} = (3; 4; -2) \implies SC = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2 + (-2)^2} = \sqrt{29}$.
• c) $\cos(\vec{BD}, \vec{SC}) = \frac{\vec{BD} \cdot \vec{SC}}{|\vec{BD}| \cdot |\vec{SC}|} = \frac{(-3)(3) + 4(4) + 0(-2)}{5\sqrt{29}} = \frac{7}{5\sqrt{29}} \approx 0.26 \implies (\vec{BD}, \vec{SC}) \approx 74.9^\circ$.

**Practice 4:** In space $Oxyz$, given points $A(0; 2; 1), B(3; -2; 1)$, and $C(-2; 5; 7)$.
a) Compute the perimeter of triangle $ABC$.
b) Compute the measure of angle $\widehat{BAC}$.

Solution for Practice 4:
• a) Edge vectors: $\vec{AB} = (3; -4; 0) \implies AB = \sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2 + 0^2} = 5$.
  $\vec{AC} = (-2; 3; 6) \implies AC = \sqrt{(-2)^2 + 3^2 + 6^2} = 7$.
  $\vec{BC} = (-5; 7; 6) \implies BC = \sqrt{(-5)^2 + 7^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{110} \approx 10.49$.
  Perimeter of $\triangle ABC$: $P = AB + AC + BC = 5 + 7 + \sqrt{110} = 12 + \sqrt{110} \approx 22.49$.
• b) We have $\cos \widehat{BAC} = \frac{\vec{AB} \cdot \vec{AC}}{|\vec{AB}| \cdot |\vec{AC}|} = \frac{3(-2) + (-4)(3) + 0(6)}{5 \times 7} = \frac{-18}{35} \approx -0.5143$.
  Hence, $\widehat{BAC} \approx 120.95^\circ$ (or $120^\circ 57'$).

## Applications of Vector Coordinates to Practical Problems

### a) Motion and Spatial Positioning Problems

**Example 5 (Aircraft Flight Trajectory):** Radar detects an airplane flying with constant speed and heading from $A(800; 500; 7)$ to $B(940; 550; 8)$ in 10 minutes. If the airplane maintains its speed and heading, determine its coordinates after an additional 5 minutes.

Solution for Example 5:
• Let $C(x; y; z)$ be the aircraft's position after the subsequent 5 minutes. Since the speed and heading are constant, $\vec{BC} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{AB}$.
• We have $\vec{AB} = (140; 50; 1) \implies \vec{BC} = (70; 25; 0.5)$.
• This gives: $\begin{cases} x - 940 = 70 \\\ y - 550 = 25 \\\ z - 8 = 0.5 \end{cases} \iff \begin{cases} x = 1\\,010 \\\ y = 575 \\\ z = 8.5 \end{cases}$.
• Thus, the aircraft's coordinates after the next 5 minutes are $C(1\\,010; 575; 8.5)$.

**Practice 5:** Under the assumptions of Example 5, determine the coordinates of the aircraft after an additional 10 minutes from point $B$.

Solution for Practice 5:
• Let $D(x; y; z)$ be the aircraft's position after 10 minutes from $B$.
• Because the elapsed time equals that from $A$ to $B$ (10 minutes), we have $\vec{BD} = \vec{AB} = (140; 50; 1)$.
• Hence: $\begin{cases} x - 940 = 140 \\\ y - 550 = 50 \\\ z - 8 = 1 \end{cases} \iff \begin{cases} x = 1\\,080 \\\ y = 600 \\\ z = 9 \end{cases} \implies D(1\\,080; 600; 9)$.

**Example 6 (Dimensions of a Triangular Prism Cabin - Introductory Problem):** A wooden cabin has the shape of a right triangular prism $OAB.O'A'B'$. A coordinate system $Oxyz$ (in cm) gives $A'(240; 450; 0)$ and $B'(120; 450; 300)$. Compute the dimensions of each direction of the cabin.

Solution for Example 6:
• Cabin length: The prism length is given by $OA = O'A' = 450\text{ cm}$.
• Cabin width: The base edge $OA'$ along the $Ox$-axis has length $240\text{ cm}$ (since $A'(240; 450; 0)$ and $O'(0; 450; 0)$).
• Roof edge length: $\vec{A'B'} = (-120; 0; 300) \implies A'B' = \sqrt{(-120)^2 + 0^2 + 300^2} = 60\sqrt{29} \approx 323\text{ cm}$.
• Roof ridge height from the ground corresponds to the $z$-coordinate of $B'$, which is $300\text{ cm}$.

**Practice 6:** In the introductory problem, determine the measure of the inclination angle $\alpha$ between the roof lateral face and the ground.

Solution for Practice 6:
• The ground lies in plane $(Oxy)$ with horizontal vector $\vec{A'O'} = (-240; 0; 0)$.
• The directional vector along the roof slope is $\vec{A'B'} = (-120; 0; 300)$.
• The angle $\alpha$ between the roof face and the ground equals $\widehat{B'A'O'}$:
  $$\cos \alpha = \frac{\vec{A'O'} \cdot \vec{A'B'}}{|\vec{A'O'}| \cdot |\vec{A'B'}|} = \frac{(-240)(-120) + 0 + 0}{240 \times 60\sqrt{29}} = \frac{28\\,800}{240 \times 323.11} = \frac{120}{60\sqrt{29}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{29}} \approx 0.3714.$$
• Thus, $\alpha = \arccos\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{29}}\right) \approx 68.2^\circ$.

**Example 7 (Distance Between Two Hot-Air Balloons):** Choose a coordinate system $Oxyz$ (in km) with origin $O$ at the launch site: $Ox$ points South, $Oy$ points East, and $Oz$ points vertically upward. The first balloon is located 2 km South, 1 km East, and 0.5 km above the ground. The second balloon is located 1 km North, 1.5 km West, and 0.8 km above the ground. Compute the straight-line distance between the two balloons.

Solution for Example 7:
• Coordinates of the first balloon: $M_1(2; 1; 0.5)$.
• Coordinates of the second balloon: North is negative along $Ox$ ($-1$), West is negative along $Oy$ ($-1.5$), altitude is $0.8 \implies M_2(-1; -1.5; 0.8)$.
• Distance between the two balloons:
  $$d = M_1M_2 = \sqrt{(-1 - 2)^2 + (-1.5 - 1)^2 + (0.8 - 0.5)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 6.25 + 0.09} = \sqrt{15.34} \approx 3.92\text{ (km)}.$$

**Practice 7:** In Example 7, which hot-air balloon is farther from the launch site?

Solution for Practice 7:
• Distance from the first balloon to $O$: $OM_1 = \sqrt{2^2 + 1^2 + 0.5^2} = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 0.25} = \sqrt{5.25} \approx 2.29\text{ km}$.
• Distance from the second balloon to $O$: $OM_2 = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + (-1.5)^2 + 0.8^2} = \sqrt{1 + 2.25 + 0.64} = \sqrt{3.89} \approx 1.97\text{ km}$.
• Since $OM_1 > OM_2$ ($2.29\text{ km} > 1.97\text{ km}$), the first balloon is farther from the launch site.

### b) Detailed Section Exercises

**Exercise 2.20:** In space $Oxyz$, given three vectors $\vec{a} = (3; 1; 2), \vec{b} = (-3; 0; 4)$, and $\vec{c} = (6; -1; 0)$.
a) Find the coordinates of $\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}$ and $2\vec{a} - 3\vec{b} - 5\vec{c}$.
b) Compute the dot products $\vec{a} \cdot (-\vec{b})$ and $(2\vec{a}) \cdot \vec{c}$.

Solution for Exercise 2.20:
• a) $\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c} = (3 - 3 + 6; 1 + 0 - 1; 2 + 4 + 0) = (6; 0; 6)$.
  $2\vec{a} = (6; 2; 4)$, $-3\vec{b} = (9; 0; -12)$, $-5\vec{c} = (-30; 5; 0)$.
  $2\vec{a} - 3\vec{b} - 5\vec{c} = (6 + 9 - 30; 2 + 0 + 5; 4 - 12 + 0) = (-15; 7; -8)$.
• b) $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 3(-3) + 1(0) + 2(4) = -9 + 0 + 8 = -1 \implies \vec{a} \cdot (-\vec{b}) = -(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}) = 1$.
  $(2\vec{a}) \cdot \vec{c} = 2(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{c}) = 2[3(6) + 1(-1) + 2(0)] = 2(18 - 1) = 34$.

**Exercise 2.21:** In space $Oxyz$, given points $M(-4; 3; 3), N(4; -4; 2)$, and $P(3; 6; -1)$.
a) Find coordinates of $\vec{MN}, \vec{MP}$, and prove $M, N, P$ are non-collinear.
b) Find $\vec{NM} + \vec{NP}$, and deduce the coordinates of $Q$ such that $MNPQ$ is a parallelogram.
c) Calculate the perimeter of parallelogram $MNPQ$.

Solution for Exercise 2.21:
• a) $\vec{MN} = (8; -7; -1)$ and $\vec{MP} = (7; 3; -4)$.
  Since $\frac{8}{7} \ne \frac{-7}{3}$, $\vec{MN}$ and $\vec{MP}$ are non-collinear. Hence, $M, N, P$ do not lie on a straight line.
• b) $\vec{NM} = (-8; 7; 1)$ and $\vec{NP} = (3 - 4; 6 - (-4); -1 - 2) = (-1; 10; -3)$.
  Thus, $\vec{NM} + \vec{NP} = (-9; 17; -2)$.
  For $MNPQ$ to be a parallelogram, $\vec{NQ} = \vec{NM} + \vec{NP} \implies \begin{cases} x_Q - 4 = -9 \\\ y_Q - (-4) = 17 \\\ z_Q - 2 = -2 \end{cases} \iff \begin{cases} x_Q = -5 \\\ y_Q = 13 \\\ z_Q = 0 \end{cases} \implies Q(-5; 13; 0)$.
• c) Side lengths: $MN = \sqrt{8^2 + (-7)^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{64 + 49 + 1} = \sqrt{114}$.
  $NP = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + 10^2 + (-3)^2} = \sqrt{1 + 100 + 9} = \sqrt{110}$.
  Perimeter of parallelogram $MNPQ$: $C = 2(MN + NP) = 2(\sqrt{114} + \sqrt{110}) \approx 42.33$.

**Exercise 2.22:** Given triangle $ABC$ with $A(1; 0; 1), B(0; -3; 1)$, and $C(4; -1; 4)$.
a) Find the coordinates of the centroid of triangle $ABC$.
b) Prove that $\widehat{BAC} = 90^\circ$.
c) Compute angle $\widehat{ABC}$.

Solution for Exercise 2.22:
• a) Centroid $G$: $x_G = \frac{1 + 0 + 4}{3} = \frac{5}{3}, y_G = \frac{0 - 3 - 1}{3} = -\frac{4}{3}, z_G = \frac{1 + 1 + 4}{3} = 2 \implies G\left(\frac{5}{3}; -\frac{4}{3}; 2\right)$.
• b) $\vec{AB} = (-1; -3; 0)$ and $\vec{AC} = (3; -1; 3)$.
  Dot product: $\vec{AB} \cdot \vec{AC} = (-1)(3) + (-3)(-1) + 0(3) = -3 + 3 + 0 = 0$.
  Since $\vec{AB} \cdot \vec{AC} = 0 \implies AB \perp AC \implies \widehat{BAC} = 90^\circ$.
• c) Because $\triangle ABC$ is a right triangle at $A$, $\tan \widehat{ABC} = \frac{AC}{AB}$.
  We have $AB = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + (-3)^2 + 0^2} = \sqrt{10}$ and $AC = \sqrt{3^2 + (-1)^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{19}$.
  Thus, $\cos \widehat{ABC} = \frac{AB}{BC} = \frac{AB}{\sqrt{AB^2 + AC^2}} = \frac{\sqrt{10}}{\sqrt{10 + 19}} = \sqrt{\frac{10}{29}} \approx 0.5872$.
  Hence, $\widehat{ABC} \approx 54.04^\circ$ (or $54^\circ 2'$).

**Exercise 2.23 (Ceiling Light Fixture Coordinates):** A classroom is designed as a rectangular box measuring 8 m long ($Ox$-axis), 6 m wide ($Oy$-axis), and 3 m high ($Oz$-axis). Origin $O$ is at a corner of the room, and floor is plane $(Oxy)$. A light is suspended at the exact center of the ceiling. Find the coordinates of the light fixture.

Solution for Exercise 2.23:
• The ceiling lies in the horizontal plane $z = 3\text{ m}$.
• The classroom length along $Ox$ is 8 m, and width along $Oy$ is 6 m.
• The midpoint of the ceiling projects onto the center of the rectangular floor:
  $$x = \frac{8}{2} = 4\text{ (m)}, \quad y = \frac{6}{2} = 3\text{ (m)}, \quad z = 3\text{ (m)}.$$
• Therefore, the coordinates of the light fixture are $I(4; 3; 3)$.

**Exercise 2.24 (Offshore Rig Radar Detection Range):** A coordinate system $Oxyz$ (in km) has origin $O$ at an offshore oil rig, with the sea surface in plane $(Oxy)$, $Ox$ pointing West, $Oy$ pointing South, and $Oz$ pointing vertically upward. The rig's radar has a monitoring range of 30 km. Can the radar detect an exploratory submarine vessel located at $(25; 15; -10)$? Explain.

Solution for Exercise 2.24:
• The straight-line distance from the radar at $O(0; 0; 0)$ to the vessel at $M(25; 15; -10)$ (negative altitude denotes depth below sea level) is:
  $$d = OM = \sqrt{25^2 + 15^2 + (-10)^2} = \sqrt{625 + 225 + 100} = \sqrt{950} \approx 30.82\text{ (km)}.$$
• Comparing with the radar tracking range: $d \approx 30.82\text{ km} > 30\text{ km}$.
• Conclusion: Since the distance exceeds the maximum 30 km radar range, the radar cannot detect the exploratory vessel.
