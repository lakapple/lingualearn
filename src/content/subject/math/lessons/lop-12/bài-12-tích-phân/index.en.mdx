### a) Area of a Curvilinear Trapezoid

Concept of a Curvilinear Trapezoid:
A plane region bounded by the graph of a continuous function $y = f(x)$, the $x$-axis, and two vertical lines $x = a$ and $x = b$ ($a < b$), where $f(x)$ is continuous and non-negative on $[a; b]$, is called a curvilinear trapezoid.

Theorem on the Area of a Curvilinear Trapezoid:
If the function $f(x)$ is continuous and non-negative on $[a; b]$, then the area $S$ of the curvilinear trapezoid bounded by the graph $y = f(x)$, the $x$-axis, and the lines $x = a$ and $x = b$ is given by:
$$S = F(b) - F(a)$$
where $F(x)$ is any antiderivative of $f(x)$ on the interval $[a; b]$.

**Example 1:** Determine whether the planar regions bounded by the following curves are curvilinear trapezoids:
a) The graph of $y = x^2$, the $x$-axis, and the lines $x = 1, x = 2$.
b) The graph of $y = x^3$, the $x$-axis, and the lines $x = 0, x = 1$.

Solution for Example 1:
• a) The function $f(x) = x^2$ is continuous and non-negative on $[1; 2]$. Therefore, the region bounded by the graph of $y = x^2$, the $x$-axis, and the lines $x = 1, x = 2$ is a curvilinear trapezoid.
• b) The function $f(x) = x^3$ is continuous and non-negative on $[0; 1]$. Therefore, the region bounded by the graph of $y = x^3$, the $x$-axis, and the lines $x = 0, x = 1$ is a curvilinear trapezoid.

**Example 2:** Find the area $S$ of the curvilinear trapezoid bounded by the graph of $y = f(x) = x^3$, the $x$-axis, and the lines $x = 1, x = 2$.

Solution for Example 2:
• An antiderivative of $f(x) = x^3$ on $[1; 2]$ is $F(x) = \frac{x^4}{4}$.
• By the theorem on the area of a curvilinear trapezoid, we have:
  $$S = F(2) - F(1) = \frac{2^4}{4} - \frac{1^4}{4} = 4 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{15}{4}.$$

### b) Definition and Geometric Interpretation of the Definite Integral

Definition of the Definite Integral:
Let $f(x)$ be a continuous function on $[a; b]$. If $F(x)$ is an antiderivative of $f(x)$ on $[a; b]$, then the difference $F(b) - F(a)$ is called the definite integral of $f(x)$ from $a$ to $b$, denoted by:
$$\int\_{a}^{b} f(x) dx$$
We denote $F(b) - F(a) = F(x)\Big|\_{a}^{b}$. Thus:
$$\int\_{a}^{b} f(x) dx = F(x)\Big|\_{a}^{b} = F(b) - F(a)$$
Conventions and Distinctive Properties:
• $a$ is called the lower limit, $b$ is the upper limit, $f(x)$ is the integrand, and $f(x)dx$ is the expression under the integral sign.
• Conventions: $\int\_{a}^{a} f(x) dx = 0$ and $\int\_{a}^{b} f(x) dx = -\int\_{b}^{a} f(x) dx$.
• The definite integral depends solely on the integrand $f$ and the limits $a, b$, and is independent of the choice of the integration variable:
  $$\int\_{a}^{b} f(x) dx = \int\_{a}^{b} f(t) dt = \int\_{a}^{b} f(u) du$$

Geometric Interpretation of the Definite Integral:
If $f(x)$ is continuous and non-negative on $[a; b]$, then the definite integral $\int\_{a}^{b} f(x) dx$ represents the exact area $S$ of the curvilinear trapezoid bounded by the graph $y = f(x)$, the $x$-axis, and the lines $x = a, x = b$:
$$S = \int\_{a}^{b} f(x) dx$$

**Example 3:** Evaluate the following definite integrals:
a) $\int\_{-1}^{3} x^2 dx$;
b) $\int\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{6}} \cos t dt$;
c) $\int\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{4}} \frac{du}{\cos^2 u}$;
d) $\int\_{1}^{2} 2^x dx$.

Solution for Example 3:
• a) $\int\_{-1}^{3} x^2 dx = \frac{x^3}{3}\Big|\_{-1}^{3} = \frac{3^3}{3} - \frac{(-1)^3}{3} = 9 - \left(-\frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{28}{3}$.
• b) $\int\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{6}} \cos t dt = \sin t\Big|\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{6}} = \sin\frac{\pi}{6} - \sin 0 = \frac{1}{2} - 0 = \frac{1}{2}$.
• c) $\int\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{4}} \frac{du}{\cos^2 u} = \tan u\Big|\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{4}} = \tan\frac{\pi}{4} - \tan 0 = 1 - 0 = 1$.
• d) $\int\_{1}^{2} 2^x dx = \frac{2^x}{\ln 2}\Big|\_{1}^{2} = \frac{2^2}{\ln 2} - \frac{2^1}{\ln 2} = \frac{4 - 2}{\ln 2} = \frac{2}{\ln 2}$.

**Practice 1:** Evaluate the following definite integrals:
a) $\int\_{0}^{1} e^x dx$;
b) $\int\_{1}^{e} \frac{1}{x} dx$;
c) $\int\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin x dx$;
d) $\int\_{\frac{\pi}{6}}^{\frac{\pi}{3}} \frac{dx}{\sin^2 x}$.

Solution for Practice 1:
• a) $\int\_{0}^{1} e^x dx = e^x\Big|\_{0}^{1} = e^1 - e^0 = e - 1$.
• b) $\int\_{1}^{e} \frac{1}{x} dx = \ln|x|\Big|\_{1}^{e} = \ln e - \ln 1 = 1 - 0 = 1$.
• c) $\int\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin x dx = -\cos x\Big|\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = -\cos\frac{\pi}{2} - (-\cos 0) = 0 - (-1) = 1$.
• d) $\int\_{\frac{\pi}{6}}^{\frac{\pi}{3}} \frac{dx}{\sin^2 x} = -\cot x\Big|\_{\frac{\pi}{6}}^{\frac{\pi}{3}} = -\cot\frac{\pi}{3} - \left(-\cot\frac{\pi}{6}\right) = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} + \sqrt{3} = \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}$.

**Example 4:** Using the geometric interpretation of the definite integral, compute:
a) $\int\_{0}^{1} (x + 1) dx$;
b) $\int\_{-1}^{1} \sqrt{1 - x^2} dx$.

Solution for Example 4:
• a) The function $y = x + 1$ is continuous and positive on $[0; 1]$.
  The integral represents the area of a right trapezoid bounded by $y = x + 1$, the $x$-axis, and the lines $x = 0, x = 1$.
  The trapezoid has lower base $f(0) = 1$, upper base $f(1) = 2$, and height $h = 1 - 0 = 1$.
  Therefore: $\int\_{0}^{1} (x + 1) dx = S = \frac{1 + 2}{2} \cdot 1 = \frac{3}{2}$.
• b) The curve $y = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$ on $[-1; 1]$ forms the upper half of a circle centered at $O(0; 0)$ with radius $R = 1$.
  The integral $\int\_{-1}^{1} \sqrt{1 - x^2} dx$ equals the area of this semi-circle.
  Thus: $\int\_{-1}^{1} \sqrt{1 - x^2} dx = \frac{1}{2}\pi R^2 = \frac{1}{2}\pi \cdot 1^2 = \frac{\pi}{2}$.

**Practice 2:** Using the geometric interpretation of the definite integral, compute:
a) $\int\_{1}^{3} (2x + 1) dx$;
b) $\int\_{-2}^{2} \sqrt{4 - x^2} dx$.

Solution for Practice 2:
• a) The graph of $y = 2x + 1$ on $[1; 3]$ forms a non-negative line segment.
  The corresponding region is a right trapezoid with bases $y(1) = 3, y(3) = 7$ and height $h = 3 - 1 = 2$.
  Applying the trapezoidal area formula:
  $$\int\_{1}^{3} (2x + 1) dx = S = \frac{3 + 7}{2} \cdot 2 = 10.$$
• b) The function $y = \sqrt{4 - x^2}$ defines the upper semi-circle centered at $O(0; 0)$ with radius $R = \sqrt{4} = 2$.
  The integral equals the area of this semi-circle of radius 2:
  $$\int\_{-2}^{2} \sqrt{4 - x^2} dx = \frac{1}{2}\pi R^2 = \frac{1}{2}\pi (2^2) = 2\pi.$$

**Application 1 (Car Braking Distance - Introductory Problem):** A car is traveling at $20\text{ m/s}$ when the driver applies the brakes. Following braking, the car decelerates according to velocity $v(t) = -40t + 20\text{ (m/s)}$, where $t$ is measured in seconds. How far does the car travel from the moment the brakes are applied until it comes to a complete stop?

Solution for Application 1:
• The car comes to a complete stop when its velocity reaches zero: $v(t) = 0 \iff -40t + 20 = 0 \iff t = \frac{20}{40} = 0.5\text{ (seconds)}$.
• The distance traveled by the car from braking ($t = 0$) until coming to a stop ($t = 0.5\text{ s}$) is:
  $$S = \int\_{0}^{0.5} v(t) dt = \int\_{0}^{0.5} (-40t + 20) dt = \left(-20t^2 + 20t\right)\Big|\_{0}^{0.5} = -20(0.5)^2 + 20(0.5) - 0 = -5 + 10 = 5\text{ (m)}.$$
• Thus, the car travels an additional $5\text{ meters}$ before stopping completely.

## Properties of Definite Integrals

\<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_tinh_chat_tich_phan"/>

### a) Basic Properties

Let $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ be continuous functions on $[a; b]$, and let $k$ be a real constant. Then:
1\. Scalar multiplication: $\int\_{a}^{b} k f(x) dx = k \int\_{a}^{b} f(x) dx$
2\. Additivity (Sum): $\int\_{a}^{b} [f(x) + g(x)] dx = \int\_{a}^{b} f(x) dx + \int\_{a}^{b} g(x) dx$
3\. Additivity (Difference): $\int\_{a}^{b} [f(x) - g(x)] dx = \int\_{a}^{b} f(x) dx - \int\_{a}^{b} g(x) dx$
4\. Interval additivity (Chasles' relation): For any $c$ (with $c \in (a; b)$ or any arbitrary real numbers $a, b, c$ within the domain):
$$\int\_{a}^{b} f(x) dx = \int\_{a}^{c} f(x) dx + \int\_{c}^{b} f(x) dx$$

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Definite Integral Properties

**Example 5:** Evaluate:
a) $\int\_{1}^{4} (x^3 + 3\sqrt{x}) dx$;
b) $\int\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} (e^x - 2\cos x) dx$;
c) $\int\_{1}^{4} \left(2^x - \frac{3}{x^2}\right) dx$.

Solution for Example 5:
• a) $\int\_{1}^{4} (x^3 + 3\sqrt{x}) dx = \int\_{1}^{4} x^3 dx + 3\int\_{1}^{4} x^{1/2} dx = \frac{x^4}{4}\Big|\_{1}^{4} + 3 \cdot \frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}\Big|\_{1}^{4}$
  $$= \frac{1}{4}(4^4 - 1^4) + 2(4^{3/2} - 1^{3/2}) = \frac{255}{4} + 2(8 - 1) = \frac{255}{4} + 14 = \frac{311}{4}.$$
• b) $\int\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} (e^x - 2\cos x) dx = \int\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} e^x dx - 2\int\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \cos x dx = e^x\Big|\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} - 2\sin x\Big|\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}$
  $$= \left(e^{\frac{\pi}{2}} - e^0\right) - 2(\sin\frac{\pi}{2} - \sin 0) = e^{\frac{\pi}{2}} - 1 - 2(1) = e^{\frac{\pi}{2}} - 3.$$
• c) $\int\_{1}^{4} \left(2^x - \frac{3}{x^2}\right) dx = \int\_{1}^{4} 2^x dx - 3\int\_{1}^{4} x^{-2} dx = \frac{2^x}{\ln 2}\Big|\_{1}^{4} + \frac{3}{x}\Big|\_{1}^{4}$
  $$= \frac{2^4 - 2^1}{\ln 2} + 3\left(\frac{1}{4} - 1\right) = \frac{14}{\ln 2} - \frac{9}{4}.$$



**Practice 3:** Evaluate the following definite integrals:
a) $\int\_{0}^{2\pi} (2x + \cos x) dx$;
b) $\int\_{1}^{2} \left(3^x - \frac{3}{x}\right) dx$;
c) $\int\_{\frac{\pi}{6}}^{\frac{\pi}{4}} \left(\frac{1}{\cos^2 x} - \frac{1}{\sin^2 x}\right) dx$.

Solution for Practice 3:
• a) $\int\_{0}^{2\pi} (2x + \cos x) dx = (x^2 + \sin x)\Big|\_{0}^{2\pi} = \left[(2\pi)^2 + \sin(2\pi)\right] - (0 + \sin 0) = 4\pi^2$.
• b) $\int\_{1}^{2} \left(3^x - \frac{3}{x}\right) dx = \left(\frac{3^x}{\ln 3} - 3\ln|x|\right)\Big|\_{1}^{2} = \left(\frac{9}{\ln 3} - 3\ln 2\right) - \left(\frac{3}{\ln 3} - 0\right) = \frac{6}{\ln 3} - 3\ln 2$.
• c) $\int\_{\frac{\pi}{6}}^{\frac{\pi}{4}} \left(\frac{1}{\cos^2 x} - \frac{1}{\sin^2 x}\right) dx = (\tan x + \cot x)\Big|\_{\frac{\pi}{6}}^{\frac{\pi}{4}}$
  $$= \left(\tan\frac{\pi}{4} + \cot\frac{\pi}{4}\right) - \left(\tan\frac{\pi}{6} + \cot\frac{\pi}{6}\right) = (1 + 1) - \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} + \sqrt{3}\right) = 2 - \frac{4\sqrt{3}}{3}.$$



**Example 6:** Evaluate the definite integral: $\int\_{0}^{3} |x - 2| dx$.

Solution for Example 6:
• Determine the sign of $x - 2$ on $[0; 3]$: $x - 2 \le 0$ on $[0; 2]$ and $x - 2 \ge 0$ on $[2; 3]$.
• Applying the interval additivity property with splitting point $c = 2$:
  $$\int\_{0}^{3} |x - 2| dx = \int\_{0}^{2} |x - 2| dx + \int\_{2}^{3} |x - 2| dx = \int\_{0}^{2} (2 - x) dx + \int\_{2}^{3} (x - 2) dx$$
  $$= \left(2x - \frac{x^2}{2}\right)\Big|\_{0}^{2} + \left(\frac{x^2}{2} - 2x\right)\Big|\_{2}^{3} = (4 - 2) + \left[\left(\frac{9}{2} - 6\right) - (2 - 4)\right] = 2 + \left(-\frac{3}{2} + 2\right) = 2 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{2}.$$

**Practice 4:** Evaluate the definite integral: $\int\_{0}^{2} |2x - 3| dx$.

Solution for Practice 4:
• Root of the linear binomial: $2x - 3 = 0 \iff x = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5 \in [0; 2]$.
• On $[0; 1.5]$, $2x - 3 \le 0 \implies |2x - 3| = 3 - 2x$.
• On $[1.5; 2]$, $2x - 3 \ge 0 \implies |2x - 3| = 2x - 3$.
• Splitting the integral at $x = 1.5$:
  $$\int\_{0}^{2} |2x - 3| dx = \int\_{0}^{1.5} (3 - 2x) dx + \int\_{1.5}^{2} (2x - 3) dx$$
  $$= \left(3x - x^2\right)\Big|\_{0}^{1.5} + \left(x^2 - 3x\right)\Big|\_{1.5}^{2} = \left(3(1.5) - 1.5^2\right) + \left[(4 - 6) - (1.5^2 - 3(1.5))\right]$$
  $$= (4.5 - 2.25) + [-2 - (2.25 - 4.5)] = 2.25 + (-2 + 2.25) = 2.25 + 0.25 = 2.5 = \frac{5}{2}.$$



## Applications and Exercises

### a) Real-World Applications

**Application 2 (Daily Mean Temperature):** The mean value of a continuous function $f(x)$ on $[a; b]$ is defined as $\frac{1}{b - a}\int\_{a}^{b} f(x) dx$. Temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$) at time $t$ (hours) from 6:00 AM to 12:00 PM in a locality is modeled by $T(t) = 20 + 1.5(t - 6)$ for $6 \le t \le 12$. Find the mean temperature during this time interval.

Solution for Application 2:
• The time interval from 6 AM to 12 PM corresponds to $[6; 12]$, with duration $b - a = 12 - 6 = 6$ hours.
• The mean temperature is:
  $$\overline{T} = \frac{1}{12 - 6}\int\_{6}^{12} [20 + 1.5(t - 6)] dt = \frac{1}{6}\int\_{6}^{12} (1.5t + 11) dt$$
  $$= \frac{1}{6}\left(0.75t^2 + 11t\right)\Big|\_{6}^{12} = \frac{1}{6}\left[(0.75 \cdot 144 + 11 \cdot 12) - (0.75 \cdot 36 + 11 \cdot 6)\right]$$
  $$= \frac{1}{6}\left[(108 + 132) - (27 + 66)\right] = \frac{1}{6}(240 - 93) = \frac{147}{6} = 24.5^\circ\text{C}.$$
• Thus, the mean temperature from 6:00 AM to 12:00 PM is $24.5^\circ\text{C}$.

**Exercise 4.11 (Rectilinear Motion - Displacement and Total Distance):** A particle moves along a straight line with velocity at time $t$ seconds given by $v(t) = t^2 - t - 6\text{ (m/s)}$.
a) Find the displacement of the particle over time interval $1 \le t \le 4$, i.e., compute $\int\_{1}^{4} v(t) dt$.
b) Find the total distance traveled by the particle over time interval $1 \le t \le 4$, i.e., compute $\int\_{1}^{4} |v(t)| dt$.

Solution for Exercise 4.11:
• a) The displacement of the particle is:
  $$\Delta s = \int\_{1}^{4} (t^2 - t - 6) dt = \left(\frac{t^3}{3} - \frac{t^2}{2} - 6t\right)\Big|\_{1}^{4}$$
  At $t = 4$: $\frac{64}{3} - 8 - 24 = \frac{64}{3} - 32 = -\frac{32}{3}$.
  At $t = 1$: $\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{2} - 6 = -\frac{37}{6}$.
  $$\Delta s = -\frac{32}{3} - \left(-\frac{37}{6}\right) = -\frac{64}{6} + \frac{37}{6} = -\frac{27}{6} = -4.5\text{ (m)}.$$
  (Negative displacement indicates the particle is located $4.5\text{ m}$ behind its starting position).
• b) Examining the sign of $v(t) = t^2 - t - 6 = (t - 3)(t + 2)$.
  On $[1; 3]$: $v(t) \le 0 \implies |v(t)| = -t^2 + t + 6$.
  On $[3; 4]$: $v(t) \ge 0 \implies |v(t)| = t^2 - t - 6$.
  The total actual distance traveled is:
  $$S = \int\_{1}^{4} |v(t)| dt = \int\_{1}^{3} (-t^2 + t + 6) dt + \int\_{3}^{4} (t^2 - t - 6) dt$$
  $$\int\_{1}^{3} (-t^2 + t + 6) dt = \left(-\frac{t^3}{3} + \frac{t^2}{2} + 6t\right)\Big|\_{1}^{3} = \left(-9 + \frac{9}{2} + 18\right) - \left(-\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2} + 6\right) = \frac{27}{2} - \frac{37}{6} = \frac{44}{6} = \frac{22}{3}.$$
  $$\int\_{3}^{4} (t^2 - t - 6) dt = \left(\frac{t^3}{3} - \frac{t^2}{2} - 6t\right)\Big|\_{3}^{4} = -\frac{32}{3} - \left(-\frac{27}{2}\right) = -\frac{32}{3} + \frac{27}{2} = \frac{17}{6}.$$
  $$S = \frac{44}{6} + \frac{17}{6} = \frac{61}{6} \approx 10.17\text{ (m)}.$$
  Thus, the total distance traveled is $\frac{61}{6}\text{ m} \approx 10.17\text{ m}$.

**Exercise 4.12 (Profit Variation from Marginal Profit):** The marginal profit (in million VND) of a product is modeled by $P'(x) = -0.0005x + 12.2$, where $x$ is the sales volume.
a) Find the change in profit when sales increase from 100 to 101 units.
b) Find the change in profit when sales increase from 100 to 110 units.

Solution for Exercise 4.12:
• The change in profit when sales increase from $a$ to $b$ is $\Delta P = P(b) - P(a) = \int\_{a}^{b} P'(x) dx$.
• a) When sales increase from 100 to 101 units:
  $$\Delta P_1 = \int\_{100}^{101} (-0.0005x + 12.2) dx = \left(-0.00025x^2 + 12.2x\right)\Big|\_{100}^{101}$$
  $$= -0.00025(101^2 - 100^2) + 12.2(101 - 100) = -0.00025(201) + 12.2 = -0.05025 + 12.2 = 12.14975\text{ (million VND)}.$$
• b) When sales increase from 100 to 110 units:
  $$\Delta P_2 = \int\_{100}^{110} (-0.0005x + 12.2) dx = \left(-0.00025x^2 + 12.2x\right)\Big|\_{100}^{110}$$
  $$= -0.00025(110^2 - 100^2) + 12.2(110 - 100) = -0.00025(2\\,100) + 122 = -0.525 + 122 = 121.475\text{ (million VND)}.$$



**Exercise 4.13 (Blood Velocity in an Artery):** The blood velocity $v$ at distance $r$ from the central axis of a cylindrical artery of radius $R$ is modeled by $v(r) = k(R^2 - r^2)$ ($0 \le r \le R$, $k$ is a positive constant). Find the mean velocity of blood flow across the cross-section of the artery and compare it with the maximum velocity.

Solution for Exercise 4.13:
• The mean blood velocity across radius $r$ on interval $[0; R]$ is:
  $$\overline{v} = \frac{1}{R - 0}\int\_{0}^{R} k(R^2 - r^2) dr = \frac{k}{R}\left(R^2 r - \frac{r^3}{3}\right)\Big|\_{0}^{R} = \frac{k}{R}\left(R^3 - \frac{R^3}{3}\right) = \frac{k}{R} \cdot \frac{2}{3}R^3 = \frac{2}{3}kR^2.$$
• The maximum blood velocity occurs at the central axis ($r = 0$):
  $$v\_{\max} = v(0) = k(R^2 - 0^2) = kR^2.$$
• Comparison: We have $\overline{v} = \frac{2}{3}v\_{\max}$. Thus, the mean velocity of blood flow equals $\frac{2}{3}$ of the maximum velocity at the center of the artery.

a) $\int\_{1}^{2} (2x + 1) dx$;
b) $\int\_{-3}^{3} \sqrt{9 - x^2} dx$.

Solution for Exercise 4.8:

**Exercise 4.9:** Given $\int\_{0}^{3} f(x) dx = 5$ and $\int\_{0}^{3} g(x) dx = 2$. Evaluate:
a) $\int\_{0}^{3} [f(x) + g(x)] dx$;
b) $\int\_{0}^{3} [f(x) - g(x)] dx$;
c) $\int\_{0}^{3} 3f(x) dx$;
d) $\int\_{0}^{3} [2f(x) - 3g(x)] dx$.

Solution for Exercise 4.9:
• a) $\int\_{0}^{3} [f(x) + g(x)] dx = \int\_{0}^{3} f(x) dx + \int\_{0}^{3} g(x) dx = 5 + 2 = 7$.
• b) $\int\_{0}^{3} [f(x) - g(x)] dx = \int\_{0}^{3} f(x) dx - \int\_{0}^{3} g(x) dx = 5 - 2 = 3$.
• c) $\int\_{0}^{3} 3f(x) dx = 3\int\_{0}^{3} f(x) dx = 3(5) = 15$.
• d) $\int\_{0}^{3} [2f(x) - 3g(x)] dx = 2\int\_{0}^{3} f(x) dx - 3\int\_{0}^{3} g(x) dx = 2(5) - 3(2) = 10 - 6 = 4$.

**Exercise 4.10:** Evaluate the following definite integrals:
a) $\int\_{0}^{1} (3x - 1)^2 dx$;
b) $\int\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} (1 + \sin x) dx$;
c) $\int\_{0}^{1} (e^{2x} + 3x^2) dx$;
d) $\int\_{-1}^{2} |2x + 1| dx$.

Solution for Exercise 4.10:
• a) Expanding the integrand: $(3x - 1)^2 = 9x^2 - 6x + 1$.
  $$\int\_{0}^{1} (3x - 1)^2 dx = \left(3x^3 - 3x^2 + x\right)\Big|\_{0}^{1} = (3 - 3 + 1) - 0 = 1.$$
• b) $\int\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} (1 + \sin x) dx = (x - \cos x)\Big|\_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = \left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \cos\frac{\pi}{2}\right) - (0 - \cos 0) = \left(\frac{\pi}{2} - 0\right) - (-1) = \frac{\pi}{2} + 1$.
• c) $\int\_{0}^{1} (e^{2x} + 3x^2) dx = \left(\frac{1}{2}e^{2x} + x^3\right)\Big|\_{0}^{1} = \left(\frac{1}{2}e^2 + 1\right) - \left(\frac{1}{2}e^0 + 0\right) = \frac{1}{2}e^2 + 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{e^2 + 1}{2}$.
• d) Analyzing the sign of $2x + 1$: $2x + 1 = 0 \iff x = -\frac{1}{2} \in [-1; 2]$.
  Splitting the integral into $[-1; -0.5]$ and $[-0.5; 2]$:
  $$\int\_{-1}^{2} |2x + 1| dx = \int\_{-1}^{-1/2} (-2x - 1) dx + \int\_{-1/2}^{2} (2x + 1) dx$$
  $$= \left(-x^2 - x\right)\Big|\_{-1}^{-1/2} + \left(x^2 + x\right)\Big|\_{-1/2}^{2}$$
  $$= \left[\left(-\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\right) - (-1 + 1)\right] + \left[(4 + 2) - \left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2}\right)\right]$$
  $$= \frac{1}{4} + \left(6 + \frac{1}{4}\right) = \frac{1}{4} + 6.25 = 6.5 = \frac{13}{2}.$$
