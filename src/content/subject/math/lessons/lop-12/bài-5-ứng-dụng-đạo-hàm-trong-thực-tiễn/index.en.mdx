## Rates of Change of a Quantity

### a) Concept and Practical Significance of the Derivative

Instantaneous Rate of Change:
Let $y = f(x)$ be a function of $x$. When $x$ changes from $x_1$ to $x_2$, the corresponding increments are $\Delta x = x_2 - x_1$ and $\Delta y = f(x_2) - f(x_1)$.
• The ratio $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(x_2) - f(x_1)}{x_2 - x_1}$ represents the average rate of change of $y$ with respect to $x$ over the interval $[x_1; x_2]$.
• The limit $\lim\_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = f'(x_1)$ is called the instantaneous rate of change of $y = f(x)$ with respect to $x$ at $x = x_1$.

Significance across Science and Everyday Life:
• Physics: If $s = s(t)$ is the position function of an object moving along a straight line, then $v(t) = s'(t)$ is its instantaneous velocity, and $a(t) = v'(t) = s''(t)$ is its instantaneous acceleration at time $t$.
• Chemistry: If $C(t)$ denotes the concentration of a reactant at time $t$, then $C'(t)$ represents the instantaneous reaction rate.
• Biology: If $P(t)$ represents the size of a biological population at time $t$, then $P'(t)$ represents the instantaneous growth rate of the population.
• Economics: If $C(x)$ is the total cost of producing $x$ units of a good, then the derivative $C'(x)$ is called the marginal cost. Practically, $C'(x)$ approximates the cost of producing one additional unit, namely the $(x + 1)$-th unit.

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Rates of Change

**Example 1:** Neglecting air resistance, the height (in meters) of an object projected vertically upward from an initial height of 2 m with an initial velocity $v_0 = 24.5\text{ m/s}$ is given by $h(t) = 2 + 24.5t - 4.9t^2$.
a) Find the velocity of the object after 2 seconds.
b) When does the object reach its maximum height, and what is this maximum height?
c) When does the object hit the ground, and what is its velocity upon impact?

Solution for Example 1:
• a) The instantaneous velocity of the object is: $v(t) = h'(t) = 24.5 - 9.8t\text{ (m/s)}$.
  After 2 seconds, the velocity is: $v(2) = 24.5 - 9.8(2) = 4.9\text{ (m/s)}$.
• b) Setting $h'(t) = 0 \iff 24.5 - 9.8t = 0 \iff t = 2.5\text{ (seconds)}$.
  Since $a = -4.9 < 0$, $h(t)$ attains its maximum at $t = 2.5\text{ s}$.
  The maximum height achieved is: $h(2.5) = 2 + 24.5(2.5) - 4.9(2.5)^2 = 32.625\text{ (m)}$.
• c) The object strikes the ground when $h(t) = 0 \iff 2 + 24.5t - 4.9t^2 = 0$.
  Solving this quadratic equation (for $t > 0$) yields $t \approx 5.08\text{ (seconds)}$.
  The velocity at impact is: $v(5.08) = 24.5 - 9.8(5.08) \approx -25.284\text{ (m/s)}$. The negative sign indicates that the object is traveling downward, opposite to the chosen upward positive direction.

**Example 2:** The population of a yeast culture in a laboratory environment is modeled by $P(t) = \frac{a}{b + e^{-0.75t}}$ ($t$ in hours). At initial time $t = 0$, the culture contains 20 cells and is growing at a rate of 12 cells/hour. Find $a, b$ and describe the long-term behavior of the yeast population.

Solution for Example 2:
• Derivative: $P'(t) = \frac{0.75a e^{-0.75t}}{(b + e^{-0.75t})^2}$.
• Given $P(0) = 20$ and $P'(0) = 12$, we obtain the system of equations:
  $$\begin{cases} \frac{a}{b + 1} = 20 \\\ \frac{0.75a}{(b + 1)^2} = 12 \end{cases} \iff \begin{cases} a = 20(b + 1) \\\ \frac{15(b + 1)}{(b + 1)^2} = 12 \end{cases} \iff \begin{cases} b + 1 = \frac{15}{12} = 1.25 \\\ a = 20(1.25) = 25 \end{cases} \iff \begin{cases} a = 25 \\\ b = 0.25 \end{cases}.$$
• Hence, $P(t) = \frac{25}{0.25 + e^{-0.75t}}$. Since $P'(t) > 0$ for all $t \ge 0$, the population is strictly increasing over time.
• In the long run ($t \to +\infty$): $\lim\_{t \to +\infty} P(t) = \lim\_{t \to +\infty} \frac{25}{0.25 + e^{-0.75t}} = \frac{25}{0.25} = 100$. The population approaches a carrying capacity of 100 cells without ever exceeding it.

**Example 3:** The total cost $C(x)$ (in thousand VND) of producing $x$ units of a commodity is given by $C(x) = 30\\,000 + 300x - 2.5x^2 + 0.125x^3$.
a) Find the marginal cost function.
b) Compute $C'(200)$ and interpret its economic meaning.
c) Compare $C'(200)$ with the actual cost of producing the 201st unit.

Solution for Example 3:
• a) Marginal cost function: $C'(x) = 300 - 5x + 0.375x^2$ (thousand VND/unit).
• b) At $x = 200$: $C'(200) = 300 - 5(200) + 0.375(200^2) = 14\\,300$ (thousand VND).
  Interpretation: Producing one additional unit beyond 200 (the 201st unit) will incur an estimated additional cost of approximately $14\\,300$ thousand VND ($14.3$ million VND).
• c) The actual cost of producing the 201st unit is: $\Delta C = C(201) - C(200) = 1\\,004\\,372.625 - 990\\,000 = 14\\,372.625$ (thousand VND).
  This actual value of $14\\,372.625$ thousand VND is very close to the marginal cost estimate $C'(200) = 14\\,300$ thousand VND.

**Practice 1:** A patient's systolic blood pressure $P$ (in mmHg) $t$ seconds after blood leaves the heart is modeled by $P(t) = \frac{25t^2 + 125}{t^2 + 1}$ ($0 \le t \le 10$). Compute the rate of change of blood pressure after 5 seconds.

Solution for Practice 1:
• Differentiating: $P'(t) = \frac{50t(t^2 + 1) - (25t^2 + 125)(2t)}{(t^2 + 1)^2} = \frac{50t^3 + 50t - 50t^3 - 250t}{(t^2 + 1)^2} = \frac{-200t}{(t^2 + 1)^2}$.
• At $t = 5\text{ s}$: $P'(5) = \frac{-200(5)}{(5^2 + 1)^2} = \frac{-1\\,000}{26^2} = \frac{-1\\,000}{676} \approx -1.48\text{ (mmHg/second)}$.
• The negative sign indicates that the systolic blood pressure is dropping at a rate of approximately $1.48\text{ mmHg/second}$.

## Simple Optimization Problems

\<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_bai_toan_toi_uu_hoa"/>

### a) Solution Procedure and Economic Modeling

Three-Step Procedure for Solving Optimization Problems:
• Step 1: Identify the target quantity $Q$ to be maximized or minimized and express it in terms of the variables involved.
• Step 2: Select a single appropriate primary variable $x$, express all other quantities in terms of $x$, establish the objective function $Q = Q(x)$, and specify its practical domain.
• Step 3: Find the absolute maximum or minimum of $Q(x)$ on its domain using differential calculus, verify with a variation table, and conclude.

Core Enterprise Economic Functions:
• Demand function (price function): $p = p(x)$ represents the unit price at which $x$ units can be sold ($p(x)$ is typically decreasing with respect to $x$).
• Revenue function: $R(x) = x \cdot p(x)$. Its derivative $R'(x)$ is the marginal revenue.
• Profit function: $P(x) = R(x) - C(x)$ (where $C(x)$ is the cost function). Its derivative $P'(x) = R'(x) - C'(x)$ is the marginal profit.
• Profit maximization criterion: Profit is maximized when marginal revenue equals marginal cost: $P'(x) = 0 \iff R'(x) = C'(x)$.

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Optimization

**Example 5:** A manufacturer plans to produce cylindrical cans with a volume $V = 1\text{ liter} = 1\\,000\text{ cm}^3$. Determine the base radius $r$ and height $h$ that minimize the total surface area (and thus material cost).

Solution for Example 5:
• Volume constraint: $V = \pi r^2 h = 1\\,000 \implies h = \frac{1\\,000}{\pi r^2}$ ($r > 0$).
• Total surface area of the cylinder:
  $$S(r) = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r \left(\frac{1\\,000}{\pi r^2}\right) = 2\pi r^2 + \frac{2\\,000}{r}, \quad r \in (0; +\infty).$$
• Derivative: $S'(r) = 4\pi r - \frac{2\\,000}{r^2} = \frac{4\pi r^3 - 2\\,000}{r^2}$.
• Setting $S'(r) = 0 \iff 4\pi r^3 = 2\\,000 \iff r^3 = \frac{500}{\pi} \iff r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{500}{\pi}} \approx 5.42\text{ (cm)}$.
• Corresponding height:
  $$h = \frac{1\\,000}{\pi r^2} = 2 \left(\frac{500}{\pi r^2}\right) = 2r \approx 10.84\text{ (cm)}.$$
• The variation table verifies that $S(r)$ achieves its global minimum at $r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{500}{\pi}}$.
• Key Geometric Insight: For any closed cylindrical container of fixed volume, material consumption is minimized when the height equals the base diameter ($h = 2r$).

**Practice 2:** An rows a boat from point $A$ on the bank of a straight river 3 km wide to reach point $B$ located 8 km downstream on the opposite bank as quickly as possible. His rowing speed is 6 km/h and his running speed is 8 km/h. At what point $D$ on the opposite bank (where $CD = x$, with $C$ being directly opposite $A$) should he land to minimize total travel time?

Solution for Practice 2:
• Rowing distance: $AD = \sqrt{AC^2 + CD^2} = \sqrt{3^2 + x^2} = \sqrt{x^2 + 9}$ (km) with $0 \le x \le 8$.
• Running distance: $DB = 8 - x$ (km).
• Total transit time function:
  $$T(x) = \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}}{6} + \frac{8 - x}{8}, \quad x \in [0; 8].$$
• Differentiating: $T'(x) = \frac{x}{6\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} - \frac{1}{8}$.
• Setting $T'(x) = 0 \iff 8x = 6\sqrt{x^2 + 9} \iff 4x = 3\sqrt{x^2 + 9} \iff 16x^2 = 9(x^2 + 9) \iff 7x^2 = 81 \iff x = \frac{9}{\sqrt{7}} \approx 3.4\text{ (km)}$.
• Comparing boundary and critical values: $T(0) = \frac{3}{6} + 1 = 1.5\text{ h}$; $T(8) = \frac{\sqrt{73}}{6} \approx 1.424\text{ h}$; $T\left(\frac{9}{\sqrt{7}}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{7}}{8} + 1 \approx 1.33\text{ h}$.
• An should land at point $D$ located $x = \frac{9}{\sqrt{7}} \approx 3.4\text{ km}$ downstream from $C$ to reach $B$ in the shortest possible time.

**Example 6:** Solving the introductory stadium ticketing problem: A soccer stadium has a seating capacity of 55,000 spectators. At a ticket price of 100 thousand VND, average attendance is 27,000. Market research shows that for each 10 thousand VND price reduction, attendance increases by 3,000. What ticket price maximizes ticket revenue?

Solution for Example 6:
• Let $p$ (thousand VND) denote the ticket price; $x$ denotes the number of spectators.
• A price drop of 10 thousand VND increases attendance by 3,000, so the rate of change is $\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta p} = \frac{3\\,000}{-10} = -300$.
• Linear demand function: $x - 27\\,000 = -300(p - 100) \implies x(p) = 57\\,000 - 300p$.
• Total revenue function:
  $$R(p) = p \cdot x(p) = p(57\\,000 - 300p) = -300p^2 + 57\\,000p.$$
• Differentiating: $R'(p) = -600p + 57\\,000$. Setting $R'(p) = 0 \iff p = \frac{57\\,000}{600} = 95\text{ (thousand VND)}$.
• The corresponding attendance is $x = 57\\,000 - 300(95) = 28\\,500 < 55\\,000$, well within stadium capacity.
• Maximum revenue achieved: $R(95) = 2\\,707\\,500$ thousand VND (over $2.7$ billion VND).
• Therefore, the organizers should set the ticket price at 95 thousand VND.

## Applications and Exercises

### a) Real-World Economic and Engineering Problems

**Application (Optimal Television Pricing):** A manufacturer sells an average of 1,000 flat-screen TVs per week at 14 million VND each. A market survey reveals that for each 500 thousand VND ($0.5$ million VND) discount, sales volume increases by 100 TVs weekly.
a) Find the demand function $p(x)$.
b) How much discount should the company offer to maximize revenue?
c) If the weekly cost function is $C(x) = 12\\,000 + 3x$ (in million VND), what selling price maximizes profit?

Solution for Application:
• a) Let $x$ be the number of TVs sold weekly. An increase of 100 TVs corresponds to a price drop of $0.5$ million VND, so slope $m = \frac{-0.5}{100} = -0.005$.
  Demand function: $p(x) - 14 = -0.005(x - 1\\,000) \implies p(x) = 19 - 0.005x$ (million VND/TV).
• b) Revenue function: $R(x) = x \cdot p(x) = 19x - 0.005x^2$.
  $R'(x) = 19 - 0.01x = 0 \iff x = 1\\,900$ (TVs).
  Corresponding price: $p(1\\,900) = 19 - 0.005(1\\,900) = 9.5$ (million VND).
  Optimal discount: $14 - 9.5 = 4.5$ (million VND/TV).
• c) Profit function: $P(x) = R(x) - C(x) = (19x - 0.005x^2) - (12\\,000 + 3x) = -0.005x^2 + 16x - 12\\,000$.
  $P'(x) = -0.01x + 16 = 0 \iff x = 1\\,600$ (TVs).
  Selling price for maximum profit: $p(1\\,600) = 19 - 0.005(1\\,600) = 11$ (million VND/TV).

**Exercise 1.28 (Optimal Apartment Rental Rate):** The property manager of a 100-unit apartment complex finds that all units are occupied when rent is 8 million VND/month. For each 100 thousand VND ($0.1$ million VND) increase in monthly rent, one unit becomes vacant. What monthly rental rate maximizes gross revenue?

Solution for Exercise 1.28:
• Let $x$ denote the number of vacant apartments ($0 \le x \le 100, x \in \mathbb{N}$). The number of rented apartments is $100 - x$.
• The monthly rent per occupied unit becomes: $p(x) = 8 + 0.1x$ (million VND/month).
• Total monthly rental revenue:
  $$R(x) = (100 - x)(8 + 0.1x) = 800 + 10x - 8x - 0.1x^2 = -0.1x^2 + 2x + 800.$$
• Differentiating: $R'(x) = -0.2x + 2 = 0 \iff x = 10$ (vacant units).
• Since $a = -0.1 < 0$, $R(x)$ attains its absolute maximum at $x = 10$.
• Optimal rental rate per unit: $p(10) = 8 + 0.1(10) = 9$ (million VND/month).
• Maximum monthly revenue: $R(10) = 90 \times 9 = 810$ (million VND/month).

### b) Detailed Section Exercises

**Exercise 1.26:** A particle moves along a vertical axis with position given by $y = t^3 - 12t + 3$ ($t \ge 0$, in meters, seconds).
a) Find the velocity and acceleration functions.
b) When is the particle moving upward, and when is it moving downward?
c) Find the total distance traveled during the time interval $0 \le t \le 3$.
d) When is the particle speeding up? When is it slowing down?

Solution for Exercise 1.26:
• a) Velocity: $v(t) = y'(t) = 3t^2 - 12\text{ (m/s)}$. Acceleration: $a(t) = v'(t) = 6t\text{ (m/s}^2\text{)}$.
• b) Setting $v(t) = 0 \iff 3t^2 - 12 = 0 \iff t = 2\text{ s}$ (since $t \ge 0$):
  + The particle moves upward when $v(t) > 0 \iff t \in (2; +\infty)$;
  + The particle moves downward when $v(t) < 0 \iff t \in (0; 2)$.
• c) At $t = 0$: $y(0) = 3\text{ m}$.
  At $t = 2$: $y(2) = 2^3 - 12(2) + 3 = -13\text{ m} \implies s_1 = |(-13) - 3| = 16\text{ m}$.
  At $t = 3$: $y(3) = 3^3 - 12(3) + 3 = -6\text{ m} \implies s_2 = |(-6) - (-13)| = 7\text{ m}$.
  Total distance traveled: $S = s_1 + s_2 = 16 + 7 = 23\text{ (m)}$.
• d) The particle is speeding up when velocity and acceleration share the same sign: $v(t) \cdot a(t) > 0 \iff 6t(3t^2 - 12) > 0 \iff t > 2\text{ s}$.
  The particle is slowing down when velocity and acceleration have opposite signs: $v(t) \cdot a(t) < 0 \iff 0 < t < 2\text{ s}$.

**Exercise 1.27:** The cost (in hundred thousand VND) of producing $x$ units of a product is $C(x) = 23\\,000 + 50x - 0.5x^2 + 0.00175x^3$.
a) Find the marginal cost function.
b) Compute $C'(100)$ and explain its meaning.
c) Compare $C'(100)$ with the actual cost of producing the 101st unit.

Solution for Exercise 1.27:
• a) Marginal cost function: $C'(x) = 50 - x + 0.00525x^2$ (hundred thousand VND/unit).
• b) At $x = 100$: $C'(100) = 50 - 100 + 0.00525(100^2) = -50 + 52.5 = 2.5$ (hundred thousand VND).
  Meaning: The estimated cost to produce the additional 101st unit is $2.5$ hundred thousand VND ($250\\,000$ VND).
• c) The actual cost of producing the 101st unit is:
  $$C(101) - C(100) = 23\\,352.53 - 23\\,350 = 2.53\text{ (hundred thousand VND)}.$$
  This actual value matches the marginal cost prediction $C'(100) = 2.5$ hundred thousand VND very closely.

**Exercise 1.29:** The demand function for a good is $p = \frac{354}{1 + 0.01x}$ ($x \ge 0$, $p$ in thousand VND).
a) Express sales volume $x$ as a function of price $p$ and determine its domain. Find $x$ when $p = 240$ thousand VND.
b) Analyze the behavior of $x(p)$. Describe how demand changes as price rises and interpret the economic meaning of $\lim\_{p \to 0^+} x(p)$.

Solution for Exercise 1.29:
• a) Solving for $x$: $1 + 0.01x = \frac{354}{p} \iff 0.01x = \frac{354}{p} - 1 \iff x(p) = \frac{35\\,400}{p} - 100$.
  Since $x \ge 0 \iff \frac{354}{p} \ge 1 \iff 0 < p \le 354$. Practical domain: $D_p = (0; 354]$.
  When $p = 240$: $x(240) = \frac{35\\,400}{240} - 100 = 147.5 - 100 = 47.5$ (units).
• b) Analysis of $x(p) = \frac{35\\,400}{p} - 100$ on $(0; 354]$:
  - Derivative: $x'(p) = -\frac{35\\,400}{p^2} < 0$ for all $p \in (0; 354]$. Thus, $x(p)$ is strictly decreasing, meaning demand steadily declines as unit price increases.
  - Limiting behavior: $\lim\_{p \to 0^+} x(p) = \lim\_{p \to 0^+} \left(\frac{35\\,400}{p} - 100\right) = +\infty$.
  - Economic Interpretation: As the selling price approaches zero (the good becomes virtually free), market demand grows without bound.
