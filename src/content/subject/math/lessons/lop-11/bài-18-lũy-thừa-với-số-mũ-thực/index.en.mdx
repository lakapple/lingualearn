## Powers with Integer Exponents

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_luy_thua_so_mu_nguyen"/>

### a) Definition and Properties

Let $n$ be a positive integer:
• For any real number $a$: $a^n = \underbrace{a \cdot a \cdot \dots \cdot a}\_{n \text{ factors}}$.
• For any non-zero real number $a$: $a^0 = 1$; $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$.
In the expression $a^m$, $a$ is called the base and $m$ is called the exponent.
Note: $0^0$ and $0^{-n}$ ($n \in \mathbb{N}^*$) are undefined.
Properties: For $a \ne 0, b \ne 0$ and $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}$, we have:
• $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$;
• $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$;
• $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$;
• $(ab)^m = a^m b^m$;
• $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^m = \frac{a^m}{b^m}$.
Comparing powers:
• If $a > 1$, then $a^m > a^n \iff m > n$.
• If $0 < a < 1$, then $a^m > a^n \iff m < n$.

### b) Examples and Practice on Integer Exponents

**Example 1:** Evaluate the expression: $A = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-8} \cdot 8^{-2} + (0{,}2)^{-4} \cdot 25^{-2}$.

Solution to Example 1:
• We have:
  $$A = 2^8 \cdot \frac{1}{8^2} + \frac{1}{0{,}2^4} \cdot \frac{1}{25^2} = 2^8 \cdot \frac{1}{2^6} + \frac{1}{0{,}2^4 \cdot 5^4} = 2^2 + \frac{1}{(0{,}2 \cdot 5)^4} = 4 + 1 = 5.$$

**Practice 1:** A positive number $x$ is written in scientific notation if $x = a \cdot 10^m$, where $1 \le a < 10$ and $m \in \mathbb{Z}$. Write the following data in scientific notation:
a) The mass of the Earth is approximately $5\\,980\\,000\\,000\\,000\\,000\\,000\\,000\\,000\text{ kg}$;
b) The mass of a proton is approximately $0{,}000\\,000\\,000\\,000\\,000\\,000\\,000\\,000\\,001\\,672\\,62\text{ kg}$.

Solution to Practice 1:
• a) Mass of the Earth: $5{,}98 \cdot 10^{24}\text{ kg}$.
• b) Mass of a proton: $1{,}67262 \cdot 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$.

## Powers with Rational Exponents

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_luy_thua_so_mu_huu_ti"/>

### a) Concept of the n-th Root

Let $a$ be a real number and $n$ be a positive integer. A number $b$ is called an $n$-th root of $a$ if $b^n = a$.
• When $n$ is odd: Every real number $a$ has a unique $n$-th root, denoted by $\sqrt[n]{a}$.
• When $n$ is even: Every positive real number $a$ has exactly two $n$-th roots that are opposites of each other; the positive value is denoted by $\sqrt[n]{a}$ (called the principal $n$-th root), and the negative value is denoted by $-\sqrt[n]{a}$. Negative numbers have no even roots in $\mathbb{R}$. $\sqrt[n]{0} = 0$.
Properties of roots ($a, b > 0$):
• $\sqrt[n]{a} \cdot \sqrt[n]{b} = \sqrt[n]{ab}$;
• $\frac{\sqrt[n]{a}}{\sqrt[n]{b}} = \sqrt[n]{\frac{a}{b}}$;
• $(\sqrt[n]{a})^m = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$;
• $\sqrt[n]{\sqrt[k]{a}} = \sqrt[nk]{a}$;
• $\sqrt[n]{a^n} = a$ if $n$ is odd, and $\sqrt[n]{a^n} = |a|$ if $n$ is even.

### b) Definition of Powers with Rational Exponents

Let $a > 0$ be a real number and $r = \frac{m}{n}$ be a rational number, where $m \in \mathbb{Z}, n \in \mathbb{N}^*$. The power of $a$ with exponent $r$ is defined by:
$$a^r = a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$$
Note: Powers with rational exponents of positive real numbers satisfy all properties of powers with integer exponents.

**Example 4:** Evaluate: a) $16^{\frac{3}{2}}$; b) $8^{-\frac{2}{3}}$.

Solution to Example 4:
• a) $16^{\frac{3}{2}} = \sqrt{16^3} = \sqrt{(4^2)^3} = \sqrt{(4^3)^2} = 4^3 = 64$.
• b) $8^{-\frac{2}{3}} = \sqrt[3]{8^{-2}} = \sqrt[3]{(2^3)^{-2}} = 2^{-2} = \frac{1}{4}$.

**Practice 4:** Simplify the expression: $A = \frac{x^{\frac{3}{2}}y + xy^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{y}} \quad (x, y > 0)$.

Solution to Practice 4:
• We have $x^{\frac{3}{2}} = x\sqrt{x}$ and $y^{\frac{3}{2}} = y\sqrt{y}$.
• Thus:
  $$A = \frac{xy\sqrt{x} + xy\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{y}} = \frac{xy(\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{y})}{\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{y}} = xy.$$

## Powers with Real Exponents

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_luy_thua_so_mu_thuc"/>

### a) Concept and Properties

Let $a$ be a positive real number and $\alpha$ be an irrational number. Consider a sequence of rational numbers $(r_n)$ such that $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} r_n = \alpha$. Then the sequence $(a^{r_n})$ has a finite limit that is independent of the choice of the sequence $(r_n)$. That limit is defined as the power of $a$ with exponent $\alpha$:
$$a^\alpha = \lim\_{n \to +\infty} a^{r_n}$$
Note: Powers with real exponents of positive real numbers satisfy all properties of powers with integer exponents.

### b) Examples and Practice on Real Exponents

**Example 5:** Simplify the expression: $A = \frac{a^{\sqrt{5} - 1} \cdot a^{3 - \sqrt{5}}}{(a^{\sqrt{3} - 1})^{\sqrt{3} + 1}} \quad (a > 0)$.

Solution to Example 5:
• Numerator: $a^{\sqrt{5} - 1} \cdot a^{3 - \sqrt{5}} = a^{\sqrt{5} - 1 + 3 - \sqrt{5}} = a^2$.
• Denominator: $(a^{\sqrt{3} - 1})^{\sqrt{3} + 1} = a^{(\sqrt{3} - 1)(\sqrt{3} + 1)} = a^{3 - 1} = a^2$.
• Therefore: $A = \frac{a^2}{a^2} = 1$.

**Example 6:** Without using a calculator, compare $8^{\sqrt{3}}$ and $4^{2\sqrt{3}}$.

Solution to Example 6:
• Express both numbers with base 2: $8^{\sqrt{3}} = (2^3)^{\sqrt{3}} = 2^{3\sqrt{3}}$ and $4^{2\sqrt{3}} = (2^2)^{2\sqrt{3}} = 2^{4\sqrt{3}}$.
• Since $3\sqrt{3} < 4\sqrt{3}$ and base $2 > 1$, we have $2^{3\sqrt{3}} < 2^{4\sqrt{3}}$.
• Thus, $8^{\sqrt{3}} < 4^{2\sqrt{3}}$.

**Practice 5:** Simplify the expression: $A = \frac{(a^{\sqrt{2} + 1})^{1 - \sqrt{2}}}{a^{\sqrt{5} - 1} \cdot a^{3 - \sqrt{5}}} \quad (a > 0)$.

Solution to Practice 5:
• Numerator: $(a^{\sqrt{2} + 1})^{1 - \sqrt{2}} = a^{(1 + \sqrt{2})(1 - \sqrt{2})} = a^{1 - 2} = a^{-1}$.
• Denominator: $a^{\sqrt{5} - 1} \cdot a^{3 - \sqrt{5}} = a^{\sqrt{5} - 1 + 3 - \sqrt{5}} = a^2$.
• Therefore: $A = \frac{a^{-1}}{a^2} = a^{-1 - 2} = a^{-3} = \frac{1}{a^3}$.

## Applications and Exercises

### a) Real-World Applications and Mathematical Modeling

**Application 1 (Compound Interest Formula):** If an initial principal $P$ is invested at an interest rate $r$ per compounding period, then after $N$ periods, the compound amount is given by $A = P(1 + r)^N$. Mr. Minh deposits 100 million VND in a 12-month term account at an annual interest rate of 6%. Assuming the interest rate remains constant, calculate the total amount accumulated after 3 years.

Solution to Application 1:
• Principal $P = 100$ million VND, interest rate $r = 6\% = 0{,}06$ per period (1 year), number of compounding periods $N = 3$.
• The total amount accumulated after 3 years is:
  $$A = 100 \cdot (1 + 0{,}06)^3 = 100 \cdot (1{,}06)^3 = 119{,}1016\text{ (million VND).}$$
• Conclusion: Mr. Minh receives approximately $119\\,101\\,600$ VND after 3 years.

**Application 2 (Population Growth):** In 2021, the population of an Asian country was approximately 19 million people, with an estimated doubling time of 30 years. The population $A$ (in millions) after $t$ years from 2021 is modeled by $A = 19 \cdot 2^{\frac{t}{30}}$. What will the population be after 20 years (rounded to the nearest million)?

Solution to Application 2:
• Substitute $t = 20$ into the formula:
  $$A = 19 \cdot 2^{\frac{20}{30}} = 19 \cdot 2^{\frac{2}{3}} = 19 \cdot \sqrt[3]{2^2} = 19 \cdot \sqrt[3]{4} \approx 19 \cdot 1{,}5874 \approx 30{,}16\text{ (million people).}$$
• Conclusion: After 20 years, the population will be approximately 30 million people.

### b) End-of-Lesson Exercises

**Exercise 6.1:** Evaluate:
a) $\left(\frac{1}{5}\right)^{-2}$;
b) $4^{\frac{3}{2}}$;
c) $\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)^{-\frac{2}{3}}$;
d) $\left(\frac{1}{16}\right)^{-0{,}75}$.

Solution to Exercise 6.1:
• a) $\left(\frac{1}{5}\right)^{-2} = 5^2 = 25$.
• b) $4^{\frac{3}{2}} = (2^2)^{\frac{3}{2}} = 2^3 = 8$.
• c) $\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)^{-\frac{2}{3}} = (8^{-1})^{-\frac{2}{3}} = 8^{\frac{2}{3}} = (2^3)^{\frac{2}{3}} = 2^2 = 4$.
• d) $\left(\frac{1}{16}\right)^{-0{,}75} = (16^{-1})^{-\frac{3}{4}} = 16^{\frac{3}{4}} = (2^4)^{\frac{3}{4}} = 2^3 = 8$.

**Exercise 6.2:** Perform the calculations:
a) $27^{\frac{2}{3}} + 81^{-0{,}75} - 25^{0{,}5}$;
b) $4^{2 - 3\sqrt{7}} \cdot 8^{2\sqrt{7}}$.

Solution to Exercise 6.2:
• a) $27^{\frac{2}{3}} = (3^3)^{\frac{2}{3}} = 3^2 = 9$; $81^{-0{,}75} = (3^4)^{-\frac{3}{4}} = 3^{-3} = \frac{1}{27}$; $25^{0{,}5} = (5^2)^{\frac{1}{2}} = 5$.
  Hence: $9 + \frac{1}{27} - 5 = 4 + \frac{1}{27} = \frac{109}{27}$.
• b) $4^{2 - 3\sqrt{7}} \cdot 8^{2\sqrt{7}} = (2^2)^{2 - 3\sqrt{7}} \cdot (2^3)^{2\sqrt{7}} = 2^{4 - 6\sqrt{7}} \cdot 2^{6\sqrt{7}} = 2^{4 - 6\sqrt{7} + 6\sqrt{7}} = 2^4 = 16$.

**Exercise 6.3:** Simplify the following expressions:
a) $A = \frac{x^5 y^{-2}}{x^3 y} \quad (x, y \ne 0)$;
b) $B = \frac{x^2 y^{-3}}{(x^{-1} y^4)^{-3}} \quad (x, y \ne 0)$.

Solution to Exercise 6.3:
• a) $A = x^{5-3} \cdot y^{-2-1} = x^2 y^{-3} = \frac{x^2}{y^3}$.
• b) Denominator: $(x^{-1} y^4)^{-3} = x^3 y^{-12}$. Thus $B = \frac{x^2 y^{-3}}{x^3 y^{-12}} = x^{2-3} y^{-3 - (-12)} = x^{-1} y^9 = \frac{y^9}{x}$.

**Exercise 6.4:** Given $x, y > 0$. Simplify the following expressions:
a) $A = \frac{x^3 \sqrt{y} + y^3 \sqrt{x}}{\sqrt[6]{x} + \sqrt[6]{y}}$ (written with fractional exponents);
b) $B = \left(\frac{x^{\sqrt{3}}}{y^{\sqrt{3} - 1}}\right)^{\sqrt{3} + 1} \cdot \frac{x^{-\sqrt{3} - 1}}{y^{-2}}$.

Solution to Exercise 6.4:
• a) Expressing as rational exponents:
  $$A = \frac{x^{\frac{1}{3}} y^{\frac{1}{2}} + y^{\frac{1}{3}} x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{x^{\frac{1}{6}} + y^{\frac{1}{6}}} = \frac{x^{\frac{1}{3}} y^{\frac{1}{3}} (x^{\frac{1}{6}} + y^{\frac{1}{6}})}{x^{\frac{1}{6}} + y^{\frac{1}{6}}} = x^{\frac{1}{3}} y^{\frac{1}{3}} = \sqrt[3]{xy}.$$
• b) Expanding the exponents:
  $$B = \frac{x^{\sqrt{3}(\sqrt{3} + 1)}}{y^{(\sqrt{3} - 1)(\sqrt{3} + 1)}} \cdot \frac{x^{-\sqrt{3} - 1}}{y^{-2}} = \frac{x^{3 + \sqrt{3}}}{y^{3 - 1}} \cdot \frac{x^{-\sqrt{3} - 1}}{y^{-2}} = \frac{x^{3 + \sqrt{3} - \sqrt{3} - 1}}{y^2 \cdot y^{-2}} = \frac{x^2}{y^0} = x^2.$$

**Exercise 6.5:** Prove that $\sqrt{4 + 2\sqrt{3}} - \sqrt{4 - 2\sqrt{3}} = 2$.

Solution to Exercise 6.5:
• We observe that $4 \pm 2\sqrt{3} = (\sqrt{3} \pm 1)^2$.
• Thus:
  $$\sqrt{4 + 2\sqrt{3}} - \sqrt{4 - 2\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{(\sqrt{3} + 1)^2} - \sqrt{(\sqrt{3} - 1)^2} = (\sqrt{3} + 1) - (\sqrt{3} - 1) = 2\text{ (Q.E.D.)}.$$

**Exercise 6.6:** Without using a calculator, compare:
a) $5^{6\sqrt{3}}$ and $5^{3\sqrt{6}}$;
b) $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-\frac{4}{3}}$ and $\sqrt{2} \cdot 2^{\frac{2}{3}}$.

Solution to Exercise 6.6:
• a) Comparing the exponents: $6\sqrt{3} = \sqrt{36 \cdot 3} = \sqrt{108}$ and $3\sqrt{6} = \sqrt{9 \cdot 6} = \sqrt{54}$. Since $108 > 54 \Rightarrow 6\sqrt{3} > 3\sqrt{6}$. Because base $5 > 1$, we have $5^{6\sqrt{3}} > 5^{3\sqrt{6}}$.
• b) $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-\frac{4}{3}} = (2^{-1})^{-\frac{4}{3}} = 2^{\frac{4}{3}}$.
  On the other hand: $\sqrt{2} \cdot 2^{\frac{2}{3}} = 2^{\frac{1}{2}} \cdot 2^{\frac{2}{3}} = 2^{\frac{1}{2} + \frac{2}{3}} = 2^{\frac{7}{6}}$.
  Comparing exponents: $\frac{4}{3} = \frac{8}{6} > \frac{7}{6}$; since base $2 > 1$, $2^{\frac{4}{3}} > 2^{\frac{7}{6}}$, meaning $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-\frac{4}{3}} > \sqrt{2} \cdot 2^{\frac{2}{3}}$.

**Exercise 6.7:** If an initial principal $P$ is deposited at an annual interest rate $r$ compounded $n$ times per year, then the accumulated amount after $N$ compounding periods is $A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^N$. Mr. An deposits 120 million VND for a 6-month term at an annual rate of 5%. Calculate the total amount accumulated after 2 years.

Solution to Exercise 6.7:
• Principal $P = 120$ million VND, annual rate $r = 0{,}05$. Compounded semi-annually (every 6 months), so $n = 2$ times per year.
• After 2 years, the number of periods is $N = 2 \times 2 = 4$ periods.
• The total amount accumulated is:
  $$A = 120 \cdot \left(1 + \frac{0{,}05}{2}\right)^4 = 120 \cdot (1{,}025)^4 \approx 120 \cdot 1{,}103813 \approx 132{,}458\text{ (million VND).}$$
• Conclusion: Mr. An receives approximately $132\\,458\\,000$ VND after 2 years.
