## Distance from a Point to a Line and to a Plane

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_khoang_cach_diem_duong_mat"/>

### a) Definition of Distance from a Point to a Line and to a Plane

• The distance from a point $M$ to a line $a$, denoted by $d(M, a)$, is the distance between $M$ and its projection $H$ on $a$.
• The distance from a point $M$ to a plane $(P)$, denoted by $d(M, (P))$, is the distance between $M$ and its projection $H$ on $(P)$.
Note:
\- $d(M, a) = 0 \iff M \in a$;
\- $d(M, (P)) = 0 \iff M \in (P)$;
\- The distance from $M$ to a line $a$ (or a plane $(P)$) is the shortest distance between $M$ and any point belonging to $a$ (belonging to $(P)$).
\- The distance from the apex to the plane containing the base of a pyramid is called the altitude (height) of that pyramid.

### b) Examples and Practice

**Example 1:** Given a regular pyramid $S.ABC$. Let the base edge and lateral edge be $a$ and $b$ respectively ($a < b\sqrt{3}$). Calculate the altitude of the pyramid.

Solution to Example 1:
• The projection of $S$ on the plane $(ABC)$ is the center $O$ of the equilateral triangle $ABC$.
• In the equilateral triangle $ABC$ of side $a$, the radius of the circumscribed circle is $OA = \frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}$.
• Considering the right-angled triangle $SOA$ at $O$, we have:
  $$SO = \sqrt{SA^2 - OA^2} = \sqrt{b^2 - \left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^2} = \sqrt{b^2 - \frac{a^2}{3}}$$
• Thus, the altitude of the pyramid is $\sqrt{b^2 - \frac{a^2}{3}}.$

**Practice 1:** Given a right prism $ABC.A'B'C'$ where $ABC$ is a right-angled isosceles triangle at $A$, $AB = a$, and $AA' = h$.
a) Calculate the distance from $A$ to the plane $(BCC'B')$.
b) What kind of triangle is $ABC'$? Calculate the distance from $A$ to $BC$.

Solution to Practice 1:
• a) Since it is a right prism, $AB \perp (BCC'B')$ at $B$. Therefore, the distance from $A$ to $(BCC'B')$ is equal to the length of segment $AB = a$.
• b) Triangle $ABC'$ is right-angled at $B$ (since $AB \perp (BCC'B') \implies AB \perp BC'$). The distance from $A$ to $BC$ is equal to the length of side $AB = a$ (since $AB \perp BC$ because triangle $ABC$ is right-angled isosceles at $A$).

## Distance Between a Line and a Parallel Plane, and Between Two Parallel Planes

### a) Definitions

• The distance between a line $a$ and a plane $(P)$ parallel to $a$, denoted by $d(a, (P))$, is the distance from any point on $a$ to the plane $(P)$.
• The distance between two parallel planes $(P)$ and $(Q)$, denoted by $d((P), (Q))$, is the distance from any point on one plane to the other plane.
• The distance between two parallel lines $m$ and $n$, denoted by $d(m, n)$, is the distance from any point on one line to the other line.
Note: The distance between the two bases of a prism is called the altitude (height) of that prism.

### b) Examples and Practice

**Example 2:** Given a right parallelepiped $ABCD.A'B'C'D'$ whose base is a rhombus with side length $a$, $\widehat{BAD} = 120^{\circ}$, and $AA' = h$. Calculate the distances between $A'C'$ and $(ABCD)$, and between $AA'$ and $(BDD'B')$.

Solution to Example 2:
• $A'C' \parallel (ABCD)$ and the parallelepiped is a right parallelepiped, so $AA' \perp (ABCD)$. Thus, $d(A'C', (ABCD)) = d(A', (ABCD)) = AA' = h$.
• $AA' \parallel BB' \parallel (BDD'B')$. Let $O$ be the center of the rhombus $ABCD$. We have $AO \perp BD$ and $AO \perp BB' \implies AO \perp (BDD'B')$.
  Therefore, the distance between $AA'$ and $(BDD'B')$ equals the length of segment $AO$.
  Isosceles triangle $BAD$ at $A$ has $\widehat{BAD} = 120^{\circ} \implies \widehat{ABO} = 30^{\circ}$.
  In the right-angled triangle $AOB$ at $O$, we have $AO = \frac{1}{2}AB = \frac{a}{2}$.
• Thus, the distance between $AA'$ and $(BDD'B')$ is $\frac{a}{2}$.

## Distance Between Two Skew Lines

### a) Common Perpendicular and Definition

• Common Perpendicular: A line $\Delta$ that intersects two skew lines $a$ and $b$ and is perpendicular to both of them is called the common perpendicular of $a$ and $b$.
• Distance Between Two Skew Lines: If the common perpendicular $\Delta$ intersects $a$ and $b$ at $M$ and $N$ respectively, the length of the segment $MN$ is called the distance between the two skew lines $a$ and $b$.
• Remarks:
\- The distance between two skew lines equals the distance from one of the lines to a plane parallel to it and containing the other line.
\- The distance between two skew lines equals the distance between two parallel planes containing the respective lines.

### b) Illustrative Example

**Example 3:** Given a pyramid $S.ABC$ with $SA \perp (ABC)$, $AB = a$, and $\widehat{ABC} = 60^{\circ}$. Identify the common perpendicular and calculate the distance between the two lines $SA$ and $BC$.

Solution to Example 3:
• Let $H$ be the projection of $A$ on $BC$. Right-angled triangle $ABH$ at $H$ has $AB = a$ and $\widehat{ABH} = 60^{\circ}$, so $BH = AB \cdot \cos 60^{\circ} = \frac{a}{2}$.
• Since $SA \perp (ABC)$, we have $SA \perp AH$ and $SA \perp BC$. Moreover, $AH \perp BC$; thus, $AH$ is the common perpendicular segment of $SA$ and $BC$.
• The distance between the two lines $SA$ and $BC$ is:
  $$d(SA, BC) = AH = AB \cdot \sin 60^{\circ} = a \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{a\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$
