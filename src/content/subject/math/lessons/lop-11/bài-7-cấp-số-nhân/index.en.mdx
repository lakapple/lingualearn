## Learning Objectives

### **Terminology:**

·       Geometric progression (geometric sequence)

·       Common ratio ($q$ or $r$)

·       General term ($n$-th term, $u_n$)

·       Sum of the first $n$ terms ($S_n$)

### **Knowledge and Skills:**

·       Identify whether a given numerical sequence is a geometric progression, and determine its first term $u_1$ and common ratio $q$.

·       Explain and proficiently apply the general term formula of a geometric progression: $u_n = u_1 \cdot q^{n-1}$ ($n \ge 2$).

·       Compute the sum of the first $n$ terms of a geometric progression: $S_n = \frac{u_1(1 - q^n)}{1 - q}$ ($q \ne 1$) or $S_n = n u_1$ ($q = 1$).

·       Apply geometric progressions to model and solve real-world problems (exponential population growth, drug elimination kinetics in bloodstream, cumulative asset depreciation, and compensation plan comparison).

## Definition of a Geometric Progression

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_dinh_nghia_cap_so_nhan"/>

### a) Concept and Recurrence Relation

Definition:
A geometric progression (or geometric sequence) is a sequence (finite or infinite) in which, beginning from the second term, each term is obtained by multiplying the immediately preceding term by a fixed non-zero constant $q$:
$$u_n = u\_{n-1} \cdot q \quad (n \ge 2)$$
• The constant $q$ is called the common ratio of the geometric progression.
• When $q = 1$, the geometric progression is a constant sequence: $a, a, a, \dots$
• When $q = 0$, the sequence takes the form: $u_1, 0, 0, \dots$
• To prove that a sequence $(u_n)$ with non-zero terms is a geometric progression, one proves that the ratio between consecutive terms $\frac{u_n}{u\_{n-1}} = q$ is constant for all $n \ge 2$.

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on the Definition

**Example 1:** Given a geometric progression with first term $u_1 = 5$ and common ratio $q = -2$. Write the first five terms of this progression.

Solution for Example 1:
• $u_1 = 5$.
• $u_2 = u_1 \cdot q = 5 \cdot (-2) = -10$.
• $u_3 = u_2 \cdot q = (-10) \cdot (-2) = 20$.
• $u_4 = u_3 \cdot q = 20 \cdot (-2) = -40$.
• $u_5 = u_4 \cdot q = (-40) \cdot (-2) = 80$.
Thus, the first five terms are: $5, -10, 20, -40, 80$.

**Example 2:** Given the sequence $(u_n)$ with $u_n = \frac{1}{3^{n-1}}$. Prove that this sequence is a geometric progression, and find $u_1$ and $q$.

Solution for Example 2:
• For all $n \ge 2$, consider the ratio: $\frac{u_n}{u\_{n-1}} = \frac{1/3^{n-1}}{1/3^{n-2}} = \frac{3^{n-2}}{3^{n-1}} = \frac{1}{3}$ (constant).
• Therefore, $(u_n)$ is a geometric progression with first term $u_1 = \frac{1}{3^0} = 1$ and common ratio $q = \frac{1}{3}$.

**Practice 1:** Given the sequence $(u_n)$ with $u_n = 2 \cdot 5^n$. Prove that this sequence is a geometric progression. Determine its first term and common ratio.

Solution for Practice 1:
• For all $n \ge 2$, the ratio is: $\frac{u_n}{u\_{n-1}} = \frac{2 \cdot 5^n}{2 \cdot 5^{n-1}} = 5$ (constant).
• Thus, $(u_n)$ is a geometric progression with first term $u_1 = 2 \cdot 5^1 = 10$ and common ratio $q = 5$.

## General Term of a Geometric Progression

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_so_hang_tong_quat_csn"/>

### a) General Term Formula

Theorem:
If a geometric progression has first term $u_1$ and common ratio $q$, then its general term $u_n$ is determined by the formula:
$$u_n = u_1 \cdot q^{n-1} \quad (n \ge 2)$$

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on the General Term

**Example 3:** Find the first five terms and the 100th term of the geometric progression: $8, -4, \dots$

Solution for Example 3:
• The geometric progression has first term $u_1 = 8$ and common ratio $q = \frac{-4}{8} = -\frac{1}{2}$.
• The first five terms are: $8, -4, 2, -1, \frac{1}{2}$.
• The 100th term is: $u\_{100} = u_1 \cdot q^{99} = 8 \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{99} = 2^3 \cdot \frac{-1}{2^{99}} = -\frac{1}{2^{96}}$.

**Example 4:** Given a geometric progression with positive terms. Knowing that the 10th term equals 1,536 and the 12th term equals 6,144, find the 20th term of this progression.

Solution for Example 4:
• Let $u_1 > 0$ be the first term and $q > 0$ be the common ratio. We have:
$$\begin{cases} u\_{10} = u_1 \cdot q^9 = 1\\,536 \\\ u\_{12} = u_1 \cdot q^{11} = 6\\,144 \end{cases} \Rightarrow \frac{u\_{12}}{u\_{10}} = q^2 = \frac{6\\,144}{1\\,536} = 4 \Rightarrow q = 2 \quad (\text{since } q > 0)$$
• From $u_1 \cdot 2^9 = 1\\,536 \Rightarrow u_1 = \frac{1\\,536}{512} = 3$.
• Thus, the 20th term is: $u\_{20} = u_1 \cdot q^{19} = 3 \cdot 2^{19} = 1\\,572\\,864$.

**Practice 2:** In a bacterial culture, there are initially 5,000 bacteria, and the bacterial count increases by 8% every hour. What will be the number of bacteria after 5 hours?

Solution for Practice 2:
• Each hour, the population is multiplied by the growth factor: $q = 1 + 8\\% = 1.08$.
• Initial population $N_0 = 5\\,000$.
• After 5 hours, the bacterial count is:
$$N_5 = N_0 \cdot (1.08)^5 = 5\\,000 \cdot (1.08)^5 \approx 7\\,347\text{ (bacteria).}$$

## Sum of the First n Terms of a Geometric Progression

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_tong_n_so_hang_csn"/>

### a) Formula for the Sum of the First n Terms

Theorem:
Let $(u_n)$ be a geometric progression with common ratio $q$. Let $S_n = u_1 + u_2 + \dots + u_n$. Then:
• If $q \ne 1$, the sum is given by:
$$S_n = \frac{u_1(1 - q^n)}{1 - q} = \frac{u_1(q^n - 1)}{q - 1}$$
• If $q = 1$, the progression is constant ($u_1, u_1, \dots, u_1$), hence:
$$S_n = n u_1$$

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Summation

**Example 5 (Introductory IT Specialist Salary Problem):** A company hires an IT specialist with an initial first-year salary of 240 million VND, committing to an annual salary increase of 5% compared to the immediately preceding year. Calculate the total cumulative salary the specialist receives after working 10 years at the company (rounded to the nearest million VND).

Solution for Example 5:
• The specialist's annual salary sequence forms a geometric progression with $u_1 = 240$ million VND and common ratio $q = 1 + 0.05 = 1.05$.
• The total cumulative salary after 10 years is:
$$S\_{10} = \frac{u_1(1 - q^{10})}{1 - q} = \frac{240[1 - (1.05)^{10}]}{1 - 1.05} = \frac{240[(1.05)^{10} - 1]}{0.05} \approx 3\\,019\text{ (million VND)} = 3.019\text{ billion VND.}$$

**Example 6:** How many terms of the geometric progression $2, 6, 18, \dots$ must be summed to yield a total of 728?

Solution for Example 6:
• The progression has $u_1 = 2$ and common ratio $q = \frac{6}{2} = 3$.
• Let $n$ be the required number of terms. We have:
$$S_n = \frac{u_1(1 - q^n)}{1 - q} = \frac{2(1 - 3^n)}{1 - 3} = 3^n - 1 = 728$$
$$\Leftrightarrow 3^n = 729 = 3^6 \Rightarrow n = 6$$
• Hence, exactly 6 terms must be summed.

## Applications and Exercises

### a) Real-World Applications and Strategy Comparison

**Application (Three-Year Salary Option Comparison):** A factory hires workers for a 3-year term (12 quarters) and offers two monthly compensation options:
• Option 1: Starting monthly salary of 5 million VND, with a quarterly raise of 500 thousand VND (0.5 million VND).
• Option 2: Starting monthly salary of 5 million VND, with a quarterly raise of 5%.
Which option yields a higher total cumulative salary after three years of employment?

Solution for Application:
• Three years consist of 12 quarters, with 3 months per quarter.
• Under Option 1 (Arithmetic Progression):
  Monthly salary in quarter $n$ is: $u_n = 5 + (n - 1) \cdot 0.5$ ($1 \le n \le 12$).
  The sum of monthly salary rates over 12 quarters is:
  $$S_1 = \frac{12}{2}[2(5) + (12 - 1) \cdot 0.5] = 6[10 + 5.5] = 6(15.5) = 93\text{ (million VND)}.$$
  Total earnings over 3 years (36 months) are: $3 \times 93 = 279$ million VND.
• Under Option 2 (Geometric Progression):
  Monthly salary in quarter $n$ forms a geometric progression with $v_1 = 5$ and $q = 1 + 0.05 = 1.05$.
  The sum of monthly salary rates over 12 quarters is:
  $$S_2 = \frac{v_1(1 - q^{12})}{1 - q} = \frac{5[1 - (1.05)^{12}]}{1 - 1.05} = \frac{5[(1.05)^{12} - 1]}{0.05} = 100[(1.05)^{12} - 1] \approx 100(0.795856) = 79.5856\text{ (million VND)}.$$
  Total earnings over 3 years are: $3 \times 79.5856 \approx 238.76$ million VND.
• Conclusion: Since $279 > 238.76$, Option 1 provides a significantly higher total income after 3 years.

**Exercise 2.19 (Bulldozer Depreciation):** A construction firm purchases a bulldozer for 3 billion VND. Each year, its market value depreciates by 20% relative to the immediately preceding year. Find the residual value of the bulldozer after 5 years of operation.

Solution for Exercise 2.19:
• Each year, the remaining value is: $100\\% - 20\\% = 80\\% = 0.8$ of its previous year value.
• Initial value: $V_0 = 3$ billion VND $= 3\\,000$ million VND.
• After 5 years, the residual value is:
$$V_5 = V_0 \cdot (0.8)^5 = 3\\,000 \cdot (0.8)^5 = 3\\,000 \cdot 0.32768 = 983.04\text{ (million VND)} \approx 0.983\text{ billion VND.}$$

**Exercise 2.20 (Geometric Population Growth):** In 2020, the population of a nation was approximately 97 million people, growing at a steady annual rate of 0.91%. Assuming this annual growth rate remains constant, estimate the nation's population in the year 2030.

Solution for Exercise 2.20:
• The elapsed time from 2020 to 2030 is $n = 10$ years.
• The annual growth multiplier is: $q = 1 + 0.91\\% = 1.0091$.
• The estimated population in 2030 is:
$$P\_{2030} = 97 \cdot (1.0091)^{10} \approx 97 \cdot 1.09482 \approx 106.2\text{ (million people).}$$

**Exercise 2.21 (Drug Elimination Kinetics):** A medication is administered once daily. The quantity of drug remaining in the bloodstream on the first day is 50 mg, and on each subsequent day, the remaining amount decreases by half relative to the day before. Calculate the total cumulative quantity of drug (in mg) present across 10 consecutive days.

Solution for Exercise 2.21:
• The daily drug amounts form a geometric progression with $u_1 = 50\text{ mg}$ and common ratio $q = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5$.
• The total cumulative quantity over 10 days is:
$$S\_{10} = \frac{u_1(1 - q^{10})}{1 - q} = \frac{50\left[1 - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{10}\right]}{1 - \frac{1}{2}} = 100\left(1 - \frac{1}{1\\,024}\right) = \frac{100 \times 1\\,023}{1\\,024} \approx 99.9\text{ (mg).}$$

### b) Detailed Section Exercises

**Exercise 2.15:** Determine the common ratio, the 5th term, the general term, and the 100th term of each geometric progression:
a) $1, 4, 16, \dots$;
b) $2, -\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{8}, \dots$

Solution for Exercise 2.15:
• a) $u_1 = 1, q = \frac{4}{1} = 4$.
  + General term: $u_n = 1 \cdot 4^{n-1} = 4^{n-1}$.
  + 5th term: $u_5 = 4^{5-1} = 4^4 = 256$.
  + 100th term: $u\_{100} = 4^{99}$.
• b) $u_1 = 2, q = \frac{-1/2}{2} = -\frac{1}{4}$.
  + General term: $u_n = 2 \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right)^{n-1}$.
  + 5th term: $u_5 = 2 \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right)^4 = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{256} = \frac{1}{128}$.
  + 100th term: $u\_{100} = 2 \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right)^{99} = -\frac{2}{4^{99}} = -\frac{1}{2^{197}}$.

**Exercise 2.16:** Write the first five terms of each sequence $(u_n)$ and determine whether it is a geometric progression. If it is a geometric progression, find $q$ and express the general term as $u_n = u_1 \cdot q^{n-1}$:
a) $u_n = 5n$;
b) $u_n = 5^n$;
c) $u_1 = 1, u_n = n \cdot u\_{n-1}$ ($n \ge 2$);
d) $u_1 = 1, u_n = 5u\_{n-1}$ ($n \ge 2$).

Solution for Exercise 2.16:
• a) First five terms: $5, 10, 15, 20, 25$. Ratio $\frac{10}{5} = 2 \ne \frac{15}{10} = 1.5$, so this is not a geometric progression.
• b) First five terms: $5, 25, 125, 625, 3\\,125$. The ratio $\frac{u_n}{u\_{n-1}} = 5$ is constant, so this is a geometric progression with $u_1 = 5, q = 5$. Formula: $u_n = 5 \cdot 5^{n-1}$.
• c) First five terms: $1, 2, 6, 24, 120$. The ratio $\frac{u_n}{u\_{n-1}} = n$ varies, so this is not a geometric progression.
• d) First five terms: $1, 5, 25, 125, 625$. By recurrence definition $\frac{u_n}{u\_{n-1}} = 5$ is constant, so this is a geometric progression with $u_1 = 1, q = 5$. Formula: $u_n = 1 \cdot 5^{n-1} = 5^{n-1}$.

**Exercise 2.17:** A geometric progression has a 6th term equal to 96 and a 3rd term equal to 12. Find the 50th term of this progression.

Solution for Exercise 2.17:
• We have: $u_6 = u_1 \cdot q^5 = 96$ and $u_3 = u_1 \cdot q^2 = 12$.
• Taking the ratio: $\frac{u_6}{u_3} = q^3 = \frac{96}{12} = 8 \Rightarrow q = 2$.
• Substituting into $u_3$: $u_1 \cdot 2^2 = 12 \Rightarrow u_1 = 3$.
• The 50th term is: $u\_{50} = u_1 \cdot q^{49} = 3 \cdot 2^{49}$.

**Exercise 2.18:** A geometric progression has a first term equal to 5 and a common ratio equal to 2. How many terms must be summed to obtain a total of 5,115?

Solution for Exercise 2.18:
• Given $u_1 = 5$ and $q = 2$. The sum of the first $n$ terms is:
$$S_n = \frac{u_1(q^n - 1)}{q - 1} = \frac{5(2^n - 1)}{2 - 1} = 5(2^n - 1) = 5\\,115$$
$$\Leftrightarrow 2^n - 1 = \frac{5\\,115}{5} = 1\\,023 \Leftrightarrow 2^n = 1\\,024 = 2^{10} \Rightarrow n = 10$$
• Thus, exactly 10 terms must be summed.
