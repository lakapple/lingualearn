## Learning Objectives

**Terminology:**

·       Mean (arithmetic average)

·       Median

·       Quartiles ($Q_1, Q_2, Q_3$)

·       Mode

·       Class midpoint (class mark), cumulative frequency

**Knowledge and Skills:**

·       Compute measures of central tendency for grouped data proficiently: mean, median, quartiles, and mode.

·       Understand the statistical meaning and role of each measure to appropriately describe the distribution of empirical data.

·       Adjust discrete integer intervals of the form $k_1 - k_2$ to continuous class boundaries $[k_1 - 0.5; k_2 + 0.5)$ prior to calculations.

·       Apply central tendency measures to solve practical statistical problems (fuel expenditure, body weights, study duration, sleep duration, exam scores, battery lifespan).

## Mean of Grouped Data

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_so_trung_binh_ghep_nhom"/>

### a) Mean Computation Formula

Computation Formula:
Let a grouped dataset consist of $k$ class intervals $[a_i; a\_{i+1})$ with corresponding frequencies $m_i$ ($i = 1, 2, \dots, k$).
• The class midpoint (representative value) of the interval $[a_i; a\_{i+1})$ is the average of its boundaries:
$$x_i = \frac{a_i + a\_{i+1}}{2}$$
• The sample mean of the grouped data, denoted by $\overline{x}$, is given by:
$$\overline{x} = \frac{m_1 x_1 + m_2 x_2 + \dots + m_k x_k}{n}$$
where $n = m_1 + m_2 + \dots + m_k$ is the total sample size.
Note: For discrete integer data given as classes $k_1 - k_2$ ($k_1, k_2 \in \mathbb{N}$), adjust them to continuous intervals $[k_1 - 0.5; k_2 + 0.5)$ before determining midpoints and evaluating statistics.

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on the Mean

**Example 1:** Find the mean weight of 42 students in class 11D given in the table:
• $[40.5; 45.5)$: 10 students;
• $[45.5; 50.5)$: 7 students;
• $[50.5; 55.5)$: 16 students;
• $[55.5; 60.5)$: 4 students;
• $[60.5; 65.5)$: 2 students;
• $[65.5; 70.5)$: 3 students.

Solution for Example 1:
• Determine the class midpoints $x_i$ for each interval:
  $x_1 = 43; x_2 = 48; x_3 = 53; x_4 = 58; x_5 = 63; x_6 = 68$.
• Total number of students is $n = 42$.
• The mean weight is:
$$\overline{x} = \frac{10 \cdot 43 + 7 \cdot 48 + 16 \cdot 53 + 4 \cdot 58 + 2 \cdot 63 + 3 \cdot 68}{42} = \frac{2\\,176}{42} \approx 51.81\text{ (kg).}$$

**Practice 1:** A survey on television viewing time during the previous week (in hours) among students yielded:
• $[0; 5)$: 8 students; $[5; 10)$: 16 students; $[10; 15)$: 4 students; $[15; 20)$: 2 students; $[20; 25)$: 2 students.
Calculate the average weekly television viewing time for these students.

Solution for Practice 1:
• Sample size: $n = 8 + 16 + 4 + 2 + 2 = 32$ students.
• Class midpoints: $2.5; 7.5; 12.5; 17.5; 22.5$.
• The mean viewing time is:
$$\overline{x} = \frac{8(2.5) + 16(7.5) + 4(12.5) + 2(17.5) + 2(22.5)}{32} = \frac{20 + 120 + 50 + 35 + 45}{32} = \frac{270}{32} = 8.4375\text{ (hours).}$$

## Median of Grouped Data

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_trung_vi_ghep_nhom"/>

### a) Median Computation Formula

Procedure for Determining the Median:
• Step 1: Identify the median class. Suppose it is the $p$-th class: $[a_p; a\_{p+1})$. This is the first class interval whose cumulative frequency is greater than or equal to $\frac{n}{2}$.
• Step 2: Apply the linear interpolation formula for the median $M_e$:
$$M_e = a_p + \frac{\frac{n}{2} - (m_1 + m_2 + \dots + m\_{p-1})}{m_p} \cdot (a\_{p+1} - a_p)$$
where $n$ is the sample size, $m_p$ is the frequency of the median class, and $a_p, a\_{p+1}$ are the class boundaries (by convention, $m_1 + \dots + m\_{p-1} = 0$ when $p = 1$).

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on the Median

**Example 2:** Evening Internet access time (in minutes) of students is recorded as follows:
• $[9.5; 12.5)$: 3 students; $[12.5; 15.5)$: 12 students;
• $[15.5; 18.5)$: 15 students; $[18.5; 21.5)$: 24 students; $[21.5; 24.5)$: 2 students.
Compute the median of this grouped dataset.

Solution for Example 2:
• Sample size: $n = 3 + 12 + 15 + 24 + 2 = 56 \Rightarrow \frac{n}{2} = 28$.
• Cumulative frequencies: $cf_1 = 3; cf_2 = 15; cf_3 = 30 > 28$. Hence, the median class is the 3rd class: $[15.5; 18.5)$.
• Parameters: $p = 3, a_3 = 15.5, m_3 = 15, cf_2 = 15, h = 18.5 - 15.5 = 3$.
• The median is:
$$M_e = 15.5 + \frac{28 - 15}{15} \cdot 3 = 15.5 + \frac{13}{15} \cdot 3 = 15.5 + 2.6 = 18.1\text{ (minutes).}$$

**Practice 2:** Serve speeds (km/h) recorded across 200 serves by a tennis player:
• $[150; 155)$: 18 serves; $[155; 160)$: 28 serves; $[160; 165)$: 35 serves;
• $[165; 170)$: 43 serves; $[170; 175)$: 41 serves; $[175; 180)$: 35 serves.
Find the median serve speed.

Solution for Practice 2:
• Sample size $n = 200 \Rightarrow \frac{n}{2} = 100$.
• Cumulative frequencies: $cf_1 = 18; cf_2 = 46; cf_3 = 81; cf_4 = 124 > 100$. The median class is the 4th class: $[165; 170)$.
• Applying the formula with $a_4 = 165, m_4 = 43, cf_3 = 81, h = 5$:
$$M_e = 165 + \frac{100 - 81}{43} \cdot 5 = 165 + \frac{19 \cdot 5}{43} = 165 + \frac{95}{43} \approx 167.21\text{ (km/h).}$$

## Quartiles of Grouped Data

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_tu_phan_vi_ghep_nhom"/>

### a) Formulas for Quartiles Q1, Q2, Q3

The second quartile coincides with the median: $Q_2 = M_e$.
First Quartile $Q_1$:
• The class containing $Q_1$ is the $p$-th class: $[a_p; a\_{p+1})$, which is the first class whose cumulative frequency is at least $\frac{n}{4}$.
$$Q_1 = a_p + \frac{\frac{n}{4} - (m_1 + m_2 + \dots + m\_{p-1})}{m_p} \cdot (a\_{p+1} - a_p)$$
Third Quartile $Q_3$:
• The class containing $Q_3$ is the $q$-th class: $[a_q; a\_{q+1})$, which is the first class whose cumulative frequency is at least $\frac{3n}{4}$.
$$Q_3 = a_q + \frac{\frac{3n}{4} - (m_1 + m_2 + \dots + m\_{q-1})}{m_q} \cdot (a\_{q+1} - a_q)$$

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Quartiles

**Example 3:** Find the first quartile $Q_1$ and third quartile $Q_3$ for the Internet usage dataset in Example 2 ($n = 56$).

Solution for Example 3:
• Calculating $Q_1$:
  We have $\frac{n}{4} = \frac{56}{4} = 14$. Cumulative frequencies: $cf_1 = 3, cf_2 = 15 > 14$. The class containing $Q_1$ is $[12.5; 15.5)$.
  $$Q_1 = 12.5 + \frac{14 - 3}{12} \cdot 3 = 12.5 + \frac{11}{4} = 15.25\text{ (minutes).}$$
• Calculating $Q_3$:
  We have $\frac{3n}{4} = \frac{3 \cdot 56}{4} = 42$. Cumulative frequencies: $cf_3 = 30, cf_4 = 54 > 42$. The class containing $Q_3$ is $[18.5; 21.5)$.
  $$Q_3 = 18.5 + \frac{42 - 30}{24} \cdot 3 = 18.5 + \frac{12}{24} \cdot 3 = 18.5 + 1.5 = 20\text{ (minutes).}$$

**Practice 3:** Find the first and third quartiles for the tennis serve speed dataset in Practice 2 ($n = 200$).

Solution for Practice 3:
• Cumulative frequencies: $cf_1 = 18; cf_2 = 46; cf_3 = 81; cf_4 = 124; cf_5 = 165; cf_6 = 200$.
• Finding $Q_1$: $\frac{n}{4} = 50$. Since $cf_2 = 46 < 50 < cf_3 = 81$, the class containing $Q_1$ is $[160; 165)$.
  $$Q_1 = 160 + \frac{50 - 46}{35} \cdot 5 = 160 + \frac{4 \cdot 5}{35} = 160 + \frac{4}{7} \approx 160.57\text{ (km/h).}$$
• Finding $Q_3$: $\frac{3n}{4} = 150$. Since $cf_4 = 124 < 150 < cf_5 = 165$, the class containing $Q_3$ is $[170; 175)$.
  $$Q_3 = 170 + \frac{150 - 124}{41} \cdot 5 = 170 + \frac{26 \cdot 5}{41} = 170 + \frac{130}{41} \approx 173.17\text{ (km/h).}$$

## Mode of Grouped Data

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_mot_ghep_nhom"/>

### a) Mode Determination Formula

Procedure for Determining the Mode $M_o$:
• Step 1: Identify the modal class (the class interval with the largest frequency), denoted by $[a_j; a\_{j+1})$.
• Step 2: Apply the formula:
$$M_o = a_j + \frac{m_j - m\_{j-1}}{(m_j - m\_{j-1}) + (m_j - m\_{j+1})} \cdot h$$
where $m_j$ is the frequency of the modal class (convention: $m_0 = m\_{k+1} = 0$), $m\_{j-1}$ and $m\_{j+1}$ are frequencies of adjacent preceding and succeeding classes, and $h = a\_{j+1} - a_j$ is the class width.
Note: The mode is defined strictly for grouped data with equal class widths. If all classes have identical frequencies, the distribution has no mode.

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on the Mode

**Example 4:** Heights (in cm) of 50 students in class 11A:
• $[145; 150)$: 7 students; $[150; 155)$: 14 students; $[155; 160)$: 10 students;
• $[160; 165)$: 10 students; $[165; 170)$: 9 students.
Compute the mode of this grouped dataset.

Solution for Example 4:
• The maximum frequency is $14$, so the modal class is $[150; 155)$.
• Parameters: $j = 2, a_2 = 150, m_2 = 14, m_1 = 7, m_3 = 10, h = 5$.
• The mode is:
$$M_o = 150 + \frac{14 - 7}{(14 - 7) + (14 - 10)} \cdot 5 = 150 + \frac{7}{7 + 4} \cdot 5 = 150 + \frac{35}{11} \approx 153.18\text{ (cm).}$$
Interpretation: The height value around $153.18\text{ cm}$ occurs with the greatest relative frequency.

**Practice 4:** Time (in minutes) required for students to complete an exam question:
• $[0.5; 10.5)$: 2 students; $[10.5; 20.5)$: 10 students; $[20.5; 30.5)$: 6 students;
• $[30.5; 40.5)$: 4 students; $[40.5; 50.5)$: 3 students.
Find the mode of this grouped dataset.

Solution for Practice 4:
• Maximum frequency is 10, so the modal class is $[10.5; 20.5)$.
• Parameters: $a_2 = 10.5, m_2 = 10, m_1 = 2, m_3 = 6, h = 10$.
• The mode is:
$$M_o = 10.5 + \frac{10 - 2}{(10 - 2) + (10 - 6)} \cdot 10 = 10.5 + \frac{8}{8 + 4} \cdot 10 = 10.5 + \frac{80}{12} = 10.5 + 6.67 \approx 17.17\text{ (minutes).}$$

## Applications and Exercises

### a) Comprehensive Real-World Applications

**Application (Fuel Purchase Expenditure Estimation):** Gasoline sales recorded for 35 motorcyclists (Table 3.1):
• $[0; 30)$: 3 customers; $[30; 60)$: 15 customers; $[60; 90)$: 10 customers; $[90; 120)$: 7 customers.
Calculate the measures of central tendency (mean, median, quartiles, mode) and interpret their significance.

Solution for Application:
• 1. Sample Mean:
  Midpoints: $x_1 = 15; x_2 = 45; x_3 = 75; x_4 = 105$.
  $$\overline{x} = \frac{3(15) + 15(45) + 10(75) + 7(105)}{35} = \frac{45 + 675 + 750 + 735}{35} = \frac{2\\,205}{35} = 63\text{ (thousand VND).}$$
• 2. Median $M_e$ ($n = 35 \Rightarrow \frac{n}{2} = 17.5$):
  Cumulative frequencies: $cf_1 = 3; cf_2 = 18 > 17.5$. Median class is $[30; 60)$.
  $$M_e = 30 + \frac{17.5 - 3}{15} \cdot 30 = 30 + \frac{14.5}{15} \cdot 30 = 30 + 29 = 59\text{ (thousand VND).}$$
• 3. Quartiles $Q_1, Q_3$:
  + $\frac{n}{4} = 8.75$. Class containing $Q_1$ is $[30; 60)$ ($cf_1 = 3 < 8.75 < cf_2 = 18$):
    $$Q_1 = 30 + \frac{8.75 - 3}{15} \cdot 30 = 30 + 2(5.75) = 41.5\text{ (thousand VND).}$$
  + $\frac{3n}{4} = 26.25$. Class containing $Q_3$ is $[60; 90)$ ($cf_2 = 18 < 26.25 < cf_3 = 28$):
    $$Q_3 = 60 + \frac{26.25 - 18}{10} \cdot 30 = 60 + 3(8.25) = 84.75\text{ (thousand VND).}$$
• 4. Mode $M_o$:
  Modal class is $[30; 60)$ with $m_1 = 3, m_2 = 15, m_3 = 10, h = 30$.
  $$M_o = 30 + \frac{15 - 3}{(15 - 3) + (15 - 10)} \cdot 30 = 30 + \frac{12}{12 + 5} \cdot 30 = 30 + \frac{360}{17} \approx 51.18\text{ (thousand VND).}$$

### b) Detailed Section Exercises

**Exercise 3.4:** Commuting distances (in km) from home to workplace for 40 factory workers:
5, 3, 10, 20, 25, 11, 13, 7, 12, 31, 19, 10, 12, 17, 18, 11, 32, 17, 16, 2, 7, 9, 7, 8, 3, 5, 12, 15, 18, 3, 12, 14, 2, 9, 6, 15, 15, 7, 6, 12.
a) Group the dataset into equal-width classes of width 5, starting with $[0; 5)$. Identify the midpoint of each class.
b) Compute the mean for the ungrouped data and the grouped data. Which value is more accurate?
c) Determine the modal class of the grouped data.

Solution for Exercise 3.4:
• a) Grouped frequency distribution across 7 intervals of width 5:
  + $[0; 5)$: frequency 5, midpoint $2.5$;
  + $[5; 10)$: frequency 9, midpoint $7.5$;
  + $[10; 15)$: frequency 11, midpoint $12.5$;
  + $[15; 20)$: frequency 9, midpoint $17.5$;
  + $[20; 25)$: frequency 2, midpoint $22.5$;
  + $[25; 30)$: frequency 1, midpoint $27.5$;
  + $[30; 35)$: frequency 3, midpoint $32.5$.
• b) Computing the mean:
  + Ungrouped raw mean: $\overline{x}\_{\text{raw}} = \frac{\sum x_i}{40} = \frac{498}{40} = 12.45\text{ km}$.
  + Grouped mean: $\overline{x}\_{\text{grouped}} = \frac{5(2.5) + 9(7.5) + 11(12.5) + 9(17.5) + 2(22.5) + 1(27.5) + 3(32.5)}{40} = \frac{515}{40} = 12.875\text{ km}$.
  The raw mean $\overline{x}\_{\text{raw}} = 12.45\text{ km}$ is more accurate because it computes directly from exact individual data points.
• c) The highest frequency is 11, so the modal class is $[10; 15)$.

**Exercise 3.5:** Operational lifespan (in years) of 50 automotive batteries:
• $[2; 2.5)$: 4; $[2.5; 3)$: 9; $[3; 3.5)$: 14; $[3.5; 4)$: 7; $[4; 4.5)$: 11; $[4.5; 5)$: 5.
a) Determine the mode and interpret its meaning.
b) Calculate the mean lifespan of these 50 automotive batteries.

Solution for Exercise 3.5:
• a) The highest frequency is 14 in class $[3; 3.5)$. With $a_3 = 3, m_3 = 14, m_2 = 9, m_4 = 7, h = 0.5$:
  $$M_o = 3 + \frac{14 - 9}{(14 - 9) + (14 - 7)} \cdot 0.5 = 3 + \frac{5}{5 + 7} \cdot 0.5 = 3 + \frac{2.5}{12} \approx 3.21\text{ (years).}$$
  Interpretation: The battery lifespan occurring with highest density is approximately $3.21$ years.
• b) Midpoints: $2.25; 2.75; 3.25; 3.75; 4.25; 4.75$.
  $$\overline{x} = \frac{4(2.25) + 9(2.75) + 14(3.25) + 7(3.75) + 11(4.25) + 5(4.75)}{50} = \frac{9 + 24.75 + 45.5 + 26.25 + 46.75 + 23.75}{50} = \frac{176}{50} = 3.52\text{ (years).}$$

**Exercise 3.6:** Mathematics exam scores (on a 100-point scale rounded to integers) of 60 candidates: 0-9 (1); 10-19 (2); 20-29 (4); 30-39 (6); 40-49 (15); 50-59 (12); 60-69 (10); 70-79 (6); 80-89 (3); 90-99 (1).
a) Adjust the discrete classes into continuous standard intervals $[a_i; a\_{i+1})$.
b) Find the quartiles and interpret their statistical meaning.

Solution for Exercise 3.6:
• a) Adjusted continuous intervals: $[-0.5; 9.5)$ (1); $[9.5; 19.5)$ (2); $[19.5; 29.5)$ (4); $[29.5; 39.5)$ (6); $[39.5; 49.5)$ (15); $[49.5; 59.5)$ (12); $[59.5; 69.5)$ (10); $[69.5; 79.5)$ (6); $[79.5; 89.5)$ (3); $[89.5; 99.5)$ (1).
• b) Sample size $n = 60$.
  + Calculating $Q_1$ ($\frac{n}{4} = 15$): Cumulative frequencies: $cf_3 = 7, cf_4 = 13, cf_5 = 28 > 15$. Class containing $Q_1$ is $[39.5; 49.5)$:
    $$Q_1 = 39.5 + \frac{15 - 13}{15} \cdot 10 = 39.5 + \frac{20}{15} \approx 40.83.$$
  + Calculating $Q_2 = M_e$ ($\frac{n}{2} = 30$): Cumulative frequencies $cf_5 = 28 < 30 < cf_6 = 40$. Class containing $Q_2$ is $[49.5; 59.5)$:
    $$Q_2 = 49.5 + \frac{30 - 28}{12} \cdot 10 = 49.5 + \frac{20}{12} \approx 51.17.$$
  + Calculating $Q_3$ ($\frac{3n}{4} = 45$): Cumulative frequencies $cf_6 = 40 < 45 < cf_7 = 50$. Class containing $Q_3$ is $[59.5; 69.5)$:
    $$Q_3 = 59.5 + \frac{45 - 40}{10} \cdot 10 = 59.5 + 5 = 64.5.$$
  Interpretation: Approximately 25% of candidates scored below $40.83$; 50% scored below $51.17$; and 75% scored below $64.5$.

**Exercise 3.7:** Nightly sleep duration (in hours) among male students (45 boys) and female students (41 girls):
• $[4; 5)$: 6 boys, 4 girls; $[5; 6)$: 10 boys, 8 girls; $[6; 7)$: 13 boys, 10 girls; $[7; 8)$: 9 boys, 11 girls; $[8; 9)$: 7 boys, 8 girls.
a) Compare the average sleep duration between boys and girls.
b) Determine the minimum number of hours that 75% of Grade 11 students sleep each night.

Solution for Exercise 3.7:
• a) Midpoints: $4.5; 5.5; 6.5; 7.5; 8.5$.
  + Boys ($n_1 = 45$): $\overline{x}\_1 = \frac{6(4.5) + 10(5.5) + 13(6.5) + 9(7.5) + 7(8.5)}{45} = \frac{27 + 55 + 84.5 + 67.5 + 59.5}{45} = \frac{293.5}{45} \approx 6.52\text{ hours}$.
  + Girls ($n_2 = 41$): $\overline{x}\_2 = \frac{4(4.5) + 8(5.5) + 10(6.5) + 11(7.5) + 8(8.5)}{41} = \frac{18 + 44 + 65 + 82.5 + 68}{41} = \frac{277.5}{41} \approx 6.77\text{ hours}$.
  The average sleep duration of female students ($6.77$ hours) is slightly higher than that of male students ($6.52$ hours).
• b) Combining all Grade 11 students ($n = 45 + 41 = 86$ students):
  Aggregated table: $[4; 5)$ has 10; $[5; 6)$ has 18; $[6; 7)$ has 23; $[7; 8)$ has 20; $[8; 9)$ has 15.
  The threshold where 75% of students sleep at least that amount corresponds to the 1st quartile $Q_1$ (since 25% sleep less than $Q_1$ and 75% sleep $Q_1$ or more).
  With $n = 86 \Rightarrow \frac{n}{4} = 21.5$. Cumulative frequencies: $cf_1 = 10, cf_2 = 28 > 21.5$. Class containing $Q_1$ is $[5; 6)$:
  $$Q_1 = 5 + \frac{21.5 - 10}{18} \cdot 1 = 5 + \frac{11.5}{18} \approx 5.64\text{ (hours)} \approx 5\text{ hours } 38\text{ minutes}.$$
  Conclusion: 75% of Grade 11 students sleep at least approximately $5.64$ hours (about 5 hours 38 minutes) each night.
