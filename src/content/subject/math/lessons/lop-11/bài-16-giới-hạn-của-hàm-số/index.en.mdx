# Lesson Objectives

**Terminology:**
- Limit of a function ($\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = L$)
- One-sided limit ($\lim_{x \to x_0^+} f(x)$, $\lim_{x \to x_0^-} f(x)$)
- Limit at infinity ($\lim_{x \to +\infty} f(x)$, $\lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x)$)
- Infinite limit ($\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = +\infty$ or $-\infty$)

**Knowledge and Skills:**
- Recognize and clearly understand the concept of the finite limit of a function at a point and at infinity.
- Recognize and clearly understand the concepts of one-sided limits and infinite limits of a function.
- Master the rules for evaluating finite and infinite limits of functions.
- Solve real-world problems involving limits of functions (such as Einstein's Theory of Relativity).

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_gioi_han_cua_ham_so" />

In Einstein's Theory of Relativity, the mass of an object moving at a velocity $v$ is given by the formula:

$$
m = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}
$$

where $m_0$ is the rest mass of the object, and $c$ is the speed of light. What happens to the mass of the object as its velocity approaches the speed of light?

## 1. Finite Limits of Functions at a Point

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_gioi_han_huu_han_tai_mot_diem" />

### a) Introductory Activity and Definition

**Activity 1. Recognizing the concept of a limit at a point:**
Given the function $f(x) = \frac{4 - x^2}{x - 2}$.
- a) Find the domain of the function $f(x)$.
- b) Let $x_n = 2 + \frac{1}{n}$. Simplify $f(x_n)$ and find the limit of the sequence $(u_n)$ where $u_n = f(x_n)$.
- c) For any sequence $(x_n)$ such that $x_n \neq 2$ and $x_n \to 2$, evaluate $f(x_n)$ and find $\lim_{n \to +\infty} f(x_n)$.

**Definition of a Finite Limit at a Point:**
- Suppose $(a; b)$ is an open interval containing $x_0$ and the function $y = f(x)$ is defined on $(a; b) \setminus \{x_0\}$. We say that the function $f(x)$ has a limit equal to the number $L$ as $x$ approaches $x_0$ if for any sequence $(x_n)$ satisfying $x_n \in (a; b) \setminus \{x_0\}$ and $x_n \to x_0$, we have $f(x_n) \to L$.
- Notation: $\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = L$ or $f(x) \to L$ as $x \to x_0$.

### b) Examples and Practice on Limits at a Point

**Example 1:** Given the function $f(x) = \frac{x - 1}{x^2 - 1}$. Prove that $\lim_{x \to 1} f(x) = \frac{1}{2}$.

**Solution to Example 1:**
- Let $(x_n)$ be any sequence such that $x_n \neq 1$ and $x_n \to 1$. We have $f(x_n) = \frac{x_n - 1}{x_n^2 - 1} = \frac{1}{x_n + 1}$.
- $\lim_{n \to +\infty} f(x_n) = \lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{1}{x_n + 1} = \frac{1}{2}$. Thus, $\lim_{x \to 1} f(x) = \frac{1}{2}$.

**Limit Laws for Functions at a Point:**
1. If $\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = L$ and $\lim_{x \to x_0} g(x) = M$, then:
   - $\lim_{x \to x_0} [f(x) + g(x)] = L + M$
   - $\lim_{x \to x_0} [f(x) - g(x)] = L - M$
   - $\lim_{x \to x_0} [f(x) \cdot g(x)] = L \cdot M$
   - $\lim_{x \to x_0} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{L}{M}$ (if $M \neq 0$).
2. $\lim_{x \to x_0} c = c$ (where $c$ is a constant), $\lim_{x \to x_0} x^n = x_0^n$ (where $n \in \mathbb{N}^*$).
3. If $f(x) \ge 0$ for all $x \in (a; b) \setminus \{x_0\}$ and $\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = L$, then $L \ge 0$ and $\lim_{x \to x_0} \sqrt{f(x)} = \sqrt{L}$.

**Example 2:** Let $f(x) = x - 1$ and $g(x) = x^3$. Evaluate the following limits:
- a) $\lim_{x \to 1} [3f(x) - g(x)]$;
- b) $\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{[f(x)]^2}{g(x)}$.

**Solution to Example 2:**
- We have $\lim_{x \to 1} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 1}(x - 1) = 0$ and $\lim_{x \to 1} g(x) = \lim_{x \to 1} x^3 = 1$.
- **a)** $\lim_{x \to 1} [3f(x) - g(x)] = 3 \cdot 0 - 1 = -1$.
- **b)** $\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{[f(x)]^2}{g(x)} = \frac{0^2}{1} = 0$.

**Example 3:** Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{x + 9} - 3}{x}$.

**Solution to Example 3:**
- Since the denominator approaches $0$, we multiply by the conjugate expression to eliminate the root in the numerator (for $x \neq 0$):

$$
\frac{\sqrt{x + 9} - 3}{x} = \frac{x}{(\sqrt{x + 9} + 3)x} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x + 9} + 3}
$$

- Therefore, $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{x + 9} - 3}{x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x + 9} + 3} = \frac{1}{3 + 3} = \frac{1}{6}$.

**Practice 1:** Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x - 1}{\sqrt{x} - 1}$.

**Solution to Practice 1:**
- We transform the expression (for $x > 0, x \neq 1$): $\frac{x - 1}{\sqrt{x} - 1} = \frac{(\sqrt{x} - 1)(\sqrt{x} + 1)}{\sqrt{x} - 1} = \sqrt{x} + 1$.
- Thus, $\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x - 1}{\sqrt{x} - 1} = \lim_{x \to 1} (\sqrt{x} + 1) = \sqrt{1} + 1 = 2$.

## 2. One-Sided Limits

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_gioi_han_mot_phia" />

### a) Right-Hand and Left-Hand Limits

**Activity 2. Recognizing the concept of one-sided limits:**
Given the function $f(x) = \frac{|x - 1|}{x - 1}$.
- a) Let $x_n = 1 - \frac{1}{n + 1}$ and $x_n' = 1 + \frac{1}{n}$. Calculate $y_n = f(x_n)$ and $y_n' = f(x_n')$.
- b) Find the limits of the sequences $(y_n)$ and $(y_n')$.
- c) Calculate the corresponding limits as $n \to +\infty$.

**Definition of One-Sided Limits:**
- **Right-hand limit:** Suppose the function $y = f(x)$ is defined on $(x_0; b)$. The number $L$ is called the right-hand limit of $f(x)$ as $x \to x_0$ if for any sequence $(x_n)$ satisfying $x_0 < x_n < b$ and $x_n \to x_0$, we have $f(x_n) \to L$. Notation: $\lim_{x \to x_0^+} f(x) = L$.
- **Left-hand limit:** Suppose the function $y = f(x)$ is defined on $(a; x_0)$. The number $L$ is called the left-hand limit of $f(x)$ as $x \to x_0$ if for any sequence $(x_n)$ satisfying $a < x_n < x_0$ and $x_n \to x_0$, we have $f(x_n) \to L$. Notation: $\lim_{x \to x_0^-} f(x) = L$.
- **Connection Theorem:** $\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = L \iff \lim_{x \to x_0^+} f(x) = \lim_{x \to x_0^-} f(x) = L$.

### b) Examples and Practice on One-Sided Limits

**Example 4:** Given the function:

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2 & \text{if } 0 < x < 1 \\ x + 1 & \text{if } 1 \le x < 2 \end{cases}
$$

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x)$ and $\lim_{x \to 1^+} f(x)$.

**Solution to Example 4:**
- When $x \to 1^-$ ($0 < x < 1$), $f(x) = x^2$, hence $\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 1^-} x^2 = 1^2 = 1$.
- When $x \to 1^+$ ($1 \le x < 2$), $f(x) = x + 1$, hence $\lim_{x \to 1^+} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 1^+} (x + 1) = 1 + 1 = 2$.

**Practice 2:** Given the function:

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases} -x & \text{if } x < 0 \\ \sqrt{x} & \text{if } x \ge 0 \end{cases}
$$

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0^+} f(x)$, $\lim_{x \to 0^-} f(x)$, and $\lim_{x \to 0} f(x)$.

**Solution to Practice 2:**
- $\lim_{x \to 0^+} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 0^+} \sqrt{x} = 0$.
- $\lim_{x \to 0^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 0^-} (-x) = 0$.
- Since $\lim_{x \to 0^+} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 0^-} f(x) = 0$, we have $\lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = 0$.

## 3. Finite Limits of Functions at Infinity

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_gioi_han_tai_vo_cuc" />

### a) Concept of Limits at Infinity

**Activity 3. Recognizing the concept of limits at infinity:**
Given the function $f(x) = 1 + \frac{2}{x - 1}$. Suppose $(x_n)$ is a sequence such that $x_n > 1$ and $x_n \to +\infty$. Evaluate $f(x_n)$ and find $\lim_{n \to +\infty} f(x_n)$.

**Definition of Limits at Infinity:**
- Suppose the function $y = f(x)$ is defined on $(a; +\infty)$. We say that $f(x)$ has a limit $L$ as $x \to +\infty$ if for any sequence $(x_n)$ satisfying $x_n > a$ and $x_n \to +\infty$, we have $f(x_n) \to L$. Notation: $\lim_{x \to +\infty} f(x) = L$.
- Similarly for the case $x \to -\infty$: $\lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) = L$.
- **Fundamental results:** $\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} c = c$, $\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{1}{x^k} = 0$ (where $k$ is a positive integer).

### b) Examples and Practice on Limits at Infinity

**Example 5:** Given $f(x) = 2 + \frac{4}{x - 1}$. Using the definition, find $\lim_{x \to +\infty} f(x)$ and $\lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x)$.

**Solution to Example 5:**
- Taking any sequence $(x_n)$ such that $x_n > 1$ and $x_n \to +\infty$, we have $f(x_n) = 2 + \frac{4}{x_n - 1} \to 2$. Thus, $\lim_{x \to +\infty} f(x) = 2$ and similarly $\lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) = 2$.

**Example 6:** Evaluate $\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}{x}$.

**Solution to Example 6:**
- For $x < 0$, we have $\sqrt{x^2} = -x$, hence $\frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}{x} = \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}{-\sqrt{x^2}} = -\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{x^2}}$.
- Consequently, $\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}{x} = \lim_{x \to -\infty} \left(-\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{x^2}}\right) = -1$.

**Practice 3:** Evaluate $\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 2}}{x + 1}$.

**Solution to Practice 3:**
- Divide both numerator and denominator by $x$ (noting that for $x < 0$, $x = -\sqrt{x^2}$), we get:

$$
\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 2}}{x + 1} = \lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{-\sqrt{1 + \frac{2}{x^2}}}{1 + \frac{1}{x}} = -1.
$$

## 4. Infinite Limits of Functions at a Point

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_gioi_han_vo_cuc" />

### a) Definition of Infinite Limits

**Activity 4. Recognizing the concept of infinite limits:**
Consider the function $f(x) = \frac{1}{x^2}$. Let $x_n = \frac{1}{n}$, show that $f(x_n) \to +\infty$.

**Definition of Infinite Limits:**
- We say that a function $f(x)$ has an infinite limit of $+\infty$ as $x \to x_0$ if for every sequence $(x_n)$ satisfying $x_n \neq x_0$ and $x_n \to x_0$, we have $f(x_n) \to +\infty$. Notation: $\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = +\infty$.
- Similarly for $-\infty$: $\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = -\infty$.

### b) Examples and Rules for Evaluating Infinite Limits

**Example 7:** Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{1}{|x - 1|}$.

**Solution to Example 7:**
- As $x \to 1$, we have $|x - 1| \to 0$ and $|x - 1| > 0$, hence $\frac{1}{|x - 1|} \to +\infty$. Therefore, $\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{1}{|x - 1|} = +\infty$.

**Example 8 (Solving the introductory problem):**
- From the mass formula $m = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$, as $v \to c^-$, we have $\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}} \to 0^+$. Thus $\lim_{v \to c^-} m(v) = +\infty$, meaning that the mass of the object approaches infinity as its velocity approaches the speed of light.

**Practice 4:** Evaluate the following limits:
- a) $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2}{|x|}$;
- b) $\lim_{x \to 2^-} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 - x}}$.

**Solution to Practice 4:**
- **a)** $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2}{|x|} = +\infty$ (since $|x| \to 0$ and $|x| > 0$ for all $x \neq 0$).
- **b)** $\lim_{x \to 2^-} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 - x}} = +\infty$ (since as $x \to 2^-$, $2 - x > 0$ and $\sqrt{2 - x} \to 0$).

**Rules for finding limits of products and quotients involving infinite limits:**
- **Multiplication Rule:** If $\lim f(x) = L \neq 0$ and $\lim g(x) = \pm\infty$, then $\lim [f(x)g(x)] = \pm\infty$ (determined by the sign of $L$ and the sign of the infinity of $g(x)$).
- **Division Rule:** If $\lim f(x) = L$ and $\lim g(x) = \pm\infty$, then $\lim \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = 0$. If $\lim f(x) = L \neq 0$ and $\lim g(x) = 0$ ($g(x)$ maintains a constant positive or negative sign near $x_0$), then $\lim \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \pm\infty$.

**Example 9:** Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x + 1}{x^2}$.

**Solution to Example 9:**
- We have $\lim_{x \to 0} (x + 1) = 1 > 0$ and $\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 = 0$ ($x^2 > 0$ for all $x \neq 0$). Thus, $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x + 1}{x^2} = +\infty$.

**Example 10:** Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 1^+} \frac{1}{x(1 - x)}$ and $\lim_{x \to 1^-} \frac{1}{x(1 - x)}$.

**Solution to Example 10:**
- As $x \to 1^+$, we have $x \to 1$ and $1 - x \to 0^-$ (since $x > 1$), hence $\lim_{x \to 1^+} \frac{1}{x(1 - x)} = -\infty$.
- As $x \to 1^-$, we have $x \to 1$ and $1 - x \to 0^+$ (since $x < 1$), hence $\lim_{x \to 1^-} \frac{1}{x(1 - x)} = +\infty$.

**Practice 5:** Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 2^+} \frac{2x - 1}{x - 2}$ and $\lim_{x \to 2^-} \frac{2x - 1}{x - 2}$.

**Solution to Practice 5:**
- $\lim_{x \to 2^+} \frac{2x - 1}{x - 2} = +\infty$ (since the numerator approaches $3 > 0$ and the denominator $x - 2 \to 0^+$ for $x > 2$).
- $\lim_{x \to 2^-} \frac{2x - 1}{x - 2} = -\infty$ (since the numerator approaches $3 > 0$ and the denominator $x - 2 \to 0^-$ for $x < 2$).

## 5. Applications and Exercises

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_ung_dung_gioi_han_ham_so" />

### a) Real-World Problems and Mathematical Modeling

**Application (Motion Speed and Limits):** A particle moves with a velocity function over time described by $v(t) = \frac{t^2 + 2t}{t + 1}$ (velocity measured in $\text{m/s}$, time $t$ in seconds). Calculate the limit of the particle's velocity as $t \to 1$.

**Solution to Application:**
- We evaluate the limit of the velocity function as $t \to 1$:

$$
\lim_{t \to 1} \frac{t^2 + 2t}{t + 1} = \frac{1^2 + 2(1)}{1 + 1} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5\text{ (m/s)}.
$$

### b) End-of-Lesson Exercises

**Exercise 5.7:** Given two functions $f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}$ and $g(x) = x + 1$. Which of the following statements are correct?
- a) $f(x) = g(x)$;
- b) $\lim_{x \to 1} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 1} g(x)$.

**Solution to Exercise 5.7:**
- **a)** Incorrect, because $f(x)$ is undefined at $x = 1$ while $g(1) = 2$ (they have different domains).
- **b)** Correct, because for all $x \neq 1$, $f(x) = g(x) = x + 1$, hence $\lim_{x \to 1} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 1} g(x) = 2$.

**Exercise 5.8:** Evaluate the following limits:
- a) $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{(x + 2)^2 - 4}{x}$;
- b) $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 9} - 3}{x^2}$.

**Solution to Exercise 5.8:**
- **a)** $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x^2 + 4x}{x} = \lim_{x \to 0} (x + 4) = 4$.
- **b)** Multiplying by the conjugate: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x^2}{x^2(\sqrt{x^2 + 9} + 3)} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9} + 3} = \frac{1}{6}$.

**Exercise 5.9:** Given the Heaviside function:

$$
H(t) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } t < 0 \\ 1 & \text{if } t \ge 0 \end{cases}
$$

Evaluate $\lim_{t \to 0^+} H(t)$ and $\lim_{t \to 0^-} H(t)$.

**Solution to Exercise 5.9:**
- $\lim_{t \to 0^+} H(t) = \lim_{t \to 0^+} 1 = 1$.
- $\lim_{t \to 0^-} H(t) = \lim_{t \to 0^-} 0 = 0$.

**Exercise 5.10:** Evaluate the one-sided limits:
- a) $\lim_{x \to 1^+} \frac{x - 2}{x - 1}$;
- b) $\lim_{x \to 4^-} \frac{x^2 - x + 1}{4 - x}$.

**Solution to Exercise 5.10:**
- **a)** The numerator approaches $-1 < 0$ and the denominator $x - 1 \to 0^+$ as $x \to 1^+$, hence $\lim_{x \to 1^+} \frac{x - 2}{x - 1} = -\infty$.
- **b)** The numerator approaches $13 > 0$ and the denominator $4 - x \to 0^+$ as $x \to 4^-$, hence $\lim_{x \to 4^-} \frac{x^2 - x + 1}{4 - x} = +\infty$.

**Exercise 5.11:** Given the function $g(x) = \frac{x^2 - 5x + 6}{|x - 2|}$. Find $\lim_{x \to 2^+} g(x)$ and $\lim_{x \to 2^-} g(x)$.

**Solution to Exercise 5.11:**
- Simplify: $g(x) = \frac{(x - 2)(x - 3)}{|x - 2|}$.
- As $x \to 2^+$ ($x > 2$), $|x - 2| = x - 2$, so $\lim_{x \to 2^+} g(x) = \lim_{x \to 2^+} (x - 3) = -1$.
- As $x \to 2^-$ ($x < 2$), $|x - 2| = -(x - 2)$, so $\lim_{x \to 2^-} g(x) = \lim_{x \to 2^-} (3 - x) = 1$.

**Exercise 5.12:** Evaluate the following limits:
- a) $\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{1 - 2x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}$;
- b) $\lim_{x \to +\infty} (\sqrt{x^2 + x + 2} - x)$.

**Solution to Exercise 5.12:**
- **a)** Divide both numerator and denominator by $x$ ($x > 0$):

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\frac{1}{x} - 2}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{x^2}}} = \frac{-2}{1} = -2.
$$

- **b)** Multiplying by the conjugate expression:

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x + 2}{\sqrt{x^2 + x + 2} + x} = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{1 + \frac{2}{x}}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{x} + \frac{2}{x^2}} + 1} = \frac{1}{2}.
$$

**Exercise 5.13:** Given the function $f(x) = \frac{2}{(x - 1)(x - 2)}$. Find $\lim_{x \to 2^+} f(x)$ and $\lim_{x \to 2^-} f(x)$.

**Solution to Exercise 5.13:**
- As $x \to 2^+$, the numerator is $2 > 0$, and the denominator $(x - 1)(x - 2) \to 1 \cdot 0^+ = 0^+$, thus $\lim_{x \to 2^+} f(x) = +\infty$.
- As $x \to 2^-$, the numerator is $2 > 0$, and the denominator $(x - 1)(x - 2) \to 1 \cdot 0^- = 0^-$, thus $\lim_{x \to 2^-} f(x) = -\infty$.
