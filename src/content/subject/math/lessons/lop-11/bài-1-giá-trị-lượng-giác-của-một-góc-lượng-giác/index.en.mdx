### a) Concept of Trigonometric Angle and Its Measure

In the plane, consider two rays $Ou, Ov$. Let $Om$ be a ray lying in the same plane. If ray $Om$ rotates around point $O$, in a specific direction from $Ou$ to $Ov$, we say it sweeps out a trigonometric angle with initial ray $Ou$ and terminal ray $Ov$, denoted by $(Ou, Ov)$.
The trigonometric angle $(Ou, Ov)$ is uniquely determined only when the rotational motion of ray $Om$ from initial ray $Ou$ to terminal ray $Ov$ is known. Convention: Counterclockwise rotation is positive, and clockwise rotation is negative.
Thus, if ray $Om$ rotates in the positive direction by exactly one full revolution, we say ray $Om$ rotates by $360^{\circ}$; by 2 revolutions, $720^{\circ}$; in the negative direction by half a revolution, $-180^{\circ}$; and by 1.5 revolutions, $-1{,}5 \cdot 360^{\circ} = -540^{\circ}$.
When ray $Om$ rotates by $\alpha^{\circ}$, the trigonometric angle swept out has measure $\alpha^{\circ}$. The measure of the trigonometric angle with initial ray $Ou$ and terminal ray $Ov$ is denoted by $\text{sd}(Ou, Ov)$.
Each trigonometric angle with vertex $O$ is determined by its initial ray $Ou$, terminal ray $Ov$, and its measure.
Note: For any two rays $Ou, Ov$, there are infinitely many trigonometric angles with initial ray $Ou$ and terminal ray $Ov$. Each such angle is denoted by $(Ou, Ov)$. Their measures differ by an integer multiple of $360^{\circ}$ (or $2\pi$ radians).

**Example 1:** Given a geometric angle $uOv$ with measure $60^{\circ}$. Determine the measures of the trigonometric angles $(Ou, Ov)$ and $(Ov, Ou)$.

Solution to Example 1:
• The trigonometric angles with initial ray $Ou$ and terminal ray $Ov$ have measures:
$$\text{sd}(Ou, Ov) = 60^{\circ} + k360^{\circ} \quad (k \in \mathbb{Z})$$
• The trigonometric angles with initial ray $Ov$ and terminal ray $Ou$ have measures:
$$\text{sd}(Ov, Ou) = -60^{\circ} + k360^{\circ} \quad (k \in \mathbb{Z})$$

### b) Chasles' Relation

Chasles' Relation: For any three rays $Ou, Ov, Ow$, we have:
$$\text{sd}(Ou, Ov) + \text{sd}(Ov, Ow) = \text{sd}(Ou, Ow) + k360^{\circ} \quad (k \in \mathbb{Z})$$
Remark: From Chasles' relation, we deduce:
For any three arbitrary rays $Ox, Ou, Ov$, we have:
$$\text{sd}(Ou, Ov) = \text{sd}(Ox, Ov) - \text{sd}(Ox, Ou) + k360^{\circ} \quad (k \in \mathbb{Z})$$
This relation plays an important role in calculating the measures of trigonometric angles.

**Example 2:** Given a trigonometric angle $(Ox, Ou)$ with measure $-270^{\circ}$ and a trigonometric angle $(Ox, Ov)$ with measure $135^{\circ}$. Calculate the measure of the trigonometric angle $(Ou, Ov)$.

Solution to Example 2:
• The measure of the trigonometric angle with initial ray $Ou$ and terminal ray $Ov$ is:
$$\text{sd}(Ou, Ov) = \text{sd}(Ox, Ov) - \text{sd}(Ox, Ou) + k360^{\circ}$$
$$= 135^{\circ} - (-270^{\circ}) + k360^{\circ} = 405^{\circ} + k360^{\circ} = 45^{\circ} + (k + 1)360^{\circ} = 45^{\circ} + m360^{\circ} \quad (m \in \mathbb{Z})$$
• Thus, the trigonometric angles $(Ou, Ov)$ have measure $45^{\circ} + m360^{\circ} \quad (m \in \mathbb{Z})$.

## Angle Units and Arc Length

### a) Angle Units and Arcs

• Degree unit: An angle of $1^{\circ}$ equals $\frac{1}{180}$ of a straight angle. Degrees are subdivided into smaller units: $1^{\circ} = 60'$, $1' = 60''$.
• Radian unit: Consider a circle $(O)$ centered at $O$ with radius $R$, and an arc $AB$ on $(O)$. We say arc $AB$ has a measure of 1 radian if its length is exactly equal to the radius $R$. In this case, angle $AOB$ has a measure of 1 radian, written $\widehat{AOB} = 1\text{ rad}$.
• Relationship between degrees and radians: Since a circle has a circumference of $2\pi R$, its measure in radians is $2\pi\text{ rad}$. On the other hand, a circle measures $360^{\circ}$, so $360^{\circ} = 2\pi\text{ rad}$.
$$\implies 1^{\circ} = \frac{\pi}{180}\text{ rad} \quad \text{and} \quad 1\text{ rad} = \left(\frac{180}{\pi}\right)^{\circ}$$
Note: When writing the measure of an angle in radians, the unit "rad" is often omitted. For example, the angle $\frac{\pi}{2}$ means $\frac{\pi}{2}\text{ rad}$.

**Example 3:** a) Convert the following degree measures to radians: $45^{\circ}$; $150^{\circ}$.
b) Convert the following radian measures to degrees: $\frac{\pi}{3}$; $\frac{5\pi}{4}$.

Solution to Example 3:
• a) $45^{\circ} = 45 \cdot \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{4}$; $150^{\circ} = 150 \cdot \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{5\pi}{6}$.
• b) $\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\pi}{3} \cdot \left(\frac{180}{\pi}\right)^{\circ} = 60^{\circ}$; $\frac{5\pi}{4} = \frac{5\pi}{4} \cdot \left(\frac{180}{\pi}\right)^{\circ} = 225^{\circ}$.

### b) Arc Length

An arc of a circle of radius $R$ with measure $\alpha$ rad has length:
$$l = R\alpha$$

**Example 4:** Given a circle of radius $2\text{ cm}$. Calculate the length of an arc of measure $\frac{2\pi}{3}$ on this circle.

Solution to Example 4:
• The length of the arc is:
$$l = R\alpha = 2 \cdot \frac{2\pi}{3} = \frac{4\pi}{3}\text{ (cm)}$$

## Trigonometric Values of a Trigonometric Angle

### a) Trigonometric Circle (Unit Circle)

A trigonometric circle is a circle centered at the origin with radius $R = 1$, oriented counterclockwise, and having point $A(1; 0)$ as its origin point.
The point on the trigonometric circle representing a trigonometric angle of measure $\alpha$ (in degrees or radians) is the point $M$ on the trigonometric circle such that $\text{sd}(OA, OM) = \alpha$.

**Example 5:** Determine points $M$ and $N$ on the trigonometric circle representing trigonometric angles with measures $\frac{13\pi}{4}$ and $-150^{\circ}$, respectively.

Solution to Example 5:
• $\frac{13\pi}{4} = 2\pi + \frac{5\pi}{4}$, point $M$ lies in the third quadrant.
• $-150^{\circ} = -180^{\circ} + 30^{\circ}$, point $N$ lies in the third quadrant.

### b) Trigonometric Values of a Trigonometric Angle

Let $M(x; y)$ be a point on the trigonometric circle representing a trigonometric angle of measure $\alpha$:
• The x-coordinate $x$ of point $M$ is called the cosine of $\alpha$, denoted by $\cos \alpha$:
$$\cos \alpha = x$$
• The y-coordinate $y$ of point $M$ is called the sine of $\alpha$, denoted by $\sin \alpha$:
$$\sin \alpha = y$$
• If $\cos \alpha \ne 0$, the ratio $\frac{\sin \alpha}{\cos \alpha}$ is called the tangent of $\alpha$, denoted by $\tan \alpha$:
$$\tan \alpha = \frac{\sin \alpha}{\cos \alpha} = \frac{y}{x} \quad (x \ne 0)$$
• If $\sin \alpha \ne 0$, the ratio $\frac{\cos \alpha}{\sin \alpha}$ is called the cotangent of $\alpha$, denoted by $\cot \alpha$:
$$\cot \alpha = \frac{\cos \alpha}{\sin \alpha} = \frac{x}{y} \quad (y \ne 0)$$
The values $\cos \alpha, \sin \alpha, \tan \alpha, \cot \alpha$ are called the trigonometric values of $\alpha$.
Notes:
• The y-axis is called the sine axis, and the x-axis is called the cosine axis.
• $\sin \alpha, \cos \alpha$ are defined for all $\alpha$, and $-1 \le \sin \alpha \le 1$, $-1 \le \cos \alpha \le 1$.
• $\sin(\alpha + k2\pi) = \sin \alpha$, $\cos(\alpha + k2\pi) = \cos \alpha$ ($k \in \mathbb{Z}$).n• $\tan \alpha$ is defined when $\alpha \ne \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi \quad (k \in \mathbb{Z})$.
• $\cot \alpha$ is defined when $\alpha \ne k\pi \quad (k \in \mathbb{Z})$.

**Example 6:** Given a trigonometric angle with measure $-\frac{\pi}{3}$.
a) Determine point $M$ on the trigonometric circle representing this angle.
b) Calculate the trigonometric values of this angle.

Solution to Example 6:
• a) Point $M$ lies in the fourth quadrant with x-coordinate $\frac{1}{2}$ and y-coordinate $-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
• b) We have:
  $$\cos\left(-\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \sin\left(-\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$
  $$\tan\left(-\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \frac{\sin(-\pi/3)}{\cos(-\pi/3)} = -\sqrt{3}, \quad \cot\left(-\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \frac{\cos(-\pi/3)}{\sin(-\pi/3)} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}.$$.

## Relationships Among Trigonometric Values

### a) Fundamental Trigonometric Identities

For trigonometric values, we have the following fundamental identities:
• $\sin^{2}\alpha + \cos^{2}\alpha = 1$
• $1 + \tan^{2}\alpha = \frac{1}{\cos^{2}\alpha} \quad \left(\alpha \ne \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi, k \in \mathbb{Z}\right)$
• $1 + \cot^{2}\alpha = \frac{1}{\sin^{2}\alpha} \quad (\alpha \ne k\pi, k \in \mathbb{Z})$
• $\tan \alpha \cdot \cot \alpha = 1 \quad \left(\alpha \ne \frac{k\pi}{2}, k \in \mathbb{Z}\right)$

**Example 9:** Calculate the trigonometric values of angle $\alpha$, given: $\sin \alpha = \frac{3}{5}$ and $90^{\circ} < \alpha < 180^{\circ}$.

Solution to Example 9:
• Since $90^{\circ} < \alpha < 180^{\circ}$, $\cos \alpha < 0$. From $\sin^{2}\alpha + \cos^{2}\alpha = 1$, we get:
$$\cos \alpha = -\sqrt{1 - \sin^{2}\alpha} = -\sqrt{1 - \frac{9}{25}} = -\frac{4}{5}$$
• Thus: $\tan \alpha = \frac{\sin \alpha}{\cos \alpha} = \frac{3/5}{-4/5} = -\frac{3}{4}$ and $\cot \alpha = \frac{1}{\tan \alpha} = -\frac{4}{3}$.

### b) Trigonometric Values of Special Related Angles

• Opposite angles ($\alpha$ and $-\alpha$):
$$\cos(-\alpha) = \cos \alpha, \quad \sin(-\alpha) = -\sin \alpha, \quad \tan(-\alpha) = -\tan \alpha, \quad \cot(-\alpha) = -\cot \alpha$$
• Supplementary angles ($\alpha$ and $\pi - \alpha$):
$$\sin(\pi - \alpha) = \sin \alpha, \quad \cos(\pi - \alpha) = -\cos \alpha, \quad \tan(\pi - \alpha) = -\tan \alpha, \quad \cot(\pi - \alpha) = -\cot \alpha$$
• Complementary angles ($\alpha$ and $\frac{\pi}{2} - \alpha$):
$$\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \alpha\right) = \cos \alpha, \quad \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \alpha\right) = \sin \alpha, \quad \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \alpha\right) = \cot \alpha, \quad \cot\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \alpha\right) = \tan \alpha$$
• Angles differing by $\pi$ ($\alpha$ and $\pi + \alpha$):
$$\sin(\pi + \alpha) = -\sin \alpha, \quad \cos(\pi + \alpha) = -\cos \alpha, \quad \tan(\pi + \alpha) = \tan \alpha, \quad \cot(\pi + \alpha) = \cot \alpha$$

**Example 10:** Calculate:
a) $\cos\left(-\frac{11\pi}{4}\right)$;
b) $\cot(-675^{\circ})$.

Solution to Example 10:
• a) $\cos\left(-\frac{11\pi}{4}\right) = \cos\left(\frac{11\pi}{4}\right) = \cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{4} + 2\pi\right) = \cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}\right) = -\cos\left(\pi - \frac{3\pi}{4}\right) = -\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
• b) $\cot(-675^{\circ}) = \cot(45^{\circ} - 2 \cdot 360^{\circ}) = \cot(45^{\circ}) = 1$.

## Applications and Exercises

### a) Real-World Applications

**Application (International Space Station ISS):** The International Space Station ISS orbits in a circular path approximately $400\text{ km}$ above the Earth's surface (Figure 1.1). If a ground station tracks the ISS when it is within a $45^{\circ}$ central angle of this circular orbit directly above the tracking antenna, how many kilometers has the ISS traveled while being tracked by the ground station? Assume the Earth's radius is $6\\,400\text{ km}$. Round the result to the nearest integer.

Solution to Application:
• The orbital radius of the ISS is: $R = 6\\,400 + 400 = 6\\,800\text{ km}$.
• The central angle of the orbit is $\alpha = 45^{\circ} = \frac{\pi}{4}\text{ rad}$.
• The distance traveled by the ISS during tracking is the arc length:
$$l = R\alpha = 6\\,800 \cdot \frac{\pi}{4} = 1\\,700\pi \approx 5\\,341\text{ (km)}$$
• Conclusion: The ISS traveled approximately $5\\,341\text{ km}$.

**Exercise 1.2:** A circle has a radius of $20\text{ cm}$. Find the lengths of the arcs on this circle with the following measures:
a) $\frac{\pi}{12}$;
b) $1{,}5$;
c) $35^{\circ}$;
d) $315^{\circ}$.

Solution to Exercise 1.2:
• a) $l = 20 \cdot \frac{\pi}{12} = \frac{5\pi}{3}\text{ cm} \approx 5{,}24\text{ cm}$.
• b) $l = 20 \cdot 1{,}5 = 30\text{ cm}$.
• c) $\alpha = 35^{\circ} = \frac{35\pi}{180}\text{ rad} \Rightarrow l = 20 \cdot \frac{7\pi}{36} = \frac{35\pi}{9}\text{ cm} \approx 12{,}22\text{ cm}$.
• d) $\alpha = 315^{\circ} = \frac{7\pi}{4}\text{ rad} \Rightarrow l = 20 \cdot \frac{7\pi}{4} = 35\pi\text{ cm} \approx 109{,}96\text{ cm}$.

**Exercise 1.4:** Calculate the trigonometric values of angle $\alpha$, given:
a) $\cos \alpha = \frac{1}{5}$ and $0 < \alpha < \frac{\pi}{2}$;
b) $\sin \alpha = \frac{2}{3}$ and $\frac{\pi}{2} < \alpha < \pi$;
c) $\tan \alpha = \sqrt{5}$ and $\pi < \alpha < \frac{3\pi}{2}$;
d) $\cot \alpha = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ and $\frac{3\pi}{2} < \alpha < 2\pi$.

Solution to Exercise 1.4:
• a) $\sin \alpha = \sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{25}} = \frac{2\sqrt{6}}{5}$; $\tan \alpha = 2\sqrt{6}$; $\cot \alpha = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{6}}$.
• b) $\cos \alpha = -\sqrt{1 - \frac{4}{9}} = -\frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$; $\tan \alpha = -\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}$; $\cot \alpha = -\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$.
• c) $\cos \alpha = -\frac{\sqrt{6}}{6}$; $\sin \alpha = -\frac{\sqrt{30}}{6}$; $\cot \alpha = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{5}$.
• d) $\sin \alpha = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$; $\cos \alpha = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$; $\tan \alpha = -\sqrt{2}$.

**Exercise 1.5:** Prove fundamental trigonometric identities.

Lời giải Bài 1.5:
• a) $\cos^{4}\alpha - \sin^{4}\alpha = (\cos^{2}\alpha - \sin^{2}\alpha)(\cos^{2}\alpha + \sin^{2}\alpha) = \cos^{2}\alpha - (1 - \cos^{2}\alpha) = 2\cos^{2}\alpha - 1$.
• b) $\frac{\cos^{2}\alpha + \tan^{2}\alpha - 1}{\sin^{2}\alpha} = \frac{\cos^{2}\alpha - \cos^{2}\alpha + \tan^{2}\alpha}{\sin^{2}\alpha} = \frac{\tan^{2}\alpha}{\sin^{2}\alpha} = \frac{1}{\cos^{2}\alpha} = 1 + \tan^{2}\alpha$.
