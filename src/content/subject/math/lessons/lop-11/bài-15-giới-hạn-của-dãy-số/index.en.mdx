## Learning Objectives

### **Terminology:**

·       Limit of a sequence ($\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = a$, zero limit, finite limit)

·       Infinite geometric series, sum of an infinite geometric series

·       Infinite limit ($+\infty, -\infty$)

### **Knowledge and Skills:**

·       Recognize and understand the concept of the limit of a sequence as $n \to +\infty$.

·       Master fundamental limits, finite limit algebraic rules, and rules for evaluating infinite limits.

·       Apply sequence limits to solve practical mathematical problems (resolving Zeno's paradox of Achilles and the tortoise, bouncing ball models, drug accumulation in the body, infinite polygonal paths).

## Finite Limits of Sequences

\<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_gioi_han_huu_han_cua_day_so"/>

### a) Zero Limit and Finite Limit Definitions

Zero Limit Definition:
• A sequence $(u_n)$ is said to have a limit of 0 as $n \to +\infty$ if $|u_n|$ can be made smaller than any arbitrarily small positive number for all $n$ sufficiently large, denoted by $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = 0$ or $u_n \to 0$ as $n \to +\infty$.
• Fundamental Results:

+ $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} \frac{1}{n^k} = 0$ (where $k$ is a positive integer);
+ $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} q^n = 0$ (if $|q| < 1$).
+ Squeeze Theorem for sequences: If $|u_n| \le v_n$ for all $n \ge 1$ and $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} v_n = 0$, then $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = 0$.

Finite Limit Definition (Real Number $a$):
• A sequence $(u_n)$ is said to have a limit equal to the real number $a$ as $n \to +\infty$ if $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} (u_n - a) = 0$, denoted by $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = a$ or $u_n \to a$ as $n \to +\infty$.
• Remark: $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = a \iff \lim\_{n \to +\infty} (u_n - a) = 0$. If $u_n = c$ (constant), then $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = c$.

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Finite Limits

**Example 1:** Consider the sequence $(u_n)$ with $u_n = \frac{1}{n^2}$. Explain why this sequence has a limit of 0.

Solution for Example 1:
• We have $|u_n| = \frac{1}{n^2}$. As $n$ becomes sufficiently large, $\frac{1}{n^2}$ becomes arbitrarily small, falling below any prescribed positive tolerance $\varepsilon > 0$.
• For instance, to ensure $|u_n| < 0.0001$, we require $\frac{1}{n^2} < \frac{1}{10\\,000} \Leftrightarrow n^2 > 10\\,000 \Leftrightarrow n > 100$. Thus, from the 101st term onward, all terms have absolute values less than $0.0001$. Therefore, $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = 0$.

**Practice 1:** Prove that $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{3^n} = 0$.

Solution for Practice 1:
• We have $\left| \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{3^n} \right| = \frac{1}{3^n} = \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^n$.
• Since $|q| = \frac{1}{3} < 1$, the fundamental limit theorem gives $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^n = 0$. Hence, $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{3^n} = 0$.

**Example 2:** Given the sequence $(u_n)$ with $u_n = \frac{2n + 1}{n}$. Prove that $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = 2$.

Solution for Example 2:
• We have $u_n - 2 = \frac{2n + 1}{n} - 2 = \frac{2n + 1 - 2n}{n} = \frac{1}{n}$.
• Since $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} \frac{1}{n} = 0$, it follows that $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} (u_n - 2) = 0$, and thus $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = 2$.

**Practice 2:** Given the sequence $(u_n)$ with $u_n = \frac{3 \cdot 2^n - 1}{2^n}$. Prove that $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = 3$.

Solution for Practice 2:
• We have $u_n - 3 = \frac{3 \cdot 2^n - 1}{2^n} - 3 = \frac{3 \cdot 2^n - 1 - 3 \cdot 2^n}{2^n} = -\frac{1}{2^n} = -\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n$.
• Since $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n = 0$ (as $|\frac{1}{2}| < 1$), we obtain $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} (u_n - 3) = 0 \Rightarrow \lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = 3$.

## Theorems on Finite Limits of Sequences

### Algebraic Limit Laws

Arithmetic Operations on Limits:
If $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = a$ and $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} v_n = b$, then:
1\. $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} (u_n + v_n) = a + b$
2\. $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} (u_n - v_n) = a - b$
3\. $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} (u_n \cdot v_n) = a \cdot b$
4\. $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} \frac{u_n}{v_n} = \frac{a}{b}$ (provided $b \ne 0$).
5\. If $u_n \ge 0$ for all $n$ and $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = a$, then $a \ge 0$ and $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} \sqrt{u_n} = \sqrt{a}$.

## Sum of an Infinite Geometric Series

\<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_cap_so_nhan_lui_vo_han"/>

### Concept and Sum Formula

Infinite Geometric Series:
• An infinite geometric sequence $(u_n)$ with common ratio $q$ satisfying $|q| < 1$ is called an infinite geometric series (or a convergent geometric progression).
• The sum of an infinite geometric series $(u_n)$ is defined as the limit of the partial sum $S_n$ of the first $n$ terms as $n \to +\infty$:
$$S = \lim\_{n \to +\infty} S_n = \lim\_{n \to +\infty} \frac{u_1(1 - q^n)}{1 - q} = \frac{u_1}{1 - q} \quad (|q| < 1)$

## Infinite Limits of Sequences

\<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_gioi_han_vo_cuc"/>

### Definition of Infinite Limits

Infinite Limits:
• A sequence $(u_n)$ is said to approach positive infinity ($+\infty$) as $n \to +\infty$ if $u_n$ exceeds any arbitrarily large positive number for all $n$ sufficiently large, denoted by $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = +\infty$.
• A sequence $(u_n)$ is said to approach negative infinity ($-\infty$) as $n \to +\infty$ if $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} (-u_n) = +\infty$, denoted by $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} u_n = -\infty$.
• Fundamental Results:

+ $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} n^k = +\infty$ (where $k$ is a positive integer);
+ $\lim\_{n \to +\infty} q^n = +\infty$ (for $q > 1$).

## Applications and Exercises

### Real-World Applications and Modeling

**Application (Resolving Zeno's Paradox - Achilles and the Tortoise):** Suppose Achilles runs at $100\text{ km/h}$, the tortoise runs at $1\text{ km/h}$, and the initial head start is $a = 100\text{ km}$.
a) Compute the time intervals $t_1, t_2, \dots, t_n, \dots$ required for Achilles to bridge each successive distance gap.
b) Calculate the total cumulative time required for Achilles to catch the tortoise.
c) Where lies the flaw in Zeno's reasoning?

Solution for Application:
• a) The time for Achilles to cover the initial $100\text{ km}$ gap is $t_1 = \frac{100}{100} = 1$ hour; during this time the tortoise crawls $1\text{ km}$. The subsequent gap requires $t_2 = \frac{1}{100}$ hours, and so on.
• b) The total time is the sum of an infinite geometric series: $T = 1 + \frac{1}{100} + \frac{1}{100^2} + \dots = \frac{1}{1 - \frac{1}{100}} = \frac{100}{99}$ hours ($\approx 1.01$ hours).
• c) Zeno's flaw was assuming that an infinite number of temporal intervals must sum to infinity. Modern mathematics proves that an infinite series of decreasing terms can converge to a finite sum.

**Exercise 5.5 (Cumulative Drug Concentration in the Body):** A patient takes a $150\text{ mg}$ medication tablet daily. Before each subsequent dose, $5\\%$ of the residual drug from prior doses remains in the body. Calculate the total drug amount after taking the 5th tablet, and estimate the steady-state drug concentration under long-term administration.

Solution for Exercise 5.5:
• Drug amount after day 1: $150\text{ mg}$.
• Drug amount after day 2: $150 + 150(0.05) = 150(1 + 0.05)$.
• After taking the 5th tablet, the total accumulated drug is the sum of the first 5 terms of a geometric series with $u_1 = 150$ and common ratio $q = 0.05$:
  $$S_5 = 150 \cdot \frac{1 - (0.05)^5}{1 - 0.05} = \frac{150(1 - 0.0000003125)}{0.95} \approx 157.89\text{ (mg).}$$
• Under long-term administration ($n \to +\infty$), the drug quantity converges to the sum of an infinite geometric series:
  $$S = \frac{150}{1 - 0.05} = \frac{150}{0.95} = \frac{3\000}{19} \approx 157.89\text{ (mg).}$$
