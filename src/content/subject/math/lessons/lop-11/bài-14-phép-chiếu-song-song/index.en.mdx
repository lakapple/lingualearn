## Parallel Projection and Projections

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_phep_chieu_song_song"/>

### a) Definition of Parallel Projection

Definition:
Given a plane (alpha) and a straight line Delta intersecting (alpha). For each point M in space, determine its corresponding point M' as follows:
• If M in Delta, then M' is the intersection point of (alpha) and Delta.
• If M not in Delta, then M' is the intersection point of (alpha) and the straight line passing through M and parallel to Delta.
The point M' is called the parallel projection of point M onto plane (alpha) along the projection direction Delta.
• The mapping that associates each point M with its projection M' is called the parallel projection onto (alpha) along direction Delta.
• Plane (alpha) is called the projection plane, and line Delta is called the direction of projection.
• The projection of a figure is the set of projections M' of all points M belonging to that figure.

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Parallel Projection

**Example 1:** Given a triangular prism ABC.A'B'C'.
a) Determine the parallel projection of point A onto plane (A'B'C') along the direction CC'.
b) Let M be a point on segment AB. Determine the parallel projection of M onto plane (A'B'C') along the direction CC'.

Solution for Example 1:
• a) Since ABC.A'B'C' is a prism, AA' || BB' || CC'. Because vertex A' in (A'B'C') and line AA' is parallel to the projection direction CC', A' is precisely the parallel projection of A onto plane (A'B'C') along direction CC'.
• b) In plane (ABB'A'), draw the line through M parallel to AA' (and thus parallel to CC') intersecting edge A'B' at point M'. Since M' in (A'B'C'), M' is the projection of M onto plane (A'B'C') along CC'.

**Practice 1:** Given a parallelepiped ABCD.EFGH. Determine the projection of point A onto plane (DCGH) along direction BC and along direction BG.

Solution for Practice 1:
• Along direction BC: Since ABCD.EFGH is a parallelepiped, base ABCD is a parallelogram, implying AB || DC. Because point D in (DCGH) and line AD is parallel to projection direction BC, D is the projection of A onto (DCGH) along BC.
• Along direction BG: The line passing through A parallel to BG intersects plane (DCGH) at point H (since AH || BG and H in (DCGH)). Thus, H is the projection of A onto (DCGH) along BG.

## Properties of Parallel Projection

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_tinh_chat_phep_chieu_song_song"/>

### a) Fundamental Properties

Core Properties of Parallel Projection:
1\. Collinearity preservation: Parallel projection maps three collinear points into three collinear points and preserves their relative order.
2\. Line preservation: Parallel projection maps lines into lines, rays into rays, and line segments into line segments.
3\. Parallelism preservation: Parallel projection maps two parallel straight lines into two parallel straight lines or two coincident lines.
4\. Ratio preservation: Parallel projection preserves the ratio of lengths of two line segments lying on the same straight line or on two parallel straight lines:
$$\frac{AB}{CD} = \frac{A'B'}{C'D'}$$

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Properties

**Example 2:** Given a parallelogram ABCD and let A'B'C'D' be its parallel projection onto a plane (P) along a direction d. Prove that quadrilateral A'B'C'D' is a parallelogram.

Solution for Example 2:
• Since ABCD is a parallelogram, AB || CD and AD || BC.
• By the properties of parallel projection, projections of parallel lines are parallel lines or coincident lines, which implies A'B' || C'D' and A'D' || B'C'.
• Therefore, quadrilateral A'B'C'D' has opposite sides parallel, proving it is a parallelogram.

**Luyện tập 2:** Prove that the parallel projection of a trapezoid is a trapezoid.

Solution for Practice 2:
• Let trapezoid ABCD have bases AB || CD. Let A'B'C'D' be the parallel projection of quadrilateral ABCD.
• Since AB || CD, their projections satisfy A'B' || C'D' (they cannot be coincident because non-parallel legs AD and BC do not project onto the same lines).
• Since A'B' || C'D', quadrilateral A'B'C'D' has at least one pair of parallel sides, confirming it is a trapezoid.

**Example 3:** Given a triangle ABC where M is the midpoint of BC. A parallel projection maps triangle ABC to triangle A'B'C' and maps M to M'. Prove that A'M' is a median of triangle A'B'C'.

Solution for Example 3:
• Since M is the midpoint of BC, points B, M, C are collinear in that order with BM/MC = 1.
• Parallel projection preserves collinearity, point ordering, and segment length ratios; hence, B', M', C' are collinear in that order with B'M'/M'C' = BM/MC = 1.
• This implies M' is the midpoint of segment B'C'. Therefore, A'M' is a median of triangle A'B'C'.

## Graphical Representation of Spatial Figures

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_hinh_bieu_dien_hinh_khong_gian"/>

### a) Concept of Graphical Representation and Drawing Rules

Concept of Graphical Representation:
• The graphical representation of a spatial figure on a plane is its parallel projection onto a plane along a projection direction, or a figure similar to that projection.
Drawing Rules for Plane Figures:
• Triangles (isosceles, equilateral, right-angled) are represented by corresponding triangles.
• Squares, rectangles, rhombuses, and parallelograms are represented by parallelograms.
• A trapezoid with bases AB || CD is represented by a trapezoid A'B'C'D' with A'B' || C'D' and AB/CD = A'B'/C'D'.
• A circle is represented by an ellipse.

### b) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Graphical Representation

**Example 4:** Draw the graphical representation of a quadrangular prism ABCD.A'B'C'D' whose base ABCD is a trapezoid with AB || CD and AB = 2CD.

Solution for Example 4:
• The lateral faces of a prism are parallelograms, so their graphical representations are parallelograms.
• Base ABCD is a trapezoid with AB = 2CD, so its representation is a trapezoid where base AB is drawn twice as long as base CD.
• Combining these yields the complete graphical representation of the prism ABCD.A'B'C'D'.

**Luyện tập 4:** Draw the graphical representation of a pyramid S.ABCD with a parallelogram base ABCD.

Solution for Practice 4:
• Draw base ABCD as a parallelogram (with edge AD drawn dashed for occlusion).
• Choose vertex S outside the base plane, and connect S to vertices A, B, C, D (draw lateral edges SA, SB, SC as solid lines and SD as a dashed line).
• This completes the graphical representation of pyramid S.ABCD.

## Applications and Exercises

### a) Real-World Applications and Modeling

**Application 1 (Goal-Line Technology in Football):** When a goal is scored, how is the orthogonal projection of the football onto the ground along the vertical direction positioned relative to the goal line?

Solution for Application:
• According to football regulations, a goal is scored when the entire ball has crossed completely beyond the vertical plane containing the goal line.
• The orthogonal projection of the entire ball onto the ground along the vertical direction must lie completely past the outer edge of the goal line into the goal interior.

**Exercise 4.34 (Shadows of Ladder Bars under Sunlight):** In a diagram, AB and CD represent the shadows cast by two parallel rungs of a ladder under sunlight. Explain why AB is parallel to CD.

Solution for Exercise 4.34:
• Sunlight rays shining onto the ground form mutually parallel straight lines, acting as the projection direction of a parallel projection.
• The two rungs of the ladder are parallel to each other in space.
• By the properties of parallel projection, projections of parallel straight lines (namely, shadows AB and CD on the ground) remain parallel.

### b) Detailed Section Exercises

**Exercise 4.29:** Which of the following propositions are true?
a) Parallel projection maps a line segment into a line segment.
b) Parallel projection maps two parallel straight lines into two intersecting straight lines.
c) Parallel projection maps an equilateral triangle into an isosceles triangle.
d) Parallel projection maps a square into a parallelogram.

Solution for Exercise 4.29:
• Proposition a) is true (preservation of line segments).
• Proposition d) is true (parallel projection maps a square into a parallelogram).
• Proposition b) is false (parallel lines project to parallel or coincident lines).
• Proposition c) is false (an equilateral triangle can project into any general triangle, not necessarily isosceles).

**Exercise 4.30:** If triangle A'B'C' is the projection of triangle ABC under a parallel projection, is triangle ABC also the projection of triangle A'B'C' under some parallel projection? Explain why.

Solution for Exercise 4.30:
• Yes. By reversing the projection direction and selecting a suitable projection plane (or inverting the parallel projection system), triangle ABC can indeed be regarded as the parallel projection of triangle A'B'C'.

**Exercise 4.31:** A parallel projection maps triangle ABC to triangle A'B'C'. Prove that the projection maps the centroid of triangle ABC to the centroid of triangle A'B'C'.

Solution for Exercise 4.31:
• Let M be the midpoint of BC and M' be its projection (so M' is the midpoint of B'C').
• Median AM of triangle ABC projects to median A'M' of triangle A'B'C'.
• Centroid G divides segment AM in the ratio AG/GM = 2. Because parallel projection preserves length ratios along a line, projection G' of G divides segment A'M' in the same ratio A'G'/G'M' = 2.
• Therefore, G' is the centroid of triangle A'B'C'.

**Exercise 4.32:** Can Figure 4.65 serve as a graphical representation of a regular hexagon? Why or why not?

Solution for Exercise 4.32:
• Yes. A regular hexagon in space, when projected onto a plane along an appropriate direction, yields a centrally symmetric graphical representation whose corresponding segment length ratios satisfy parallel projection properties (Figure 4.65 can indeed represent a regular hexagon).

**Exercise 4.33:** Draw the graphical representation of a pyramid S.ABCD whose base ABCD is a trapezoid with AB || CD, AB = 2 cm, and CD = 6 cm.

Solution for Exercise 4.33:
• Draw base ABCD as a trapezoid with CD = 3AB (maintaining the proportional scale of 2 cm and 6 cm).
• Choose apex S above the base and connect S to vertices A, B, C, D (drawing edge AD as dashed) to complete the pyramid representation.
