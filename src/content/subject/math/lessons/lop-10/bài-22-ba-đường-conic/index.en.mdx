## Learning Objectives

### **Terminology:**

·       Conic sections: ellipse, hyperbola, parabola

·       Foci, focal length (focal distance), axis of symmetry, vertices

·       Directrix, focal parameter of a parabola

·       Canonical (standard) equation of conic sections

### **Knowledge and Skills:**

·       Recognize the geometric definitions and characteristic elements of the three conic sections: ellipse, hyperbola, and parabola.

·       Recognize, derive, and write canonical (standard) equations for ellipses, hyperbolas, and parabolas.

·       Determine foci, focal distance, directrices, and points on a conic section from its canonical equation.

·       Apply conic properties and optical reflection laws to solve real-world problems (lithotripsy shockwave focus, searchlights, astronomical telescopes, LORAN radio navigation).

## Ellipse

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_duong_elip"/>

### a) Geometric Definition of an Ellipse

Definition: Let $F_1$ and $F_2$ be two fixed distinct points in a plane with focal distance $F_1F_2 = 2c > 0$. Let $a$ be a given real constant such that $a > c$.
The locus of all points $M$ in the plane such that:
$$MF_1 + MF_2 = 2a$$
is called an ellipse.
• The two points $F_1$ and $F_2$ are called the foci (singular: focus) of the ellipse.
• The constant distance $F_1F_2 = 2c$ is called the focal distance (or focal length).
• Condition $a > c$: In triangle $MF_1F_2$, the triangle inequality requires $MF_1 + MF_2 > F_1F_2 \Leftrightarrow 2a > 2c \Leftrightarrow a > c$.

### b) Canonical Equation of an Ellipse

Canonical (Standard) Equation:
Choose a Cartesian coordinate system $Oxy$ whose origin $O$ is the midpoint of the segment $F_1F_2$, and the positive $x$-axis lies along the ray $OF_2$. Then:
• The foci have coordinates: $F_1(-c; 0)$ and $F_2(c; 0)$ (where $c > 0$).
• The canonical equation of the ellipse is:
$$\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1 \quad (\text{where } a > b > 0 \text{ and } b^2 = a^2 - c^2)$$
• Conversely, every equation in this canonical form represents an ellipse with foci $F_1(-\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}; 0)$, $F_2(\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}; 0)$, focal distance $2c = 2\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$, and constant focal sum $MF_1 + MF_2 = 2a$.

### c) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Ellipses

**Example 1:** Given a regular hexagon $ABCDEF$. Prove that the four vertices $B, C, E, F$ lie on an ellipse whose foci are $A$ and $D$.

Solution for Example 1:
• In the regular hexagon $ABCDEF$, all sides are equal and all interior angles equal $120^\circ$. The triangles $ABC, BCD, DEF, EFA$ are congruent (SAS), which yields equal short diagonal lengths: $AC = BD = DF = AE$.
• Therefore: $BA + BD = CA + CD = EA + ED = FA + FD > AD$.
• By definition, the four points $B, C, E, F$ lie on a single ellipse whose foci are $A$ and $D$.

**Practice 1:** An elliptical billiard table has a pocket located at focus $F_2$. If a billiard ball placed at focus $F_1$ is struck against the cushion at point $M$ and rebounds into pocket $F_2$, does the total distance traveled $MF_1 + MF_2$ depend on the contact point $M$? Why?

Solution for Practice 1:
• No, the distance does not depend on $M$. Since the cushion forms an ellipse with foci $F_1$ and $F_2$, by the geometric definition of an ellipse, the sum of distances $MF_1 + MF_2 = 2a$ is a constant for every contact point $M$ along the boundary.

**Example 2:** Given the ellipse with canonical equation $\frac{x^2}{25} + \frac{y^2}{16} = 1$. Find its foci, focal distance, and the sum of focal distances from any point on the ellipse.

Solution for Example 2:
• We have $a^2 = 25 \Rightarrow a = 5$ and $b^2 = 16 \Rightarrow b = 4$.
• The linear eccentricity parameter satisfies: $c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = \sqrt{25 - 16} = \sqrt{9} = 3$.
• The foci of the ellipse are: $F_1(-3; 0)$ and $F_2(3; 0)$.
• The focal distance is: $F_1F_2 = 2c = 2 \cdot 3 = 6$.
• The focal sum from any point on the ellipse is: $2a = 2 \cdot 5 = 10$.

**Practice 2:** Given the ellipse with canonical equation $\frac{x^2}{100} + \frac{y^2}{64} = 1$. Find its foci and focal distance.

Solution for Practice 2:
• We have $a^2 = 100$ and $b^2 = 64 \Rightarrow c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = \sqrt{100 - 64} = \sqrt{36} = 6$.
• The foci are: $F_1(-6; 0)$ and $F_2(6; 0)$.
• The focal distance is: $2c = 2 \cdot 6 = 12$.

**Application 1 (Elliptical Transom Window Arch):** The arched transom of a window is designed as the upper half of the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1$ above the horizontal axis, where 1 unit on the blueprint represents $30\text{ cm}$ in reality. Determine the clearance height $h$ of the arch at a point $75\text{ cm}$ from the central midpoint of the base.

Solution for Application 1:
• At a distance of $75\text{ cm}$ from the center, the coordinate on the blueprint is: $x_0 = \frac{75}{30} = 2.5$.
• Substituting $x_0 = 2.5$ into the ellipse equation:
$$\frac{(2.5)^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1 \Leftrightarrow \frac{6.25}{16} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1 \Leftrightarrow \frac{y^2}{4} = 1 - \frac{6.25}{16} = \frac{9.75}{16} \Rightarrow y^2 = \frac{9.75}{4} = 2.4375$$
• Since $y > 0$, we have $y = \sqrt{2.4375} \approx 1.5612$ (coordinate units).
• The actual clearance height $h$ is:
$$h = 1.5612 \cdot 30 \approx 46.84\text{ (cm)}.$$

## Hyperbola

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_duong_hypebol"/>

### a) Geometric Definition of a Hyperbola

Definition: Let $F_1$ and $F_2$ be two fixed distinct points in a plane with focal distance $F_1F_2 = 2c > 0$. Let $a$ be a given positive real constant such that $a < c$.
The locus of all points $M$ in the plane such that the absolute difference of their distances to $F_1$ and $F_2$ is constant:
$$|MF_1 - MF_2| = 2a$$
is called a hyperbola.
• The points $F_1$ and $F_2$ are called the foci of the hyperbola.
• The distance $F_1F_2 = 2c$ is called the focal distance.
• The hyperbola consists of two separate symmetric branches: one branch satisfies $MF_1 - MF_2 = 2a$, and the other satisfies $MF_2 - MF_1 = 2a$.
• Condition $a < c$: In triangle $MF_1F_2$, the triangle inequality ensures $|MF_1 - MF_2| < F_1F_2 \Leftrightarrow 2a < 2c \Leftrightarrow a < c$.

### b) Canonical Equation of a Hyperbola

Canonical (Standard) Equation:
Choose a Cartesian coordinate system $Oxy$ with origin $O$ at the midpoint of $F_1F_2$, and the positive $x$-axis pointing along ray $OF_2$. Then:
• The foci have coordinates: $F_1(-c; 0)$ and $F_2(c; 0)$.
• The canonical equation of the hyperbola is:
$$\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1 \quad (\text{where } a > 0, b > 0 \text{ and } b^2 = c^2 - a^2)$$
• Conversely, every equation in this canonical form represents a hyperbola with foci $F_1(-\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}; 0)$, $F_2(\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}; 0)$, focal distance $2c = 2\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$, and focal difference $|MF_1 - MF_2| = 2a$.

### c) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Hyperbolas

**Example 3:** Two circular islands with centers $O_1, O_2$ and distinct radii $R_1, R_2$ ($R_1 \ne R_2$) are situated in open sea. Prove that the boundary equidistant from the two islands belongs to one branch of a hyperbola.

Solution for Example 3:
• A maritime point $M$ is equidistant from the two islands if and only if: $MO_1 - R_1 = MO_2 - R_2 \Leftrightarrow MO_1 - MO_2 = R_1 - R_2$.
• Since $R_1 \ne R_2$, the difference $|MO_1 - MO_2| = |R_1 - R_2| = 2a$ is constant.
• Because the circular islands are disjoint, $O_1O_2 > R_1 + R_2 > |R_1 - R_2| \Leftrightarrow 2c > 2a \Leftrightarrow c > a$. By definition, the boundary path lies on one branch of a hyperbola whose foci are $O_1$ and $O_2$.

**Practice 3:** Given a rectangle $ABCD$ where $M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of opposite sides $AB$ and $CD$, respectively. Prove that the four vertices $A, B, C, D$ lie on a single hyperbola with foci $M$ and $N$.

Solution for Practice 3:
• By rectangle symmetry, $AM = BM$ and $DN = CN$. The right triangles $\triangle AMN \cong \triangle BMN$ and $\triangle DMN \cong \triangle CMN$ are congruent, yielding $AN = BN$ and $DM = CM$.
• Consequently: $|AM - AN| = |BM - BN| = |DM - DN| = |CM - CN|$.
• Since $|AM - AN| < MN$, by definition all four vertices $A, B, C, D$ lie on the hyperbola with foci $M$ and $N$.

**Example 4:** Given the hyperbola with canonical equation $\frac{x^2}{9} - \frac{y^2}{16} = 1$. Find its foci, focal distance, and the absolute difference of focal distances from any point on the hyperbola.

Solution for Example 4:
• We have $a^2 = 9 \Rightarrow a = 3$ and $b^2 = 16 \Rightarrow b = 4$.
• The focal parameter is: $c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$.
• The foci are: $F_1(-5; 0)$ and $F_2(5; 0)$.
• The focal distance is: $2c = 2 \cdot 5 = 10$.
• The absolute difference of focal distances is: $2a = 2 \cdot 3 = 6$.

**Practice 4:** Given the hyperbola $(H): \frac{x^2}{144} - \frac{y^2}{25} = 1$. Find the foci and focal distance of $(H)$.

Solution for Practice 4:
• Here $a^2 = 144 \Rightarrow a = 12$ and $b^2 = 25 \Rightarrow b = 5$.
• Focal parameter: $c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = \sqrt{144 + 25} = \sqrt{169} = 13$.
• The foci of $(H)$ are: $F_1(-13; 0)$ and $F_2(13; 0)$.
• The focal distance is: $2c = 2 \cdot 13 = 26$.

## Parabola

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_duong_parabol"/>

### a) Geometric Definition of a Parabola

Definition: Let $F$ be a fixed point and $\Delta$ be a fixed line not containing $F$.
The locus of all points $M$ in the plane equidistant from $F$ and $\Delta$ is called a parabola:
$$MF = d(M, \Delta)$$
• The point $F$ is called the focus of the parabola.
• The line $\Delta$ is called the directrix of the parabola.
• The distance from the focus $F$ to the directrix $\Delta$ is called the focal parameter of the parabola, denoted by $p$ ($p = d(F, \Delta) > 0$).

### b) Canonical Equation of a Parabola

Canonical (Standard) Equation:
Let $H$ be the orthogonal projection of focus $F$ onto directrix $\Delta$. Choose a Cartesian coordinate system $Oxy$ whose origin $O$ is the midpoint of segment $HF$, and whose positive $x$-axis points along ray $OF$. Then:
• Focus coordinates: $F\left(\frac{p}{2}; 0\right)$.
• Directrix equation: $\Delta: x = -\frac{p}{2}$ (or $x + \frac{p}{2} = 0$).
• The canonical equation of the parabola $(P)$ is:
$$y^2 = 2px \quad (p > 0)$$
• Conversely, every equation of the form $y^2 = 2px$ ($p > 0$) represents a parabola with focus $F\left(\frac{p}{2}; 0\right)$ and directrix $\Delta: x = -\frac{p}{2}$.

### c) Illustrative Examples and Practice on Parabolas

**Example 5:** Given the parabola $(P): y^2 = x$.
a) Find the focus $F$ and directrix $\Delta$ of $(P)$.
b) Find all points on $(P)$ whose distance to the focus $F$ equals 3.

Solution for Example 5:
• a) Matching with $y^2 = 2px = x \Rightarrow 2p = 1 \Rightarrow p = \frac{1}{2}$.
  The focus is: $F\left(\frac{p}{2}; 0\right) = F\left(\frac{1}{4}; 0\right)$.
  The directrix has equation: $\Delta: x = -\frac{p}{2} = -\frac{1}{4}$ (or $x + \frac{1}{4} = 0$).
• b) A point $M(x_0; y_0) \in (P)$ satisfies $MF = 3 \Leftrightarrow d(M, \Delta) = 3$.
  Since $x_0 = y_0^2 \ge 0$, we have $d(M, \Delta) = \left|x_0 + \frac{1}{4}\right| = x_0 + \frac{1}{4}$.
  Setting $x_0 + \frac{1}{4} = 3 \Leftrightarrow x_0 = \frac{11}{4}$.
  Substituting into the parabola equation: $y_0^2 = \frac{11}{4} \Rightarrow y_0 = \pm \frac{\sqrt{11}}{2}$.
  Thus, there are two points: $M_1\left(\frac{11}{4}; \frac{\sqrt{11}}{2}\right)$ and $M_2\left(\frac{11}{4}; -\frac{\sqrt{11}}{2}\right)$.

**Application 2:** In a coastal region between a mainland shoreline and an island, a maritime boundary is demarcated to be equidistant from the mainland and the island. Model the coastline as a straight line and the island as a circle with center $O$ and radius $R$. What geometric shape does this boundary curve form, and why?

Solution for Application 2:
• Let $\Delta$ denote the linear shoreline and $(O; R)$ represent the circular island.
• A boundary point $M$ is equidistant from the coastline and the island if and only if:
$$d(M, \Delta) = MO - R \Leftrightarrow MO = d(M, \Delta) + R = d(M, \Delta')$$
where $\Delta'$ is an imaginary line parallel to the coastline $\Delta$, shifted inland by a distance $R$.
• Hence, point $M$ is always equidistant from the fixed center point $O$ and the fixed directrix line $\Delta'$. By definition, this boundary forms a section of a parabola with focus $O$ and directrix $\Delta'$.

## Applications and Exercises

### a) Optical Properties and Practical Problems

Optical Reflection Properties of Conic Sections:
• Elliptical reflector: Any ray emitted from one focus reflects off the interior elliptical surface and converges directly onto the second focus (applications: whispering galleries, extracorporeal shockwave lithotripsy).
• Hyperbolic reflector: A ray directed toward one focus of a convex hyperbolic mirror reflects so that its backward ray trace passes through the second focus (applications: Cassegrain reflector telescopes).
• Parabolic reflector: A ray originating from the focus $F$ reflects off the parabolic curve parallel to the axis of symmetry (applications: automotive headlamps, searchlights); conversely, incoming parallel rays reflect and converge precisely at focus $F$ (applications: satellite dishes, solar concentrators).

**Application 3 (Shockwave Lithotripter):** The elliptical reflector in a medical shockwave lithotripter is governed by the canonical equation $\frac{x^2}{400} + \frac{y^2}{76} = 1$ (in cm). The shockwave generator is stationed at one focus, and the kidney stone is positioned at the other focus. Compute the distance from the shockwave generator to the targeted kidney stone.

Solution for Application 3:
• From the equation, $a^2 = 400$ and $b^2 = 76$.
• The half-focal parameter is: $c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = \sqrt{400 - 76} = \sqrt{324} = 18\text{ (cm)}$.
• The distance from the shockwave source (focus $F_1$) to the kidney stone (focus $F_2$) is the focal distance:
$$F_1F_2 = 2c = 2 \cdot 18 = 36\text{ (cm)}.$$

**Exercise 7.24 (Ship Positioning via LORAN Navigation):** Two radio transmission stations located at positions $A$ and $B$ are separated by $300\text{ km}$. Both stations broadcast synchronized signals at a speed of $292\\,000\text{ km/s}$ to a ship. The signal from $A$ arrives $0.0005\text{ s}$ earlier than the signal from $B$. Identify the hyperbola on which the ship is located and write its canonical equation in kilometers.

Solution for Exercise 7.24:
• Let $M$ be the coordinates of the ship. The difference in distance to the two radio beacons is:
$$MB - MA = v \cdot \Delta t = 292\\,000 \cdot 0.0005 = 146\text{ (km)}$$
• By definition, $M$ lies on a hyperbola with foci at $A$ and $B$ and focal distance $2c = AB = 300\text{ km} \Rightarrow c = 150\text{ km}$.
• The transverse semi-axis is $2a = 146\text{ km} \Rightarrow a = 73\text{ km}$.
• Thus: $b^2 = c^2 - a^2 = 150^2 - 73^2 = 22\\,500 - 5\\,329 = 17\\,171$.
• The canonical equation of the hyperbola is:
$$\frac{x^2}{5\\,329} - \frac{y^2}{17\\,171} = 1$$
(The vessel lies along the branch corresponding to $x > 0$).

**Exercise 7.25 (Parabolic Road Curve):** A highway bend is shaped as a parabola with entrance point $A$ and exit point $B$, where $AB = 400\text{ m}$. The apex (vertex) of the parabola $(P)$ is $20\text{ m}$ from segment $AB$ and is equidistant from $A$ and $B$.
a) Formulate the canonical equation of $(P)$, where 1 unit on the coordinate axes corresponds to $1\text{ m}$ in reality.
b) Formulate the canonical equation of $(P)$, where 1 unit on the coordinate axes corresponds to $1\text{ km}$ in reality.

Solution for Exercise 7.25:
• a) Metric scale ($1\text{ unit} = 1\text{ m}$):
  Place the vertex at the origin $O(0; 0)$ with axis of symmetry along $Ox$, giving canonical form $y^2 = 2px$ ($p > 0$).
  The distance from the vertex to segment $AB$ is $20\text{ m}$, so $x_A = x_B = 20$.
  Since $AB = 400\text{ m}$ is bisected symmetrically, $y_A = 200$ and $y_B = -200$. Substituting $A(20; 200)$ into $y^2 = 2px$:
$$200^2 = 2p(20) \Leftrightarrow 40\\,000 = 40p \Rightarrow p = 1\\,000$$
  The canonical equation is: $y^2 = 2\\,000x$.
• b) Kilometer scale ($1\text{ unit} = 1\text{ km}$):
  Convert: $AB = 400\text{ m} = 0.4\text{ km} \Rightarrow y_A = 0.2$; vertex offset $20\text{ m} = 0.02\text{ km} \Rightarrow x_A = 0.02$.
  Substituting $A(0.02; 0.2)$ into $y^2 = 2px$:
$$(0.2)^2 = 2p(0.02) \Leftrightarrow 0.04 = 0.04p \Rightarrow p = 1$$
  The canonical equation is: $y^2 = 2x$.

### b) Detailed Section Exercises

**Exercise 7.19:** Given the ellipse with equation: $\frac{x^2}{36} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$. Find its foci and focal distance.

Solution for Exercise 7.19:
• We have $a^2 = 36, b^2 = 9 \Rightarrow c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = \sqrt{36 - 9} = \sqrt{27} = 3\sqrt{3}$.
• The foci of the ellipse are: $F_1(-3\sqrt{3}; 0)$ and $F_2(3\sqrt{3}; 0)$.
• The focal distance is: $2c = 2 \cdot 3\sqrt{3} = 6\sqrt{3}$.

**Exercise 7.20:** Given the hyperbola with equation: $\frac{x^2}{7} - \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$. Find its foci and focal distance.

Solution for Exercise 7.20:
• We have $a^2 = 7, b^2 = 9 \Rightarrow c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = \sqrt{7 + 9} = \sqrt{16} = 4$.
• The foci of the hyperbola are: $F_1(-4; 0)$ and $F_2(4; 0)$.
• The focal distance is: $2c = 2 \cdot 4 = 8$.

**Exercise 7.21:** Given the parabola with equation: $y^2 = 8x$. Find its focus and directrix.

Solution for Exercise 7.21:
• The equation is in standard form $y^2 = 2px = 8x \Rightarrow 2p = 8 \Rightarrow p = 4$.
• The focus is: $F\left(\frac{p}{2}; 0\right) = F(2; 0)$.
• The directrix has equation: $\Delta: x = -\frac{p}{2} = -2$ (or $x + 2 = 0$).

**Exercise 7.22:** Write the canonical equation of the ellipse passing through point $A(5; 0)$ and having a focus at $F_2(3; 0)$.

Solution for Exercise 7.22:
• The canonical equation of an ellipse is $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ ($a > b > 0$).
• Focus $F_2(3; 0) \Rightarrow c = 3$.
• Passing through $A(5; 0) \Rightarrow \frac{5^2}{a^2} + 0 = 1 \Rightarrow a^2 = 25 \Rightarrow a = 5$.
• We have: $b^2 = a^2 - c^2 = 25 - 3^2 = 16$.
• The canonical equation of the ellipse is: $\frac{x^2}{25} + \frac{y^2}{16} = 1$.

**Exercise 7.23:** Write the canonical equation of the parabola passing through point $M(2; 4)$.

Solution for Exercise 7.23:
• The canonical equation of a parabola has the form: $y^2 = 2px$ ($p > 0$).
• Since the curve passes through $M(2; 4)$, its coordinates satisfy:
$$4^2 = 2p(2) \Leftrightarrow 16 = 4p \Rightarrow p = 4$$
• Thus, the canonical equation of the parabola is: $y^2 = 8x$.
