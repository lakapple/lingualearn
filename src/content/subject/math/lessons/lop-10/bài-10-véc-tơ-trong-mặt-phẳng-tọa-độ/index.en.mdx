**Learning Objectives**

**Terminology:**

Coordinate plane, Cartesian coordinate system $Oxy$

Standard unit vectors $\vec{i}, \vec{j}$

Coordinates of a vector, abscissa (x-component), ordinate (y-component)

Coordinates of a point, distance between two points

Coordinates of Midpoint of a segment, centroid of a triangle

**Knowledge and skills:**

Identify and represent the coordinates of a vector and the coordinates of a point in the Cartesian coordinate plane $Oxy$.

Perform vector operations (addition, subtraction, scalar multiplication) in coordinate form.

Determine relationships between vectors (equality, collinearity) and the condition for three collinear points using coordinates.

Calculate the magnitude of a vector, the distance between two points, and the coordinates of midpoints and centroids.

Apply vector coordinates to model real-world uniform linear motion problems (tracking storm paths, vessel navigation).

**Coordinates of a Vector**

**a) Coordinate Axes and the Cartesian Coordinate System Oxy**

**Coordinate axis:** A coordinate axis (or number line) is a straight line on which an origin point $O$ and a unit vector $\vec{i}$ of length $1$ ($|\vec{i}| = 1$) have been designated.

**Cartesian coordinate system Oxy:** Consists of two perpendicular coordinate axes: the horizontal axis $Ox$ (x-axis) and the vertical axis $Oy$ (y-axis) intersecting at the common origin $O$. The unit vectors along $Ox$ and $Oy$ are denoted by $\vec{i}$ and $\vec{j}$, respectively ($|\vec{i}| = |\vec{j}| = 1$ and $\vec{i} \perp \vec{j}$). The plane equipped with this system is called the Cartesian coordinate plane $Oxy$.

**b) Definition of Vector Coordinates**

**Definition:** For each vector $\vec{u}$ in the coordinate plane $Oxy$, there exists a unique ordered pair of real numbers $(x_0; y_0)$ such that:

$$\vec{u} = x_0\vec{i} + y_0\vec{j}$$

We say that vector $\vec{u}$ has coordinates $(x_0; y_0)$ and write $\vec{u} = (x_0; y_0)$ or $\vec{u}(x_0; y_0)$. The scalar $x_0$ is called the abscissa (x-coordinate), and $y_0$ is called the ordinate (y-coordinate) of $\vec{u}$.

**Coordinates of special vectors:** $\vec{i} = (1; 0); \quad \vec{j} = (0; 1); \quad \vec{0} = (0; 0)$.

**Equality of two vectors:** Two vectors are equal if and only if their corresponding coordinates are equal:

$$\vec{u}(x; y) = \vec{v}(x'; y') \Leftrightarrow \begin{cases} x = x' \\\ y = y' \end{cases}$$

**c) Illustrative Examples on Vector Coordinates**

**Example 1 (Reading vector coordinates from component form):** Determine the coordinates of the following vectors: a) $\vec{a} = 2\vec{i} - 5\vec{j}$; b) $\vec{b} = -3\vec{i}$; c) $\vec{c} = 4\vec{j}$; d) $\vec{d} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{i} + \frac{3}{4}\vec{j}$.

**Solution for Example 1:**

• a) Since $\vec{a} = 2\vec{i} + (-5)\vec{j}$, we have $\vec{a} = (2; -5)$.

• b) Since $\vec{b} = -3\vec{i} + 0\vec{j}$, we have $\vec{b} = (-3; 0)$.

• c) Since $\vec{c} = 0\vec{i} + 4\vec{j}$, we have $\vec{c} = (0; 4)$.

• d) Since $\vec{d} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{i} + \frac{3}{4}\vec{j}$, we have $\vec{d} = \left(\frac{1}{2}; \frac{3}{4}\right)$.

**Example 2 (Condition for equal vectors):** Find the real numbers $x$ and $y$ such that the vectors $\vec{u} = (2x - 1; 4)$ and $\vec{v} = (5; y + 2)$ are equal.

**Solution for Example 2:**

• By the condition for vector equality, we have the system of equations:

$$\vec{u} = \vec{v} \Leftrightarrow \begin{cases} 2x - 1 = 5 \\\ y + 2 = 4 \end{cases} \Leftrightarrow \begin{cases} 2x = 6 \\\ y = 2 \end{cases} \Leftrightarrow \begin{cases} x = 3 \\\ y = 2 \end{cases}$$

• Thus, $x = 3$ and $y = 2$.

**Coordinate Expressions of Vector Operations**

**a) Addition, Subtraction, and Scalar Multiplication**

**Operational formulas:** Let $\vec{u} = (x; y), \vec{v} = (x'; y')$ and $k \in \mathbb{R}$. Then:

• $\vec{u} + \vec{v} = (x + x'; y + y')$

• $\vec{u} - \vec{v} = (x - x'; y - y')$

• $k\vec{u} = (kx; ky)$

**b) Collinearity of Two Vectors in Coordinate Form**

**General condition:** Vector $\vec{v}(x'; y')$ is collinear with vector $\vec{u}(x; y) \ne \vec{0}$ if and only if there exists a scalar $k$ such that:

$$\begin{cases} x' = kx \\\ y' = ky \end{cases}$$

**Proportionality condition:** When $xy \ne 0$, vectors $\vec{u}(x; y)$ and $\vec{v}(x'; y')$ are collinear if and only if: $\frac{x'}{x} = \frac{y'}{y}$.

**c) Illustrative Examples on Vector Operations**

**Example 3 (Evaluating linear combinations and testing collinearity):** In the coordinate plane $Oxy$, given $\vec{a} = (1; -2)$, $\vec{b} = (-3; 4)$, and $\vec{c} = (2; 1)$. a) Find the coordinates of vector $\vec{u} = 2\vec{a} - 3\vec{b} + \vec{c}$. b) Determine whether $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are collinear.

**Solution for Example 3:**

• a) We have: $2\vec{a} = (2; -4)$, $-3\vec{b} = (9; -12)$, $\vec{c} = (2; 1)$.

  Thus: $\vec{u} = (2 + 9 + 2; -4 - 12 + 1) = (13; -15)$.

• b) Comparing coordinates: $\frac{1}{-3} \ne \frac{-2}{4}$ (since $-\frac{1}{3} \ne -\frac{1}{2}$). Therefore, $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are not collinear.

**Point Coordinates and Geometric Formulas**

**a) Coordinates of a Point and Vector Magnitude**

**Point coordinates:** In the coordinate plane $Oxy$, the coordinates of point $M$ are identical to the coordinates of the position vector $\vec{OM}$:

$$M(x; y) \Leftrightarrow \vec{OM} = (x; y) = x\vec{i} + y\vec{j}$$

**Magnitude of a vector:** The magnitude of vector $\vec{u} = (x; y)$ is: $|\vec{u}| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$.

**Distance from the origin:** The distance from point $M(x; y)$ to the origin $O(0; 0)$ is: $OM = |\vec{OM}| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$.

**b) Vector Connecting Two Points and Distance Between Two Points**

**Vector between two points:** Given two points $A(x_A; y_A)$ and $B(x_B; y_B)$, the displacement vector $\vec{AB}$ is:

$$\vec{AB} = (x_B - x_A; y_B - y_A)$$

**Distance between two points:** The Euclidean distance between $A$ and $B$ (length of segment $AB$) is:

$$AB = |\vec{AB}| = \sqrt{(x_B - x_A)^2 + (y_B - y_A)^2}$$

**c) Midpoint and Centroid Formulas**

**Midpoint coordinates:** The midpoint $M(x_M; y_M)$ of line segment $AB$ has coordinates:

$$x_M = \frac{x_A + x_B}{2}, \quad y_M = \frac{y_A + y_B}{2}$$

**Centroid coordinates:** The centroid $G(x_G; y_G)$ of triangle $ABC$ has coordinates:

$$x_G = \frac{x_A + x_B + x_C}{3}, \quad y_G = \frac{y_A + y_B + y_C}{3}$$

**d) Illustrative Examples on Point Coordinates**

**Example 4 (Geometric properties in coordinate form):** In the coordinate plane $Oxy$, consider $A(1; 3)$, $B(-2; 6)$, $C(5; 1)$. a) Prove that $A, B, C$ are the vertices of a triangle. b) Find the midpoint $M$ of $AB$ and the centroid $G$ of $\triangle ABC$. c) Find the coordinates of point $D$ such that $ABCD$ is a parallelogram.

**Solution for Example 4:**

• a) Compute the vectors: $\vec{AB} = (-2 - 1; 6 - 3) = (-3; 3)$ and $\vec{AC} = (5 - 1; 1 - 3) = (4; -2)$.

  Since $\frac{-3}{4} \ne \frac{3}{-2}$, $\vec{AB}$ and $\vec{AC}$ are not collinear. Hence $A, B, C$ form a triangle.

• b) Midpoint $M$ of $AB$: $x_M = \frac{1 + (-2)}{2} = -\frac{1}{2}; \ y_M = \frac{3 + 6}{2} = \frac{9}{2} \Rightarrow M\left(-\frac{1}{2}; \frac{9}{2}\right)$.

  Centroid $G$ of $\triangle ABC$: $x_G = \frac{1 + (-2) + 5}{3} = \frac{4}{3}; \ y_G = \frac{3 + 6 + 1}{3} = \frac{10}{3} \Rightarrow G\left(\frac{4}{3}; \frac{10}{3}\right)$.

• c) Let $D(x_D; y_D)$. Quadrilateral $ABCD$ is a parallelogram $\Leftrightarrow \vec{AB} = \vec{DC}$.

  We have $\vec{DC} = (5 - x_D; 1 - y_D)$. Equating components with $\vec{AB} = (-3; 3)$:

  $$\begin{cases} 5 - x_D = -3 \\\ 1 - y_D = 3 \end{cases} \Leftrightarrow \begin{cases} x_D = 8 \\\ y_D = -2 \end{cases} \Rightarrow D(8; -2)$$

**Real-World Applications of Vector Coordinates**

**a) Modeling Uniform Linear Motion**

**Kinematic model:** If an object departs from initial position $A(x_0; y_0)$ and moves with constant velocity vector $\vec{v} = (v_x; v_y)$, its position $M(x; y)$ after time $t$ satisfies:

$$\vec{AM} = t\vec{v} \Leftrightarrow \begin{cases} x = x_0 + t \cdot v_x \\\ y = y_0 + t \cdot v_y \end{cases}$$

**b) Practical Example**

**Example 5 (Predicting the trajectory of a storm):** A typhoon's center travels with uniform velocity over a $12\text{-hour}$ period from $A(13.8; 108.3)$ to $B(14.1; 106.3)$ on a coordinate map. Determine the coordinates of the storm center $M$ exactly $9\text{ hours}$ after tracking began.

**Solution for Example 5:**

• Since motion is uniform over 12 hours, after 9 hours the displacement ratio is:

$$k = \frac{9}{12} = \frac{3}{4}$$

• Therefore, the vector equation is: $\vec{AM} = \frac{3}{4}\vec{AB}$.

• Compute the total displacement: $\vec{AB} = (14.1 - 13.8; 106.3 - 108.3) = (0.3; -2.0)$.

• For $M(x; y)$, we have $\vec{AM} = (x - 13.8; y - 108.3)$. Equating components:

$$\begin{cases} x - 13.8 = \frac{3}{4} \cdot 0.3 = 0.225 \\\ y - 108.3 = \frac{3}{4} \cdot (-2.0) = -1.5 \end{cases} \Leftrightarrow \begin{cases} x = 13.8 + 0.225 = 14.025 \\\ y = 108.3 - 1.5 = 106.8 \end{cases}$$

• Conclusion: After 9 hours, the storm center is located at $M(14.025; 106.8)$.
