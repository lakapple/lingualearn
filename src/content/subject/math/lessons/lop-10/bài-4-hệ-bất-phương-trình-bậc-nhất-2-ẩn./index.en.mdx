## **Systems of Linear Inequalities in Two Variables**

### **a) Concept of a System of Linear Inequalities in Two Variables**

**Definition:** A system of linear inequalities in two variables is a collection of two or more linear inequalities in two variables $x, y$.

**General form:** A system of linear inequalities in two variables generally has the form:

$$\begin{cases} a_1 x + b_1 y \le c_1 \\\ a_2 x + b_2 y \le c_2 \\\ \vdots \\\ a_m x + b_m y \le c_m \end{cases}$$

(where the inequality symbols $\le$ may be replaced by $\ge, <, >$).

**Example:** The system $\begin{cases} x \ge 0 \\\ y \ge 0 \\\ x + y \le 150 \end{cases}$ is a system of linear inequalities in two variables. In contrast, the system $\begin{cases} x + y^2 < 0 \\\ y - x > 1 \end{cases}$ is not, because it contains the quadratic term $y^2$.

### **b) Solutions of a System of Linear Inequalities in Two Variables**

**Definition of a solution:** An ordered pair $(x_0; y_0)$ is called a solution of a system of linear inequalities in two variables if $(x_0; y_0)$ simultaneously satisfies all inequalities in the system.

**Example:** Given the system $\begin{cases} x \ge 0 \\\ y \ge 0 \\\ x + y \le 150 \end{cases}$. The ordered pair $(0; 0)$ satisfies all three inequalities, and thus $(0; 0)$ is a solution of the system.

## **Graphing the Feasible Region of a System of Linear Inequalities on the Coordinate Plane**

### **a) Concept of Feasible Region**

**Definition of feasible region:** In the Cartesian coordinate plane $Oxy$, the set of all points whose coordinates $(x; y)$ are solutions of a system of linear inequalities in two variables is called the feasible region (or solution set) of that system.

**Geometric property:** The feasible region of a system is the intersection of the solution regions of all individual inequalities in the system.

### **b) Procedure for Graphing the Feasible Region of a System**

**Procedure:** To determine the feasible region of a system of linear inequalities in two variables on the coordinate plane $Oxy$, perform the following steps:

**Step 1:** On the same coordinate plane $Oxy$, draw the boundary lines corresponding to each inequality in the system.

**Step 2:** Determine the solution region of each individual inequality in the system and shade (cross out) the region that does not satisfy it.

**Step 3:** Conclusion: The unshaded (unhatched) region (including or excluding boundaries depending on the inequality signs) is the feasible region of the given system.

**Note on boundary lines:** If an inequality is strict ($<$ or $>$), the corresponding boundary line is drawn as a dashed line, and the points on this line do not belong to the feasible region.

## **Applications of Systems of Linear Inequalities in Optimization Problems**

### **a) Fundamental Theorem of Linear Programming on a Polygonal Region**

**Statement of the theorem:** The maximum (or minimum) value of a linear function $F(x; y) = ax + by$ on a polygonal region $A_1A_2...A_n$ (i.e., the set of points lying inside or on the edges of the polygon) is always attained at one of the vertices of the polygon.

### **b) Solving Linear Programming Problems**

**Optimization procedure:** To find the maximum (or minimum) value of the objective function $F(x; y) = ax + by$ over a bounded polygonal feasible region:

• Step 1: Model the problem by introducing variables $x, y$ and establishing the system of linear inequalities representing the constraints.

• Step 2: Graph the feasible region of the system on the coordinate plane $Oxy$ and determine the coordinates of all its vertices.

• Step 3: Evaluate the objective function $F(x; y)$ at each vertex of the polygon.

• Step 4: Compare the evaluated values to find the maximum (or minimum) value and state the conclusion for the practical problem.
