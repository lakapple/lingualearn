## **Learning Objectives**

### **Terminology:**

Unit semicircle

Trigonometric values of an angle from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$ (sine, cosine, tangent, cotangent)

Supplementary angles, complementary angles

Table of trigonometric values of special angles

### **Knowledge and skills:**

Recognize and comprehend the definition of trigonometric values of an arbitrary angle from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$ via coordinates of a point on the unit semicircle.

Explain and apply the relationships between trigonometric values of supplementary angles and complementary angles.

Utilize the table of trigonometric values of special angles and handheld calculators to evaluate trigonometric values of an angle or determine an angle given its trigonometric value.

Apply fundamental trigonometric identities to simplify expressions, prove identities, and solve practical real-world problems.

## **Trigonometric Values of an Angle from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$**

<Youtube url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example_gtlg_0_180"/>

### **a) The Unit Semicircle**

**Definition of the unit semicircle:** In the Cartesian coordinate plane $Oxy$, the semicircle centered at the origin $O(0; 0)$ with radius $R = 1$ located above the $x$-axis (including the two points $(-1; 0)$ and $(1; 0)$) is called the unit semicircle.

**Determination of point $M$:** For each angle $\alpha$ ($0^\circ \le \alpha \le 180^\circ$), there exists a unique point $M(x_0; y_0)$ on the unit semicircle such that $\widehat{xOM} = \alpha$.

### **b) Definitions of Trigonometric Values**

**Definition of sine:** The sine of angle $\alpha$ is the $y$-coordinate (ordinate) $y_0$ of point $M$, denoted by $\sin \alpha = y_0$.

**Definition of cosine:** The cosine of angle $\alpha$ is the $x$-coordinate (abscissa) $x_0$ of point $M$, denoted by $\cos \alpha = x_0$.

**Definition of tangent:** When $\alpha \ne 90^\circ$ (i.e., $x_0 \ne 0$), the tangent of angle $\alpha$ is the ratio $\frac{y_0}{x_0}$, denoted by $\tan \alpha = \frac{\sin \alpha}{\cos \alpha} = \frac{y_0}{x_0}$.

**Definition of cotangent:** When $\alpha \ne 0^\circ$ and $\alpha \ne 180^\circ$ (i.e., $y_0 \ne 0$), the cotangent of angle $\alpha$ is the ratio $\frac{x_0}{y_0}$, denoted by $\cot \alpha = \frac{\cos \alpha}{\sin \alpha} = \frac{x_0}{y_0}$.

**Reciprocal relation:** For $\alpha \notin \\{0^\circ; 90^\circ; 180^\circ\\}$, we have: $\tan \alpha = \frac{1}{\cot \alpha}$.

### **c) Signs and Ranges of Trigonometric Values**

**Sign of sine:** For all $0^\circ \le \alpha \le 180^\circ$, we always have $\sin \alpha \ge 0$ ($0 \le \sin \alpha \le 1$).

**Sign of cosine:** If $\alpha$ is an acute angle ($0^\circ < \alpha < 90^\circ$), then $\cos \alpha > 0$. If $\alpha = 90^\circ$, then $\cos 90^\circ = 0$. If $\alpha$ is an obtuse angle ($90^\circ < \alpha \le 180^\circ$), then $\cos \alpha < 0$. We always have $-1 \le \cos \alpha \le 1$.

**Signs of tangent and cotangent:** When angle $\alpha$ is acute, $\tan \alpha > 0$ and $\cot \alpha > 0$. When angle $\alpha$ is obtuse, $\tan \alpha < 0$ and $\cot \alpha < 0$.

### **d) Table of Trigonometric Values of Special Angles**

**Angle $0^\circ$:** $\sin 0^\circ = 0; \quad \cos 0^\circ = 1; \quad \tan 0^\circ = 0; \quad \cot 0^\circ$ is undefined.

**Angle $30^\circ$:** $\sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2}; \quad \cos 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}; \quad \tan 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}; \quad \cot 30^\circ = \sqrt{3}$.

**Angle $45^\circ$:** $\sin 45^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}; \quad \cos 45^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}; \quad \tan 45^\circ = 1; \quad \cot 45^\circ = 1$.

**Angle $60^\circ$:** $\sin 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}; \quad \cos 60^\circ = \frac{1}{2}; \quad \tan 60^\circ = \sqrt{3}; \quad \cot 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$.

**Angle $90^\circ$:** $\sin 90^\circ = 1; \quad \cos 90^\circ = 0; \quad \tan 90^\circ$ is undefined; $\quad \cot 90^\circ = 0$.

**Angle $180^\circ$:** $\sin 180^\circ = 0; \quad \cos 180^\circ = -1; \quad \tan 180^\circ = 0; \quad \cot 180^\circ$ is undefined.

## **Relationships Between Trigonometric Values of Supplementary and Complementary Angles**

### **a) Supplementary Angles (summing to $180^\circ$)**

**Geometric property:** Two points $M(x_0; y_0)$ and $M'(-x_0; y_0)$ on the unit semicircle corresponding to two supplementary angles $\alpha$ and $180^\circ - \alpha$ are symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis $Oy$.

**Reduction formulas:** For two supplementary angles $\alpha$ and $180^\circ - \alpha$, we have:

• $\sin(180^\circ - \alpha) = \sin \alpha$

• $\cos(180^\circ - \alpha) = -\cos \alpha$

• $\tan(180^\circ - \alpha) = -\tan \alpha \quad (\alpha \ne 90^\circ)$

• $\cot(180^\circ - \alpha) = -\cot \alpha \quad (0^\circ < \alpha < 180^\circ)$

**Mnemonic rule:** "Supplementary angles have equal sines; opposite cosines, tangents, and cotangents."

### **b) Complementary Angles (summing to $90^\circ$)**

**Reduction formulas:** For two complementary angles $\alpha$ and $90^\circ - \alpha$ ($0^\circ \le \alpha \le 90^\circ$), we have:

• $\sin(90^\circ - \alpha) = \cos \alpha$

• $\cos(90^\circ - \alpha) = \sin \alpha$

• $\tan(90^\circ - \alpha) = \cot \alpha \quad (0^\circ < \alpha < 90^\circ)$

• $\cot(90^\circ - \alpha) = \tan \alpha \quad (0^\circ < \alpha < 90^\circ)$

**Mnemonic rule:** "Complementary angles: the sine of one equals the cosine of the other, and the tangent of one equals the cotangent of the other (cofunction identities)."

## **Fundamental Trigonometric Identities**

**Pythagorean trigonometric identity:** For any angle $\alpha$ ($0^\circ \le \alpha \le 180^\circ$), we have: $\sin^2 \alpha + \cos^2 \alpha = 1$.

**Identity relating tangent and cosine:** For any angle $\alpha \ne 90^\circ$, we have: $1 + \tan^2 \alpha = \frac{1}{\cos^2 \alpha}$.

**Identity relating cotangent and sine:** For any angle $0^\circ < \alpha < 180^\circ$, we have: $1 + \cot^2 \alpha = \frac{1}{\sin^2 \alpha}$.

**Product of tangent and cotangent:** For any angle $\alpha \notin \\{0^\circ; 90^\circ; 180^\circ\\}$, we have: $\tan \alpha \cdot \cot \alpha = 1$.
