## **Learning Objectives**

### **Terminology:**

Vector, zero vector

Magnitude (length) of a vector

Line of action of a vector

Collinear vectors

Vectors with the same direction, opposite directions

Equal vectors

### **Knowledge and skills:**

Recognize the concepts of vectors, magnitude of a vector, collinear vectors, vectors with the same direction, opposite directions, and equal vectors.

Understand the concept and fundamental properties of the zero vector.

Know how to determine a point $M$ given an initial point $O$ and a vector $\vec{a}$ such that $\vec{OM} = \vec{a}$.

Utilize vector notation and terminology to formulate and interpret basic geometric relationships (collinear points, parallelograms).

Model directional physical quantities such as force, velocity, and displacement using vectors.

## **Concept of a Vector**

### **a) Definition of a Vector**

**Definition:** A vector is a directed line segment, meaning that between the two endpoints of the segment, an initial point (tail) and a terminal point (head) are explicitly designated.

**Notation with initial and terminal points:** A vector with initial point $A$ and terminal point $B$ is denoted by $\vec{AB}$ (or $\overrightarrow{AB}$), read as "vector $AB$".

**Geometric representation:** To draw a vector, draw the line segment connecting its initial point to its terminal point, then place an arrow indicating direction at the terminal point.

**General notation:** When it is not necessary to specify the initial and terminal points, vectors are denoted by lowercase letters with an arrow above, such as $\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{x}, \vec{y},...$

### **b) Magnitude of a Vector**

**Definition:** The magnitude (or length) of a vector is the distance between its initial point and terminal point.

**Magnitude notation:** The magnitude of vector $\vec{AB}$ is denoted by $|\vec{AB}|$, and the magnitude of vector $\vec{a}$ is denoted by $|\vec{a}|$. We have: $|\vec{AB}| = AB$.

**Properties of magnitude:** The magnitude of a vector is always a non-negative real number: $|\vec{a}| \ge 0$. The magnitude is zero if and only if it is the zero vector.

## **Collinear, Codirectional, and Equal Vectors**

### **a) Line of Action and Collinear Vectors**

**Line of action:** The straight line passing through the initial point and terminal point of a vector is called the line of action (or support line) of that vector.

**Definition of collinear vectors:** Two vectors are called collinear (or parallel) if their lines of action are parallel or coincident.

**Condition for collinear points:** Three distinct points $A, B, C$ are collinear if and only if the two vectors $\vec{AB}$ and $\vec{AC}$ are collinear.

### **b) Vectors with the Same or Opposite Direction**

**Relationship between line of action and direction:** Two collinear vectors can either have the same direction or opposite directions.

**Important remark:** Only when two vectors are collinear can we discuss whether they have the same direction or opposite directions. Two non-collinear vectors are neither in the same direction nor in opposite directions.

### **c) Equal Vectors**

**Definition:** Two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are called equal, denoted by $\vec{a} = \vec{b}$, if they have the same magnitude and the same direction:

$$\vec{a} = \vec{b} \Leftrightarrow \begin{cases} |\vec{a}| = |\vec{b}| \\\ \vec{a}, \vec{b} \text{ have the same direction} \end{cases}$$

**Point-specification property:** For each given point $O$ and vector $\vec{a}$, there exists a unique point $A$ such that $\vec{OA} = \vec{a}$.

**Parallelogram application:** A quadrilateral $ABCD$ is a parallelogram if and only if $\vec{AB} = \vec{DC}$ (or $\vec{AD} = \vec{BC}$).

### **d) The Zero Vector**

**Definition:** Vectors whose initial and terminal points coincide (such as $\vec{AA}, \vec{BB}, \vec{MM}$) are called zero vectors (or null vectors), denoted collectively by $\vec{0}$.

**Conventions for the zero vector:**

• The magnitude of the zero vector is zero, i.e., $|\vec{0}| = 0$.

• The zero vector is conventionally regarded as having the same direction (and therefore collinear with) every vector.

• Since all zero vectors have the same magnitude ($0$) and the same direction, they are all equal: $\vec{AA} = \vec{BB} = \vec{0}$.

## **Representing Physical Quantities by Vectors**

**Principle of representation:** Physical quantities characterized by both magnitude and direction, such as force, velocity, acceleration, and displacement, are naturally modeled by mathematical vectors:

• The initial point (tail) of the vector is placed at the point of application of the force or the initial position of the moving object.

• The line of action and arrow of the vector indicate the direction of the force or motion.

• The length of the vector is proportional to the magnitude of the physical quantity according to an appropriate scale.

**Example of balanced forces:** When an object at rest is submerged in a fluid, it is acted upon by gravity $\vec{P}$ and the buoyant force (Archimedes' force) $\vec{F}$; these two forces are collinear, act in opposite directions, and their magnitudes depend on the specific weights of the object and the liquid.
